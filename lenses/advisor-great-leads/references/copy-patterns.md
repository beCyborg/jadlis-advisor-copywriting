# Great Leads — Copy Patterns (openers & first-500 words)  ·  prefix [GL]
> The physical construction of the lead — the headline plus the first ~100–600 words whose only job is to earn the next paragraph. Use this file once `lead-types.md` has told you *which* lead the awareness level calls for; this tells you how to write its opening lines. All few-shots below are invented for teaching; RU examples are built on Russian-market situations, not translated from the EN.

## Principles

### [GL:LEADJOB] The lead's job and length  (ch 1, 7)
- **Essence:** The lead is the opening 100–600 words (short products need less, complex ones more). Its job is not to make the sale — it's to *emotionally persuade the reader to keep reading* until the full benefits and USP land. Selling the next line is the whole assignment.
- **Copy move:** Write the opener to end on a slope: every paragraph should make stopping feel like leaving something unfinished. Judge a draft opener by one test — "does this earn the next sentence?" — not "does this sell?". Reserve the offer/USP for after the lead has done its emotional work.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL.
- **Applies / fails when:** Universal. Fails when the writer tries to close in the first paragraph (dumps price/USP too early) or pads the opener with warm-up that carries no forward pull.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Strong: "Six weeks ago I couldn't have written that sentence. Here's what changed —" (nothing sold; you must read on). Negative: "Our SaaS is $49/mo and syncs your calendar" as line one — the deal is spent before any desire exists.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Сильно: «Три месяца назад этот отчёт стоил бы мне клиента. Сейчас объясню, что его спасло…» — крючок тянет дальше, продукт не назван. Слабо: «Курс по таргету — 19 900 ₽, старт в понедельник» первой строкой: сделка выложена до того, как возникло желание.
- **Book ref:** The authors define the lead as the number of words (roughly 100–600) needed to persuade the reader emotionally before the pitch turns overt — everything before that handoff is the lead's real work.

### [GL:OFFEROPEN] Offer opener formula  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** The most direct opener: put the deal on the table immediately, then justify it. Four moves — (1) open on the most emotionally-compelling deal detail, (2) underscore its single most valuable benefit, (3) elaborate that same deal-benefit, (4) give a credible "reason why" the deal exists, answering "what's the catch?".
- **Copy move:** Lead with whichever deal element hits hardest — price, discount, premium, or (often stronger) the guarantee. Do the math for the reader ("that's 67% off"). Add a dissolving bonus to melt final resistance. For exclusive/anticipated products, swap explicit price for the **Invitation** frame. Keep it short — an aware buyer needs proof of value, not warm-up.
- **Applies to:** email to house lists | landing for warm traffic | renewals.
- **Applies / fails when:** For Most-Aware buyers. Fails on cold/skeptical readers and when heavy "FREE" undercuts a high-value product.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Don't pay a cent for the toolkit until it saves you your first ten hours — and if it doesn't, keep it free." Then: benefit → elaborate → "We're capping this at 100 seats because support is hands-on" (reason-why). Negative: "50% off!!! Buy now!!!" — deal with no benefit, no reason, no credibility.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Заберите годовой доступ к боту-аналитику бесплатно — вы платите, только если за первый месяц он не найдёт вам ни одной прибыльной сделки». Далее: выгода → раскрытие → «Открываем всего 200 мест: каждого новичка ведёт куратор лично» (reason-why). Слабо: «Скидка 50%, успей купить!» — оффер без выгоды и без причины, тонет среди таких же.
- **Book ref:** Book-of-the-Month Club's "no-strings" opener led with "any 4 books for $1 each" and no obligation, then framed it as an experiment for both sides — the reason-why that made a too-good deal believable.

### [GL:PROMOPEN] Promise opener formula  (ch 5)
- **Essence:** Open on the single biggest, most unique benefit — stated so plainly it's instantly comprehensible — then connect it to a desire the reader already holds and start proving it. The promise carries the whole opener, so it must be original and believable.
- **Copy move:** Derive the promise from the USP (unique + provable + targets an existing desire). Reach past the functional benefit to the emotional/social one. If the honest claim is huge and risks disbelief, either turn it into a question ("Can you really…?") or scale it down until it's surprisingly small but credible. Follow immediately with bigger proof.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL for warm-to-lukewarm traffic.
- **Applies / fails when:** For Product-Aware / on-the-fence prospects. Fails on hardened skeptics, the ad-saturated "hyper-aware," and the fully unaware.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Write a week of posts in the time it used to take to write one — without sounding like a robot." (unique + provable + targets an existing want). Believability rescue: "Grow $500 into $8.4M?" reframed as a question defuses the disbelief a flat claim would trigger. Negative: "The #1 revolutionary breakthrough that changes everything!" — big, vague, unbelievable, promises nothing specific.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Собираете неделю контента за то время, что раньше уходило на один пост — и без штампов нейросетей». Спасение правдоподобия: вместо «заработайте миллион на маркетплейсе» → «Можно ли выйти на 300 000 ₽ оборота за 90 дней? Посчитаем честно». Слабо: «Уникальная революционная методика, которая изменит вашу жизнь!» — крупно, размыто, не обещает ничего конкретного.
- **Book ref:** Schwab sold Carnegie's book by lifting the promise already on its cover — "How to Win Friends and Influence People" — the plainest, most-wanted benefit, then reinforced it with promise-laden chapter titles as proof.

### [GL:PROBOPEN] Problem-Solution opener  (ch 6)
- **Essence:** Open by naming the worry the prospect feels most sharply, at its emotional peak (the "point of maximum anxiety"), to prove you understand before you offer relief. Five reliable entry formats package the worry.
- **Copy move:** Sum up the core worry in as instant a phrase as possible, stir the emotion just long enough to win the read-on, then turn to hope. Pick a format: **If-Then** ("If you feel X, then Y"); **Inversion / Negative-Promise** (promise the escape before naming the problem); **Identification** (address the person by their trouble — "To people who X but can't Y"); **The Question** (a challenge that forces a "yes" and starts the mental conversation); **Instruction** (coin a term that names the problem — "tired blood" — and teach it).
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | long email; health, finance, self-improvement.
- **Applies / fails when:** For Solution-/Problem-Aware readers. Fails on the Most-Aware (empathy they don't need), when you dwell on the pain too long, or when the reader isn't problem-minded.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Question format: "Do you reread your own emails three times before hitting send — and still dread the reply?" (inevitable yes from the anxious writer). Instruction format: coin "inbox debt" for the pile of unanswered mail, then promise to clear it. Negative: "Are you sad sometimes?" — too generic to force a yes or stir a specific emotion.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Формат «вопрос»: «Проверяете остаток на карте перед каждой покупкой в конце месяца — и всё равно не понимаете, куда уходят деньги?» (неизбежное «да» у тревожного). Формат «инструкция»: назвать это «финансовый туман», а затем пообещать его рассеять. Слабо: «Бывает, что вам тревожно?» — слишком общо, не вытягивает конкретную эмоцию.
- **Book ref:** "For the Woman Who is Older Than She Looks" beat "…Who Looks Younger Than She Is" by a wide margin — the winning version named a feeling the reader wanted rid of, the loser addressed someone who felt no need to change.

### [GL:SECRETOPEN] Secret opener — the velvet pouch  (ch 7)
- **Essence:** Create tension by describing the benefits of something while keeping the thing itself hidden — the way a salesman fondles a pot inside a velvet pouch before revealing it. The reader's instinct to see what's inside holds his attention.
- **Copy move:** Put an intriguing, benefit-bearing secret in the headline and refuse to disclose it in the lead. Manufacture the secret two ways: find a genuine unknown inside the product, or **neologize** — invent a fresh name for an ordinary thing ("dividend stocks" → "the Chaffee Royalty Program") so the reader must read on to learn what it is; **transubstantiate** by letting the glow of something special (rock-star royalty checks) bleed onto the ordinary thing you're actually selling. Drip **clues** like breadcrumbs — details that feel like progress toward the reveal without giving it away. Often, reveal only inside the purchased product. Also: telling the reader what the secret *isn't* makes him feel closer to what it *is*.
- **Applies to:** VSL | long landing | direct-response email; info-products, supplements, finance, systems.
- **Applies / fails when:** For skeptical, aware readers. Fails the instant you disclose in the lead, or when the secret isn't fastened to a real benefit.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "The '11-Minute Rule' that quietly funded three of my friends' early retirements — banks won't mention it." (named, benefit-loaded, undisclosed). Clue-drip: "It's not index funds, not real estate, not crypto…" Negative: "Our secret is dollar-cost averaging into an S&P 500 ETF" in line two — curiosity satisfied, reader gone.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «"Правило тихой полки", которое переводит обычную зарплату в капитал — про него молчат в банках». (название есть, выгода есть, суть скрыта). Дроп подсказок: «Это не вклад, не облигации и не инвесткопилка…». Слабо: «Наш секрет — это регулярные покупки индексного фонда» во второй строке — любопытство удовлетворено, читатель ушёл.
- **Book ref:** The "Texas company tapping oil under the Eiffel Tower" letter fed the reader ever-more-specific figures — 40 billion barrels, confirmed by the French Oil Ministry — each detail feeling like a step toward the secret while the actual stock stayed hidden.

### [GL:PROCOPEN] Proclamation opener  (ch 8)
- **Essence:** Open with a bold assertion — fact, opinion, or prediction — that startles, intrigues, and tempts in one line, then spend the opening proving it. It reads like a news headline and carries an implicit promise the reader chases.
- **Copy move:** Craft a statement near the edge of the incredible but still relevant and provable; ordinary statements get skipped. Bake in an implicit promise ("Read This Or Die" implies "…and you'll avoid a preventable death"). For **predictions**, attach a specific date to spike credibility and urgency, and open the copy by establishing the forecaster's credentials. Prove the proclamation before naming the USP; once hooked, you may shift to a direct invitation.
- **Applies to:** VSL | landing | space ads; political/health/financial/big-idea products.
- **Applies / fails when:** For the Unaware (and aware B2B if the claim genuinely alarms them). Fails when the statement is ordinary, irrelevant, unprovable, or too incredible to believe.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Your résumé will be unreadable to employers by March — and it's not your fault." (startles + implicit promise + date). Then establish the source: "I've reviewed hiring software for 40 Fortune-500 firms…" Negative: "The job market is changing fast" — true, ordinary, instantly skipped.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Ваше резюме перестанут открывать HR уже к весне — и вы тут ни при чём». (шок + скрытое обещание + срок). Затем — авторитет: «Я настраивал системы отбора для сорока крупных компаний…». Слабо: «Рынок труда быстро меняется» — верно, банально, пролистывается мгновенно.
- **Book ref:** A debt-crisis promo predicted three specific events would wipe out investors by December 31 of that year — the fixed date pushed the reader to half-accept the forecast and hunt for what the events could be.

### [GL:STORYOPEN] Story opener  (ch 9)
- **Essence:** Open inside a story so the reader forgets he's being sold and gets emotionally involved — resolving his want-to-buy / don't-want-to-be-sold ambivalence. Nothing melts resistance like a well-told story.
- **Copy move:** Start *in medias res* on a conflict affecting an ordinary protagonist the reader recognizes as himself; carry it to an emotionally-satisfying resolution that implies the product's promise. Follow good-story rules: relatable hero attempts something extraordinary → people doubt → he proves them wrong. Keep the **Golden Thread** taut — every line tied to both the product and the prospect's heart; the moment it demonstrates the promise instead of merely asserting it, it's working. Tell it fast; usually keep the product name out of the headline but never let the story drift free of it.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email | fundraising; travel, finance, self-improvement.
- **Applies / fails when:** For Unaware/skeptical readers; the most flexible type. Fails when dull, overlong, slow, or entertaining-but-disconnected (the product isn't fused to the tale).
- **Few-shot (EN):** "The first invoice bounced. The second one, my biggest client, went unread for nine days. I was three weeks from closing the studio when a stranger's one-line email changed everything." (conflict, relatable hero, unresolved → read on; the "one-line email" is the taut thread to the product). Negative: a charming two-page anecdote about the founder's ski trip that never connects to the software — thread slack, sale lost.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Первый платёж не прошёл. Второй — от самого крупного клиента — провисел непрочитанным девять дней. До закрытия студии оставалось три недели, пока одно письмо от незнакомца всё не изменило». (конфликт, узнаваемый герой, развязки нет → читаешь дальше; «одно письмо» — натянутая нить к продукту). Слабо: обаятельная история про то, как основатель катался на сноуборде, никак не сшитая с продуктом — нить провисла, продажа потеряна.
- **Book ref:** International Living opened a promo with editors who bounced through Ecuador, Mexico, Panama and Nicaragua before finding their overseas "sweet spot" — the narrative *demonstrated* the promise of the retire-abroad kit instead of just claiming it.

### [GL:BRIDGE] The indirect-to-offer pivot  (ch 3, 8, 9)
- **Essence:** Every indirect opener (Story, Secret, Proclamation, and the delayed Problem-Solution) must eventually turn back to the product, its big claims, and the offer — that pivot is where most indirect leads are won or lost. The freedom of an indirect opening is a debt that comes due.
- **Copy move:** Plan the bridge before you write the opener: know the sentence that will carry the reader from the story/secret/jolt into the promise. Make the transition feel earned by the opener's own logic (the story's resolution *is* the product's promise; the proven proclamation *implies* the offer). A common, clean bridge is to switch to a direct **invitation** once the emotional hook is set. If you can't see the bridge, the opener is too indirect — rework it.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email — any indirect lead.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to every indirect opener. Fails when the opener is so clever or oblique that no honest path back to the product exists (brand-humor that never sells), or when the pivot is so abrupt it snaps the reader out of the spell.
- **Few-shot (EN):** After the bounced-invoice story: "That email was an automated nudge from [tool] — and it's why I still have a studio. Here's what it does →" (resolution = product; smooth pivot to invitation). Negative: three pages of oil-under-Paris intrigue that never names the stock or asks for the order — no bridge, no sale.
- **Few-shot (RU):** После истории с провисшим платежом: «Это письмо оказалось автонапоминанием от [сервиса] — и именно поэтому студия ещё жива. Вот что он делает за вас →» (развязка = продукт, мягкий переход к приглашению). Слабо: три экрана интриги про «нефть под Эйфелевой башней», где так и не назвали акцию и не позвали купить — моста нет, продажи нет.
- **Book ref:** The "Read This Or Die" health promo rode its startling proclamation through pages of proof and then pivoted with a plain line — "the purpose of this bulletin is to cordially invite you to become one of us" — the direct invitation that carried the hooked reader into the offer.
