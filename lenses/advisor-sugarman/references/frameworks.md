## [ADW:SS] The Slippery Slide  (ch 8)
**Principle (Axiom 6):** Readers must be so compelled that they cannot stop until they've read everything — like a greased playground slide: once gravity takes you, grabbing the rails doesn't help. Every element's compulsion transfers to the next.

**The chain of compulsion (each link's only job is to deliver the reader to the next):**
```
Headline        → forces reading the subheadline
Subheadline     → forces reading the caption / first sentence
First sentence  → forces the second   (short, easy, near-incomplete — [ADW:04])
Second sentence → forces the third
... copy body ... (kept greased by seeds of curiosity — [ADW:08])
→ 25% threshold: once a reader passes ~25% of the copy, they very likely finish
→ the offer / close / satisfaction conviction ([ADW:T09])
```

**Operational rules:**
1. **No stopping points.** Audit the draft for any sentence a reader could comfortably stop on. Break it, shorten it, or add a forward hook.
2. **The 25% rule.** The whole battle is the first quarter. Front-load environment + momentum + curiosity so the reader crosses the threshold; after that, gravity carries them.
3. **"Traffic," not circulation.** Success = the number of people who actually slide through the copy, not the size of the audience. A huge-circulation magazine guarantees nothing if the ad doesn't grease the slide.
4. **Start with a story or offbeat news item.** A compelling narrative opener (even loosely tied, or tied only by a twist at the end) is the strongest way onto the slide. Save odd articles for reuse (see pattern [ADW:P02] in copy-patterns.md).
5. **Grease with curiosity seeds.** At every natural slow-down, plant a forward-pointing teaser ([ADW:08]).
6. **Shorter = more slippery.** Ruthless editing ([ADW:15]) makes the slide steeper — the reader reaches the bottom faster with full impact.

**Diagnostic (run on any draft):** read it as a hostile skimmer. Mark the first place your attention could exit. That exit point is the leak — every leak above the 25% line is a lost sale. Fix leaks top-down.

**Book ref:** A busy scientist wrote to *complain* that his thermostat ad wasted five minutes of her time even though she had no need for a thermostat — for a copywriter, the ideal complaint: proof the slide worked. The "Magic Baloney" and "Consumers Hero" ads open by attacking their own product / confessing a scam, so the reader cannot help but slide down to find the catch.

## [ADW:T01]–[ADW:T31] The 31 Psychological Triggers  (ch 19)
Sugarman's buying-motive checklist. After drafting, run every trigger against the copy and ask "have I engaged this — and should I?" Not all fire for every product; the skill is knowing which the product's nature ([ADW:T10]) and the prospect's nature ([ADW:T11]) call for. Honesty ([ADW:T02]) is the one he'd keep above all others.

| Code | Trigger | When to use / it fires | Wording / copy move |
|------|---------|------------------------|---------------------|
| [ADW:T01] | Involvement / Ownership | Any product the reader can picture using; boost mail/online where they can't touch it | "Hold it in your hand... feel the keys snap." Future-pace use; add involvement devices (circle the typos for a discount, "yes/no" tokens) |
| [ADW:T02] | Honesty | Always — the single most important trigger | State real flaws up front, then why they don't matter. Consumers detect fakery; truth outsells cover-ups |
| [ADW:T03] | Integrity | Always; conveyed by look as much as words | Clean layout, no screaming color bars / underlines / exaggeration. The medium signals the writer's character |
| [ADW:T04] | Credibility | Prices "too good," bold claims, unknown brand | Explain *why* the price/claim is real (bulk buy, closeout); raise-and-resolve every objection; borrow host-medium credibility (WSJ vs. tabloid); brand names, apt endorsers |
| [ADW:T05] | Value & Proof of Value | Any purchase; stronger at higher price | Compare to similar products; educate on intrinsic worth (education ≈ lowering price); prove "best price" so the value question is closed |
| [ADW:T06] | Justify the Purchase | Whenever an objection "can I justify this?" arises; grows with price | Hand the buyer a defense: "you deserve it," savings, health, recognition, tax angle. Higher price → more justification |
| [ADW:T07] | Greed | Low price or expensive-thing-at-low-price | Give more than expected for the money; if price is too low, justify it (else credibility drops). Bargains override need |
| [ADW:T08] | Establish Authority | Every ad, any company size | "America's largest single source of..."; category-specialist framing; a name that signals size/expertise. People need reassurance they're buying from experts |
| [ADW:T09] | Satisfaction Conviction | At the close, to break last resistance | Go beyond a trial period so the reader thinks "they must really believe in this / won't they get ripped off?" Raise an objection, then over-resolve it |
| [ADW:T10] | Nature of Product | Before writing anything | Diagnose the product's inherent personality (fun toy? serious medical? security?) and let it dictate tone and drama |
| [ADW:T11] | Prospect Nature | Before writing; pair with T10 | Profile the buyer's real emotional needs and motivations; craft the pitch to that core, matching product-nature to prospect-nature |
| [ADW:T12] | Current Fads | A trend is peaking; you can enter early | Harmonize with the fad's language; ride it — but exit right after the peak. Beware associating a product with a dying fad |
| [ADW:T13] | Timing | New product / market-condition sensitive | Test to learn if you're early/late; watch external events (news, campaigns) that suddenly help or kill demand. Know when timing is bad |
| [ADW:T14] | Linking | New, unusual, or "miracle" products | Bridge the unfamiliar to the familiar: "The Nose" (smoke detector), "Pocket CB," "vitamins for your car." Relate old→new so the mind works less |
| [ADW:T15] | Consistency | To grow order value after first yes | Make the first commitment tiny and easy; once they've bought, offer more — they act consistently with the commitment (upsell on the order) |
| [ADW:T16] | Harmonize | Product/idea too far from market | Match what the market is already buying to earn entry (Bobby Darin cutting "Splish Splash" first); tweak the product to fit the prospect, not vice versa |
| [ADW:T17] | Desire to Belong | Identity/brand/lifestyle goods | Position ownership as joining a group ("the crowd that drives a Mercedes," educated Volvo owners). Even loners want to belong to a group |
| [ADW:T18] | Desire to Collect | Collectibles — and any repeatable product | Your best buyer is an existing owner; offer the next in a set; supply a holder/display to seed the collection instinct |
| [ADW:T19] | Curiosity | Strongest motive in mail/online (can't touch it) | Withhold the payoff — don't show the view through the lens; tease what they'll discover. "The only way to see it is to buy it." Kill curiosity by over-showing |
| [ADW:T20] | Sense of Urgency | At the close, to stop "I'll think about it" | Real deadlines/limits: limited edition, price rise, closeout, ship-if-order-by. Never fake it — a false scarcity line breaks integrity. Also: omit no info they need to decide now |
| [ADW:T21] | Fear | Loss of opportunity, safety, aging, money | Frame the consequence of inaction (miss the price, the risk, the decline). Part of urgency; use carefully against a real perceived danger |
| [ADW:T22] | Instant Gratification | Overcome mail/online's core disadvantage | Promise and deliver speed — "order Monday, use it Monday night," next-day shipping — to match retail's take-it-home edge |
| [ADW:T23] | Exclusivity / Rarity / Uniqueness | Limited or premium items | "Only 1,000 made / only six in the state." Make the buyer feel part of an envied few; imply future value from scarcity |
| [ADW:T24] | Simplicity | Always — offer, copy, layout | One clear offer; plain words; clean layout. Too many choices kills sales (Swiss Army watch: one model outpulled nine 3-to-1). Never write up or down |
| [ADW:T25] | Human Relationships | Any ad; the resonance layer | Add human elements — a hand holding the product, attractive models, first-person voice, humor — so all "tuning forks" (headline→caption→copy) resonate with the reader |
| [ADW:T26] | Storytelling | Openers, human-interest bonds | Lead with a relevant story — discovery, personal experience — to rivet attention and bond. We've been primed for stories since childhood |
| [ADW:T27] | Mental Engagement | Sophisticated/curious audiences | Don't state the obvious conclusion — let the reader reach it (whole-brain effort = pleasure). Imply "jewelers make a fortune" without saying it. Vague > over-explicit (Hemingway describing women) |
| [ADW:T28] | Guilt | Reciprocity setups; repetition | Give real value/entertainment/info so the reader feels obliged to respond; repeated exposure (multi-magazine, repeat mailings, gifts) builds mild obligation |
| [ADW:T29] | Specificity | Any claim needing belief | Exact figures over vague puffery: "92% of new dentists" not "dentists everywhere"; "72,000 nerve endings," "242 miles of blood vessels." Specific = credible + expert-sounding |
| [ADW:T30] | Familiarity | Repeat exposure, brand, known words | Keep a consistent format/logo so you're "an old friend" among foreign ads; never kill a working campaign from boredom — only when sales drop. Use familiar words (7, red, chair) |
| [ADW:T31] | Hope | Health, beauty, self-improvement, "future benefit" goods | Imply a possible future result via credibility + testimonials — never a specific measurable promise (the $600 formula failed the moment it gave a dated guarantee). Establish credibility so hope compels reorder |

**Usage note.** The book's appendix list has a numbering typo (it prints "13. Current Fads / 13. Timing"); the correct sequence is #12 Current Fads, #13 Timing, as reflected above. Total is 31 distinct triggers.

**How to run the checklist:**
1. Draft first (emotional dump — [ADW:01]); don't checklist while drafting.
2. Diagnose T10 (product nature) + T11 (prospect nature) — these gate which other triggers are relevant.
3. Sweep T01–T31; for each, mark engaged / missing / not-applicable.
4. Prioritize the close cluster — T09 (satisfaction conviction), T20 (urgency), T06 (justify) — which collide at the end of the copy and must blend seamlessly.
5. Never let a trigger override T02 (honesty) or T03 (integrity): a manufactured scarcity, fake specific, or invented affliction wins once and loses the reader for good.
