# The Adweek Copywriting Handbook — Joseph Sugarman  ·  prefix [ADW]
> Copy is salesmanship-in-print: an emotional, personal, frictionless slide that gets one specific reader to say "yes" all the way to the order. Dominant lens when the decision is about *direct-response long-copy that must convert a cold reader on its own* — a landing page, sales letter, VSL, or email that carries the whole sale with no live salesperson.

## Principles

### [ADW:01] Copywriting is a Mental Process  (ch 1,3)
- **Essence:** Copy is not a writing gift; it's the mental act of assembling everything you know about product, customer, and life, then transferring it to the page to make a sale. The first draft is meant to be ugly — an emotional dump; the skill is what you do after.
- **Copy move:** Separate the work into two phases. Phase 1: dump every thought/feeling about the product onto the page without judging grammar or order. Phase 2 (see [ADW:15]) edit it into shape. Never try to write clean on the first pass — it strangles the emotion.
- **Applies to:** all formats — especially long-form landing, email, VSL.
- **Applies / fails when:** Frees blocked writers and raises ceiling on quality. Fails as an excuse to skip editing — the raw dump alone rarely sells.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — draft a SaaS onboarding email by voice-noting "why I'd actually care about this" then transcribing, mess and all, before shaping. Negative — polishing sentence one to perfection for an hour, so the emotional through-line never gets on the page.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Пишешь лендинг для курса по таргету в Telegram Ads: сначала на диктофон вываливаешь всё, что реально бесит новичка ("слил бюджет, а заявок ноль"), потом чистишь. Механика — сырой эмоциональный слой первым, редактура вторым, а не наоборот.
- **Book ref:** Sugarman insists his own first drafts would be the worst in any class — bad grammar, bad spelling — yet his finished ads outsell everyone's because the value is created entirely in the rewrite.

### [ADW:02] General + Specific Knowledge → Become the Expert  (ch 1,2)
- **Essence:** Two knowledge stocks feed the big idea. *General* = broad curiosity, hobbies, wide reading — the raw material for lateral connections. *Specific* = deep study of this exact product and this exact customer until you can honestly say "I am the expert." The winning concept usually hides in one obscure specific fact.
- **Copy move:** Before writing, interrogate the product to exhaustion (how it's made, why now, what's unique) and profile the customer's motivations. Hunt for the single non-obvious detail that becomes the headline concept.
- **Applies to:** all formats; decisive for technical/novel products.
- **Applies / fails when:** Essential for unfamiliar or breakthrough products. Less critical when you *are* the typical customer and already hold the knowledge intuitively.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — interviewing an engineer, you learn the watch's glowing display is only possible because a *laser* seals the capsule → headline "Laser Beam Digital Watch." Negative — writing "high-quality precision timepiece" because you never dug past the spec sheet.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Продаёшь российскую кофемолку: копаешь и узнаёшь, что жернова — из той же керамики, что в стоматологических борах → концепт "жернова медицинской точности". Механика — одна редкая специфическая деталь вместо общих слов "качественная кофемолка".
- **Book ref:** He spends days in the Sensor Watch lab, and the offhand fact that a laser is required to seal the radioactive capsule becomes the concept that sells millions.

### [ADW:03] Every Element Exists to Sell the First Sentence  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** Headline, subhead, photo, caption, logo, layout — none exist for their textbook purpose. Their single job is to get the reader into the *first sentence of the copy*. (Axiom 2.)
- **Copy move:** Judge every graphic/structural element by one test only: "Does this pull the eye toward the opening line?" Kill anything that draws attention sideways or feels self-contained.
- **Applies to:** landing pages, print/space ads, any visual-first layout.
- **Applies / fails when:** Reorients design decisions around reading momentum. Fails if taken to mean elements can be sloppy — each must still individually earn attention, just aimed forward.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — a hero image cropped so the model's gaze points straight at the first line of body copy. Negative — a gorgeous full-bleed photo that's admired and then the visitor bounces, never reaching the text.
- **Few-shot (RU):** На лендинге онлайн-школы подзаголовок и фото ведут взгляд ровно в первую строку текста, а не в отдельный красивый блок с наградами. Механика — каждый элемент тянет в первое предложение, "иконки-достижения" сбоку убираем.
- **Book ref:** He drills seminar students until they stop answering "the subhead gives more information" and accept the real answer: it exists to get the copy read.

### [ADW:04] The First Sentence Sells the Second  (ch 5)
- **Essence:** The sole purpose of the first sentence is to get the second read; the second's job is the third, and so on. (Axiom 3.) So the opener carries *zero* selling load — it only needs to be effortless. Make it short, simple, almost too incomplete to stop at.
- **Copy move:** Open with a tiny sentence — often three to five words. No benefits, no features, no multisyllable words. Like a locomotive leaving the station: the hardest work is the first few feet of motion.
- **Applies to:** all long-copy formats, email subject-to-body, VSL open.
- **Applies / fails when:** Best when readers aren't yet committed (cold traffic). A leading sales claim up front more often stalls momentum than helps.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — "It's easy." / "You're stuck." / "This may surprise you." as the first line, forcing the eye onward. Negative — "Our revolutionary AI-powered platform leverages machine learning to transform your workflow." — a wall the cold reader never climbs.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Первая строка письма о сервисе доставки: "Опять не привезли." Коротко, цепляет, тянет читать дальше. Механика — крошечное открывающее предложение без выгод, а не "Представляем инновационную логистическую платформу".
- **Book ref:** His typical JS&A openers — "Losing weight is not easy," "It's you against a computer" — are so short they barely qualify as sentences, and that's precisely why readers get sucked in.

### [ADW:05] Create the Buying Environment  (ch 6)
- **Essence:** The opening layout and first paragraphs must build the mood most conducive to *this* sale — the print equivalent of the art dealer walking you into a dim, music-filled private room before showing the painting. (Axiom 4.) In print you fully control the environment, so choose it deliberately.
- **Copy move:** Match tone, typography, and word choice to the product's price and nature: upscale/conservative for expensive/prestige; busy/loud/big-price-type for bargains. Set that environment in the first few paragraphs, alongside the reading momentum.
- **Applies to:** landing pages, catalogs, sales letters, VSL staging.
- **Applies / fails when:** Powerful when environment and product match. Fails on mismatch — a bargain-store frame around a luxury item (or vice versa) destroys credibility.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — a premium coaching page opens with calm, spacious prose, serif type, muted palette before any offer appears. Negative — selling a $5,000 mastermind on a page screaming "70% OFF!!! LIMITED!!!" in red — the frame contradicts the price.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Лендинг наставничества за 300к открывается спокойным, "дорогим" текстом и воздухом, а не кислотными баннерами "СКИДКА". Механика — среда должна соответствовать цене и природе продукта; премиум нельзя продавать в обёртке рынка-однодневки.
- **Book ref:** The Honolulu gallery moves him from the street into a carpeted back room with spotlights and classical music until he's ready to buy a $2,000 painting — the environment did the selling.

### [ADW:06] Harmonize — the Yes-Momentum  (ch 7)
- **Essence:** The reader and writer must "vibrate together." You build harmony by making statements that are true, agreeable, and relevant, so the reader keeps mentally nodding "yes." The moment they think "no" or "that's not me," the sale is lost. (Axiom 5.)
- **Copy move:** Open with statements the target *cannot help but agree with* — shared frustrations, obvious truths, felt realities. Speak the customer's own language and grievances back to them. Stack small yeses toward the eventual "yes" of the order.
- **Applies to:** all formats; core to cold-audience openers.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works when statements are accurate AND true to the reader's world. Fails if you fish for agreement with hype the reader distrusts — one perceived lie breaks harmony for good.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — "You've bought courses before. Most are recycled fluff. You want the actual system." Three nods, then the pitch. Negative — "You've always dreamed of financial freedom on a yacht" — assumes a life the reader may not want → instant "no."
- **Few-shot (RU):** Для сервиса аналитики Wildberries: "Ты сводишь отчёты в Excel по ночам. ЛК не показывает реальную маржу. Хочешь видеть прибыль по каждому SKU." Три "да" на реальных болях селлера. Механика — согласие на правдивых утверждениях, а не "мечтаешь о миллионах" (легко получить "нет").
- **Book ref:** The Food Crunch ad ties into the just-experienced fuel shortage so every reader nods; the car salesman's "Nice day, Mr. Jones" — "Yes" — is the same mechanism scaled to print.

### [ADW:07] Break Assumed Constraints  (ch 9)
- **Essence:** Like the circus elephant held by a stake it could easily pull, we obey limits that no longer (or never did) exist. In copy and marketing, don't rule out approaches, lists, prices, or channels because "everyone knows that won't work." (Axiom 7.)
- **Copy move:** When you catch "you can't sell X by mail / at that price / to that list," treat it as a hypothesis to test, not a fact. Step outside the problem (lateral thinking) to find the angle competitors ignore.
- **Applies to:** offer strategy, channel/price decisions, concept ideation.
- **Applies / fails when:** Pays off where the "constraint" is inherited belief. Genuinely binding limits (legal, physical, unit-economics) still apply — test, don't fantasize.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — "Nobody buys mattresses online without lying on them" → ship-in-a-box with 100-night trial builds a category. Negative — refusing to try long-form copy because "no one reads past the fold," so you never discover your buyers do.
- **Few-shot (RU):** "Юристов через Telegram-канал не продать — там несерьёзная аудитория" — гипотеза, а не факт: тестируешь и находишь платящих B2B-подписчиков. Механика — мнимое ограничение проверяем сплитом, а не хороним идею заранее.
- **Book ref:** Told his airplane couldn't fetch over $190k, he sold it for $240k in ten days; the "presidents-of-companies" mailing list he expected to flop was his best.

### [ADW:08] Seeds of Curiosity  (ch 10)
- **Essence:** Short forward-pointing lines — "But there's more." "Let me explain." "Now here comes the good part." — keep the reader sliding even where the copy naturally slows. Curiosity is the grease. (Axiom 8.)
- **Copy move:** End paragraphs (especially before a dull or transitional stretch) with a tiny teaser that promises payoff in the next line. Also plant a big open loop early ("read to the end to find out...") that only closes at the offer. Don't overdo it.
- **Applies to:** all long-copy; email, VSL, landing.
- **Applies / fails when:** Sustains readership through slow sections. Fails if overused (reads as manipulative filler) or if the promised payoff never lands.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — "...and that's when the numbers stopped making sense. Here's what I found." Negative — closing every paragraph with "But wait, there's more!" until it's noise and the reader tunes out.
- **Few-shot (RU):** В письме про кейс: "...и тут конверсия упала в три раза. Сейчас объясню, почему это оказалось удачей." Механика — короткий крючок перед абзацем-провалом; но не "а дальше — больше!" в каждом абзаце, иначе выгорает.
- **Book ref:** He deliberately withholds the end of the "Ginger" story, shunting it to an appendix, so the reader stays glued to the copywriting argument — curiosity weaponized structurally.

### [ADW:09] Copy as Emotion  (ch 11)
- **Essence:** People buy on emotion and justify with logic. Every word carries an emotional charge and tells a small story; a good ad is an emotional outpouring first, a fact-sheet second. (Emotion Principles 1–3.)
- **Copy move:** Lead with the emotional experience (the feeling of using/owning it, the fear or aspiration), then *supply* logical justifications so the buyer can defend the purchase to themselves and others. Choose words for their emotional connotation ("cottage" over "motel," "fix" over "repair").
- **Applies to:** all formats; strongest for prestige, lifestyle, identity goods.
- **Applies / fails when:** Wins on emotionally-driven categories. Even utilitarian/technical buys need the emotional layer — but don't fabricate facts to fit a feeling; reshape emphasis, not truth.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — sell the standing desk as "the 3pm slump that never comes, the energy to actually play with your kids after work," then cite the ergonomics study. Negative — a bulleted spec list (height range, motor wattage, weight capacity) with no feeling — technically complete, emotionally dead.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Умные часы: сначала "утром видишь, что выспался, и день идёт иначе", потом характеристики датчика пульса. Механика — эмоция ведёт, логика оправдывает; Caples поменял "repair" на "fix" и получил +20% — русский аналог: "починить" против "устранить поломку".
- **Book ref:** Two competitors sold the alphanumeric-display calculator on logic ("what alphanumeric means") and failed; his "Pocket Yellow Pages" opened on the *emotion* of standing at a payphone pulling numbers from your calculator — and won.

### [ADW:10] Sell the Concept, Not the Product  (ch 12)
- **Essence:** Never sell the product; sell a concept — the position, big idea, USP, angle. (Axiom 9.) Same object, different concept = wildly different results. Only exception: a product so new the product itself *is* the concept.
- **Copy move:** Before writing, name the concept in a phrase (often the headline). Combine, reframe, or challenge to manufacture a concept if the product doesn't hand you one. Even price can shift the concept.
- **Applies to:** positioning, headlines, campaign strategy.
- **Applies / fails when:** Essential in crowded/commodity markets. When a product is genuinely first-of-kind, sell the product plainly until competitors force differentiation.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — don't sell "a chess computer," sell "Soviet Challenge: American technology vs. the world champion." Negative — "Buy our chess computer, 16 levels, great price" — indistinguishable from every competitor.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Не "приложение для учёта финансов", а "конверт-система в телефоне: деньги на еду не утекут на такси". Механика — продаём концепт/позицию в заголовке; конкуренты продают "приложение для бюджета" и сливаются в фон.
- **Book ref:** His chess-computer sales tripled competitors' not because of the hardware but because of the *concepts* — "Soviet Challenge," "Karpov Accepts" — wrapped around it.

### [ADW:11] The Incubation Process  (ch 13)
- **Essence:** After intense preparation, the subconscious keeps working while you consciously step away. Incubation efficiency is governed by time, creative orientation, environment, and ego. (Axiom 10.) The breakthrough concept often arrives *after* you stop grinding.
- **Copy move:** Front-load the study and note-taking, then deliberately disengage — sleep on it, take a walk, switch tasks. Build a real time buffer into the schedule; don't demand the idea on command.
- **Applies to:** concept ideation, headline generation, stuck drafts.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works when preparation was thorough — incubation processes what you fed it. Fails as procrastination cover if you never did the prep.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — cram all product research Monday, draft nothing, walk the dog, and the headline surfaces Tuesday morning. Negative — staring at a blank doc for three hours forcing the angle, producing tired clichés.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Забил день на изучение продукта, вечером ушёл гулять — заголовок пришёл утром сам. Механика — сначала плотная подготовка, потом сознательное отключение; "высидеть" идею силой в пустом документе не работает.
- **Book ref:** He frames incubation as the subconscious "always working," and pairs it with left-brain/right-brain switching — the reason he tells writers to stop editing and go do something pleasurable (also step 6 of [ADW:18]).

### [ADW:12] Long Enough but Short Enough  (ch 14)
- **Essence:** There's no fixed right length. Copy should be exactly long enough to make the reader take the action — no more, no less. (Axiom 11.) Interest, not word count, is the constraint: intensely interested readers devour long copy; bored readers quit short copy.
- **Copy move:** Let two factors pull length up — higher price point (more justification needed) and more unusual product (more environment + explanation needed). Let low price / high perceived value / catalog-style visual environments pull it down. Never pad; never truncate the sale.
- **Applies to:** landing length, sales-letter, VSL runtime, catalog copy.
- **Applies / fails when:** Long copy wins when the reader is genuinely interested and the sale is complex/expensive. Short copy wins for cheap, familiar, visually-sold items. Fails when length is chosen by dogma instead of by the sale's needs.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — a $2,000 course gets a 4,000-word page that answers every objection; a $9 ebook gets three tight paragraphs. Negative — forcing 3,000 words onto a $9 impulse buy, or cramming a $2,000 offer into two lines "to respect attention spans."
- **Few-shot (RU):** Курс за 90к — длинный лендинг со снятием всех возражений; чек-лист за 490₽ — три абзаца. Механика — длину диктует цена и сложность продажи, а не мода на "короткие тексты" или на "лонгриды".
- **Book ref:** He proves interest drives length with the fill-in-the-blank exercise — put the reader's own name and street in a "you inherited millions" headline and they'll read all 3,000 words, because it's about them.

### [ADW:13] Personal Communication  (ch 15)
- **Essence:** Every communication should feel like one person writing to one person, in any medium. (Axiom 12.) Mass copy is still read by a single individual — so write eyeball-to-eyeball, using I / you / me, not a corporation addressing a crowd.
- **Copy move:** Adopt a byline (yours or a named person) so first-person voice is natural. Replace "We at ABC invite all of you..." with "Hi — I'd like to invite you..." Give the writing a personality; even a brand can read as a warm individual.
- **Applies to:** email, sales letters, landing, About pages, VSL host.
- **Applies / fails when:** Builds trust and closeness broadly. A too-folksy voice can misfit a formal/enterprise context — match register, but stay personal.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — "I built this because I was drowning in the same spreadsheets you are. Let me show you what fixed it." Negative — "Our organization is pleased to offer valued customers a suite of solutions." — a podium, not a person.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Письмо: "Я сам год сводил остатки вручную и ненавидел это. Покажу, что помогло." Механика — от первого лица, по имени, как один человек другому; не "Наша компания рада предложить уважаемым клиентам".
- **Book ref:** He contrasts the cold "We here at Consolidated..." thank-you with the warm "I just wanted to thank you personally... I even showed your order to our president" — same purpose, one reads as a human.

### [ADW:14] Logical Copy Sequence  (ch 16)
- **Essence:** Copy must flow downward in a logical order, anticipating the prospect's next question and answering it as if asked face-to-face. (Axiom 13.) You don't have the buyer in front of you, so you must engineer the sequence of questions and pre-empt each.
- **Copy move:** Block-diagram the ad top-to-bottom: hook → environment → what it is → why it's different → justify the purchase → lasting value → service/objections → offer → close. At each block ask "what would the reader now object to or wonder?" and put the answer next.
- **Applies to:** landing structure, sales letters, VSL script, email sequences.
- **Applies / fails when:** Produces the "he read my mind" effect. Fails if you answer questions in the wrong order (resolving objections the reader hasn't formed yet, or too late).
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — right after the price reveal, the next line handles "can I justify this?" with a cost-per-day comparison. Negative — burying the shipping/returns answer below the buy button, so the unanswered doubt kills the click.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Сразу после цены — блок "как это окупится" (стоимость в пересчёте на день), потом гарантия. Механика — предугадать следующий вопрос и ответить строго в его момент; не оставлять "а как вернуть?" ниже кнопки.
- **Book ref:** His Bally "Fireball" pinball ad is mapped block by block — interest, differentiation, justification (tax write-off!), lasting play value, service — each block answering the exact next question a buyer would raise.

### [ADW:15] The Editing Process  (ch 17)
- **Essence:** Editing turns the raw emotional dump into a tuning fork that vibrates with the reader: express exactly what you mean in the fewest words, keeping the same feeling. (Axiom 14.) This is where good copy separates from great.
- **Copy move:** Cut "that"-clauses and preambles ("it is important to note..."), combine sentences, delete unnecessary articles, rearrange for better flow, edit for rhythm (vary sentence length). Fewer words = less imposing + faster slide. Let time pass, then re-read; get a grammar check but keep your style.
- **Applies to:** all formats — the universal finishing pass.
- **Applies / fails when:** Always improves. Only fails if over-edited into sterility — don't cut the emotional beats or the rhythm along with the fat.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — "And if you've tried it, you know that losing weight is hard" → "Losing weight is hard." Negative — trimming so aggressively the voice flattens into a spec sheet, killing the feeling you worked to create.
- **Few-shot (RU):** "Стоит отметить, что наш сервис позволяет вам экономить время" → "Сервис экономит ваше время." Механика — режем преамбулы и слова-паразиты, сохраняя эмоцию и ритм; не выхолащиваем голос до сухого перечня.
- **Book ref:** He shows a bathroom-scale opener cut from 66 to 43 words (~40% shorter) with identical or better emotional impact, and lists his editing rules: kill "that," combine, delete "the," reorder for flow, edit for rhythm.

### [ADW:16] Sell a Cure, Not a Preventive  (ch 20)
- **Essence:** Cures sell easily and command high prices; preventives sell poorly because people assume the affliction won't hit them. (Axiom 15.) The lever: reframe a preventive *as* a cure, or emphasize its curative aspects.
- **Copy move:** First classify the product — cure or preventive? If preventive, either target people already "afflicted" (recently robbed → alarm) or foreground what it *cures/fixes right now* (engine knock, failed emissions) and underplay what it merely prevents. Ride market trends that shift a preventive into cure territory.
- **Applies to:** offer framing, product selection, headline angle.
- **Applies / fails when:** Powerful when a curative frame is honest. Fails/ethically risky if you invent an affliction, or when the product truly only prevents and no current pain exists.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — sell a backup service on "recover the files you *just* lost" (cure) to people mid-crisis, not only "protect against future loss" (preventive). Negative — a landing page selling antivirus purely as "someday you might get a virus" — easy to ignore.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Сервис резервных копий: на аудиторию, которая уже потеряла данные — "верни файлы, которые пропали вчера" (лечение), а не только "защитись на будущее" (профилактика). Механика — профилактику подаём как лечение текущей боли; "когда-нибудь может случиться" читатель игнорирует.
- **Book ref:** The Midex alarm, a preventive, sold to the recently-robbed as a cure; "The Pill" fuel treatment is pitched on QVC by what it *cures* (knock, ping, failed emissions), not what it prevents.

### [ADW:17] Rate Your Writing Level  (ch 21)
- **Essence:** Clarity widens the market. The Gunning Fog Index estimates the school grade needed to read your copy; lower grade = broader audience. Most breakthrough mass ads read at a strikingly low level.
- **Copy move:** Sample ~100 words, compute average sentence length + % of 3+-syllable words, ×0.4 = grade level. Lower it for mass markets with short sentences and plain words; allow higher for upscale niches. Check several blocks for consistency — but don't chase a low score at the expense of rhythm and sense.
- **Applies to:** any format; QA pass on finished copy.
- **Applies / fails when:** Useful as a clarity gauge and mass-market check. Fails if treated as a target to game — mechanical simplification can wreck flow; some audiences want higher register.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — a DTC supplement page reworked from grade 11 to grade 5 (short sentences, everyday words) and conversion widens. Negative — "utilize," "facilitate," "leverage synergies" on a mass-market page — high Fog, narrow reach.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Лендинг БАДа переписан с "осуществляет нормализацию метаболических процессов" на "помогает не срываться на сладкое" — проще, шире охват. Механика — короткие предложения и бытовые слова для массового рынка; канцелярит сужает аудиторию.
- **Book ref:** His most successful ad, "Vision Breakthrough," opens at roughly a fifth-grade reading level — deliberately comprehensible to the widest possible audience.

### [ADW:18] Seven Steps to Great Copy  (ch 22)
- **Essence:** The whole method compressed into a repeatable sequence: (1) become the product expert, (2) know the prospect, (3) write headline + subhead to earn the first sentence, (4) dump the copy without judging, (5) edit for fewest words, (6) incubate, (7) take a final look.
- **Copy move:** Run the steps in order every time; steps 5–6 can loop until satisfied. Headlines short; subheads ~16 words; first sentences as short as possible. It's a checklist, not inspiration-on-demand.
- **Applies to:** the production workflow for any piece.
- **Applies / fails when:** Reliable for consistent output. Fails if steps are skipped (no expertise → weak concept; no incubation → no final polish); it presupposes you've internalized [ADW:01]–[ADW:17].
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — a solo founder runs the 7 steps as a literal Notion checklist per landing page, hitting "incubate" overnight before the final look. Negative — jumping straight to step 4 (writing) with no product/prospect research, then shipping with no incubation.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Основатель прогоняет 7 шагов чек-листом на каждый лендинг: между "редактурой" и "финальным взглядом" — ночь на инкубацию. Механика — порядок шагов соблюдаем всегда; прыжок сразу к письму без изучения продукта/аудитории = слабый концепт.
- **Book ref:** Sugarman presents the seven steps as the crystallized summary of Sections One–Two — expertise and prospect-knowledge first, then draft-edit-incubate-review — and guarantees improvement with each repetition.
