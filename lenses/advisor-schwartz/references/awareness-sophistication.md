# Breakthrough Advertising — Eugene Schwartz  ·  prefix [BTA]
> Two diagnostic axes decide the headline before a word is written: how much the prospect already knows (**awareness**) and how many rival claims they have already heard (**sophistication**). When you must pick a headline strategy, locate the market on both scales first.

## [BTA:16] The Five States of Awareness  (ch 2)
The gap between prospect and purchase, measured as knowledge. The more aware, the shorter the headline and the more mechanical the job; the less aware, the more the headline must *create* recognition before it can sell. Your headline **begins at the prospect's current state**, never behind it (over-explaining bores) and never ahead of it (leaping a psychological wall loses them).

| # | State | What the prospect knows | Headline / lead strategy |
|---|-------|-------------------------|--------------------------|
| 1 | **Most Aware** | Knows the product, knows what it does, already wants it — just hasn't bought. | Name + price/offer. The deal *is* the headline. No persuasion; the least creative job. Sole full-selling-job headline (product name + price reduction). |
| 2 | **Product-Aware** | Knows your product but isn't sold — unconvinced it's better, or unaware of a new improvement. | Lead with product name + a **superiority claim**; body elaborates the superiority (proof, sharper image, new mechanism, wider use). The bulk of all advertising lives here. |
| 3 | **Solution-Aware** (desire-aware) | Wants the result the product delivers, but doesn't know a product like yours exists. | **Crystallize the desire/solution** in the headline, then attach it to your product. Name the still-amorphous want sharply; prove it's achievable; show your product is the mechanism. |
| 4 | **Problem-Aware** (need-aware) | Feels a problem/need, recognizes it, but hasn't connected it to any product. | **Name the problem (and/or its solution)**, dramatize the need until it stings, then present the product as the inevitable fix. The classic problem-solution ad. |
| 5 | **Completely Unaware** | Doesn't know the desire/need, or won't admit it, or it can't be put into words. Also where exhausted/"dead" markets land. | Do **not** open with price or product name (both mean nothing here). Enter through **identity, emotion, or a wider accepted image** — mirror the prospect back to themselves, then bridge to the desire, then the product. Hardest, most creative job. |

**Wall rule:** each state is separated from the next by a psychological wall — indifference on one side, intense interest on the other. A headline built for one state fails when aimed at another, and fails even in its own market once that market moves to a new state. Markets drift *down* awareness (more prospects, colder) and *up* sophistication (more claims heard) over a product's life; re-diagnose over time.

**Ch-2 seven sub-tasks inside State 2 (Product-Aware) — what the superiority headline can do:**
(a) reinforce the existing desire · (b) sharpen the image of *how* it satisfies · (c) extend *where/when* it satisfies · (d) add new proof/documentation · (e) announce a new mechanism that satisfies better · (f) announce a new mechanism that removes an old limitation · (g) change image/mechanism entirely to escape competitors — task (g) is the doorway into the **sophistication** problem ([BTA:17]).

## [BTA:17] The Five Stages of Market Sophistication  (ch 3)
Independent of awareness. Measures **how many competitors and claims the prospect has already been exposed to** in your category. It decides not *what* you promise but *how* you must phrase the promise so it still registers. The one question: *how many products have been here before me?*

| # | Stage | Market state | Claim / mechanism strategy |
|---|-------|--------------|----------------------------|
| 1 | **First to market** | No prior claims; market unsophisticated. | Be simple, direct, un-fancy. State the **bare claim or need** in the headline, dramatize it, bring in the product, prove it. Nothing more is needed. |
| 2 | **Second in** | Direct claim still works but has been made. | **Copy the winning claim and outbid it** — enlarge, drive it to the limit of believability. Bigger number, faster, more (e.g. escalating "lose X lbs", horsepower race). |
| 3 | **Claims exhausted** | Prospect has heard every claim and every extreme; disbelief sets in. | Stop competing on the promise — introduce a **NEW MECHANISM**. Shift emphasis from *what it does* → *HOW it works*. Mechanism goes in the headline to restore freshness/belief; the full claim is restated in the lead. |
| 4 | **Mechanisms exhausted** | Competitors copied your mechanism; a mechanism race is on. | **Elaborate/enlarge the mechanism** — make it easier, quicker, surer, remove limitations, add benefits. Mirrors Stage 2 but on mechanism instead of claim. Same escalation, same eventual ceiling. |
| 5 | **Market exhausted / "dead"** | No longer believes any advertising; doesn't want to be aware of the product at all. | Abandon promise+mechanism. Win through **identification** — bring the prospect into the ad through *who they are*, not what they want. Pull them in by mirrored identity, then reintroduce desire. |

**Escalation logic:** Stages 1→2 escalate the **claim**; 3→4 escalate the **mechanism**; each escalation ceiling forces the jump to the next tool (claim → bigger claim → new mechanism → bigger mechanism → identity). Belief erodes at each step (heavy-hype markets auto-discount claims ~50%), which is what pushes the market forward.

## How the two axes interact
- **Orthogonal, read together.** Awareness = *content* of the headline (what to say). Sophistication = *form/angle* of the claim (how to say it so it survives the noise). A Solution-Aware market (awareness 3) that is also claims-exhausted (soph 3) needs the desire crystallized **through a new mechanism**, not a bare promise.
- **The extremes converge.** Sophistication Stage 5 (exhausted market) collapses into Awareness Stage 5 (unaware market): both stop responding to desire/claims and can only be re-entered through **identification** ([BTA:07]). Schwartz treats them as the same strategic problem.
- **Awareness sub-task (g) = the sophistication trigger.** When a Product-Aware headline must "change the image/mechanism to escape competition," you have crossed from a pure awareness question into a sophistication question.
- **Both are moving targets.** Left alone, markets slide *down* the awareness scale (colder, broader prospects) and *up* the sophistication scale (more claims absorbed). Yesterday's winning headline dies on both counts — re-diagnose each campaign.
- **Downstream techniques.** Sophistication Stage 3–4 is where **Mechanization** ([BTA:10]) becomes the primary lever; Stage 5 / Awareness 5 is where **Identification** ([BTA:07]) and **Gradualization** ([BTA:08]) carry the copy. Awareness 3–4 leans on **Intensification** ([BTA:06]) to dramatize the desire/need before the product appears.
