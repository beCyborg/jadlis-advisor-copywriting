# Breakthrough Advertising — Eugene Schwartz  ·  prefix [BTA]
> Advertising's power comes from mass desire that already exists in the market — the copy's only job is to channel that desire onto one product and intensify belief in it. This lens dominates whenever you must decide *what to say and in what order* to move a reader from indifference to purchase.

## Principles

### [BTA:01] Mass Desire & the Amplification Effect  (ch 1)
- **Essence:** The urge to buy comes from the market, not the copy. Copy cannot manufacture desire; it can only seize the hopes, fears and wants already alive in millions and focus them onto one product. Directing an existing desire commands an economic force far larger than the ad budget (the *Amplification Effect* — $1 of ad → many $ of sales); trying to *create* desire degrades advertising into education, which barely returns its cost.
- **Copy move:** Before writing, inventory the mass desires already moving through your market (permanent forces — instincts, unsolved technical problems; forces of change — style, trend, mass education) and pick one that already exists to harness. Never try to talk people into a want they don't have.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL — the foundational diagnosis for all of them.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies when a real, pre-existing desire exists to tap. Fails for genuinely novel categories where no desire yet exists — there you must first ride an adjacent existing desire, not educate from zero on your own budget.
- **Few-shot (EN):** For a cold-brew sleep aid, don't teach people to want magnesium — hook the already-raging desire to "fall asleep in 15 minutes without grogginess." Negative: "Discover the science of GABA receptors" tries to educate a want into being and burns budget.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Онлайн-курс по Excel — не «объясняем, зачем нужны сводные таблицы» (создание желания с нуля), а цепляем существующий страх «уйти с работы в 20:00 из-за отчёта»: «Считаете отчёты вручную до ночи? Сводная таблица делает это за 3 минуты».
- **Book ref:** Cars that fought mass desire (Chrysler's short-squat 1948 model, Ford selling safety in the horsepower era, the Edsel against the compact-car switch) drew huge budgets yet failed, because they ran against the tide instead of riding it.

### [BTA:02] Channel, Don't Fight, the Trend  (ch 1)
- **Essence:** A mass desire has a *direction* as well as a magnitude. You must detect the trend early, gauge its force and direction, and harness your product onto its back — pointing the product where the desire is already flowing. Fighting the direction, however good the product, meets a wall of indifference.
- **Copy move:** Continuously chart which of the several desires built into your product is rising *right now*, stress that one in the headline, and be ready to shift the angle when the trend turns. Timing (when the shift becomes statistically significant) is the copywriter's ongoing job.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form | email — anywhere the angle/positioning is chosen.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies when multiple appeals live in one product and a trend favors one. Fails if you lock a single evergreen angle and ignore a market shift — yesterday's winning appeal goes dead.
- **Few-shot (EN):** A protein bar built on "muscle gain" pivots its headline to "no crash, steady energy" when the market's attention shifts from bulking to sustained-energy/GLP-1-era eating. Negative: keeping "pack on mass" copy while the audience has moved to appetite control.
- **Few-shot (RU):** VPN-сервис: в 2021 угол «смотри Netflix из любой страны», в 2024 разворот на «доступ к заблокированным рабочим сервисам и безопасность» — тот же продукт, но headline едет за сменившимся спросом.
- **Book ref:** The horsepower appeal of the 1950s and its sudden subordination to gas economy — the copywriter must catch such "straws in the wind" and switch appeals before competitors do.

### [BTA:03] Product = What It Does, Not What It Is  (ch 1)
- **Essence:** Every product is two products: the *physical* one (steel, glass, code, ingredients) and the *functional* one (the series of benefits it performs). The physical product never sells — people pay for what it *does*. Physical facts have value only as later documentation of the promised performance.
- **Copy move:** Build the headline on the functional benefit (what it does for the reader); demote every physical spec into the body as *proof* that justifies price and documents quality. No physical attribute may become the headline.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | VSL — headline vs body-copy division of labor.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies universally. The trap: engineer-founders headline the spec ("titanium chassis," "256-bit encryption") instead of the outcome it buys.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Headline the doing: "Your files, safe even if your laptop is stolen" (functional). Push the spec down: "…protected by 256-bit AES encryption" becomes body proof. Negative headline: "Now with 256-bit AES encryption."
- **Few-shot (RU):** Матрас: заголовок — «Просыпаетесь без боли в пояснице» (что делает), а «независимые пружины + 7 зон жёсткости» уходит в тело как доказательство и оправдание цены. Плохо: заголовок «Матрас с независимым пружинным блоком».
- **Book ref:** No one buys the steel in a car, the glass in a vase or the tobacco in a cigarette; the physical product is merely the "excuse for charging the price" once the performance is promised.

### [BTA:04] Three Dimensions of Mass Desire  (ch 1)
- **Essence:** Every mass desire has three dimensions: **urgency/intensity** (degree of demand — arthritic pain vs. a minor headache), **staying power** (repetition, inability to be satiated — raw hunger vs. a gourmet craving), and **scope** (how many people share it). A product touches several desires, but only one can predominate in the headline; choosing that desire is the single most important decision in the ad — get it wrong and nothing else matters.
- **Copy move:** List every desire the product can plausibly serve, score each on the three dimensions, and lead with the one that maximizes all three at the moment of publication. Embody that choice in the headline.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form | VSL — the top-of-funnel angle decision.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies when the product serves multiple desires. Fails when you default to the desire *you* find most interesting rather than the one strongest across the three dimensions for the market.
- **Few-shot (EN):** A budgeting app could sell "organize your spending" (low urgency, wide scope) or "get out of the debt that's wrecking your sleep" (high urgency + high staying power) — lead with the debt-anxiety desire. Negative: leading with "beautiful spending charts" (weak on all three).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Стоматология может продавать «красивую улыбку» (широко, но не срочно) или «снять острую зубную боль сегодня» (максимум срочности) — для дежурного приёма ведём заголовком боль, а не эстетику.
- **Book ref:** More men will pay $10 for a gas-saving auto accessory than for one that merely prevents future repair bills — same price, but the desire with greater urgency and scope wins.

### [BTA:05] Raw Materials of the Mind: Desires · Identifications · Beliefs  (ch 6)
- **Essence:** The headline only stops the prospect; body copy sells by altering three dimensions of the mind. **Desires** — the physical/material/sensual wants that drive them (can't be created, only sharpened and given a goal). **Identifications** — the roles and self-image the prospect wants the product to project (each purchase does double duty: lose weight *and* become the radiant person). **Beliefs** — the prejudices and reasoning patterns that form a filter your claims must pass; you build on the prospect's logic, never argue with it head-on.
- **Copy move:** Diagnose which dimension your ad must move most, and budget copy length accordingly: how much to build *desire* ([BTA:06]), how much to make the reader feel *complimented/at home* via identity ([BTA:07]), how much to overcome *disbelief* ([BTA:08]/[BTA:10]). Weave all three into one path, don't treat them separately in the final draft.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email — body-copy architecture; long-form especially.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to any persuasion beyond a pure price offer. Fails if you pour all copy into desire while ignoring the belief filter — an intense but unbelieved claim still gets discounted ~50%.
- **Few-shot (EN):** A career-coaching page stacks desire ("the corner office"), identity ("the kind of leader people follow"), and belief ("here's the framework, with the logic you already trust"). Negative: hammering desire alone at a skeptical audience with no proof scaffold.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Продукт для инвесторов-новичков: желание («первый пассивный доход»), идентичность («ты уже не тратишь всё до зарплаты, ты инвестор»), убеждение («на понятной логике: вот как индексный фонд обгоняет вклад») — все три сплетены в один поток.  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
- **Book ref:** A woman buying low-calorie food gets thin (desire) and also rebuilds a youthful, attractive personality (identification); a man buys a car for transport and for the prestige it signals to neighbors — every purchase serves a double duty.

### [BTA:06] Intensification — Strengthen the Desire  (ch 7)
- **Essence:** Desires as they exist are vague blurs with a fraction of their potential power. Intensification fills them with concrete images — one vivid scene of fulfillment after another — until the want overwhelms skepticism, lethargy and price. You are the scriptwriter of the prospect's dreams: show every tomorrow the product makes possible.
- **Copy move:** Work Schwartz's thirteen levers to strengthen the claim/desire, roughly in copy order: (1) first presentation of the claim, (2) put the claim in action, (3) bring in the reader ("you"), (4) show how to test it, (5) stretch benefits out over time, (6) bring in an audience, (7) show experts approving, (8) compare/contrast/prove superiority, (9) picture the black side too (the pain of *not* having it), (10) show how easy it is to get, (11) use metaphor/analogy/imagination, (12) summarize before closing, (13) put the guarantee to work. In small space, use *compression* (boil images to key words) + *the campaign* (repeat across many ads).
- **Applies to:** VSL | landing | email — the desire-building engine; scale count of levers to available space.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies when the reader already shares the desire and needs it magnified. Fails when overused into empty repetition/boredom, or when belief hasn't been earned first — intensity without believability reads as hype.
- **Few-shot (EN):** For a sleep aid: action ("you're out before the chapter ends"), future-stretch ("wake up sharp every morning this month"), black side ("no more 3 a.m. ceiling-staring"), test ("try it 30 nights, feel the difference by night three"). Negative: repeating "amazing sleep!" five times with no concrete image.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Курс английского: в действии («заказываешь кофе в Лиссабоне без запинки»), растягивание во времени («через полгода — собеседование в международной компании»), тёмная сторона («хватит молча кивать на созвонах»), эксперт («методика преподавателя Кембриджа»). Плохо: пять раз «отличный курс!» без картинок.
- **Book ref:** Advertising is "salesmanship in print" and "the literature of desire" — its core function is to take unformulated desire and translate it into vivid scene after scene of fulfillment, adding concrete satisfactions to the basic drive.

### [BTA:07] Identification — Sell the Role  (ch 8)
- **Essence:** People buy to become who they want to be. Two role types: **Character Roles** (personality traits, usually adjectives — chic, brilliant, well-read, disciplined) and **Achievement Roles** (status/title nouns — Executive, Good Mother, Career Woman, Founder). A role is worthless until acknowledged, so a product serves it three ways: help *achieve* mastery, *speed/simplify* mastery, or act as a visible *symbol* of it for others to admire. Identification is also the only lever left in an exhausted market (soph 5 / awareness 5).
- **Copy move:** Decide which role your prospect is buying into, then build that role into the product so purchasing it lets them step into the identity — and give them a symbol to display. Beyond the physical benefit, promise the self-definition.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email — especially premium, lifestyle, status, and "dead-market" revival copy.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies when the purchase signals identity or status. Fails / backfires when the claimed role exceeds what the prospect will believe about themselves, or for pure commodity/price buys where no identity is at stake.
- **Few-shot (EN):** A mechanical keyboard sold not as "tactile switches" but "the desk of someone who ships serious code" (character role), with the product as the visible symbol on their stream. Negative: assigning a role the buyer rejects — "for elite 10x engineers" alienates the humble competent majority.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Онлайн-школа программирования продаёт не «выучишь Python», а роль «ты — разработчик, которого зовут в команду» (achievement role): диплом и портфолио — символ для показа рекрутеру и родителям. Плохо: навязать роль «гений IT», в которую новичок не верит.
- **Book ref:** A book on philosophy, a speed-reading course, and a fine bookshelf all serve the same "well-read" character role — achieve it, speed it, and display it; in America one is known "by the products we own," not function alone.

### [BTA:08] Gradualization — Build Belief Before the Claim  (ch 9)
- **Essence:** Desire and identification are never enough without **Belief**, which fused with desire yields Absolute Conviction — the real goal. Belief is emotional security; asking a prospect to overturn an established belief is asking them to become a child again, and they won't. Rule: violate a belief even slightly and no promise can save the ad; channel the force of belief behind even one small claim and it outsells all your competitors' half-doubted promises. Gradualization builds a *bridge of belief* — start from a fact the prospect already accepts and lead them through a chain of ever-more-remote facts, each pre-accepted, to your claim.
- **Copy move:** Open with something the prospect already believes as true; never contradict their worldview head-on; advance one small agreed step at a time (use their logic, not yours) until the big claim arrives as the inevitable next step. Exploit belief *structures* too — contingency ("if…then"), promise-then-proof cadence, paragraph parallelism that borrows acceptance for the next line.
- **Applies to:** VSL | landing | email — long-form belief-building; cold or skeptical traffic.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies when the claim is a leap from the prospect's current beliefs. Fails the instant a sentence clashes with their existing convictions — the rest of the ad dies with it.
- **Few-shot (EN):** For a screen-time app: start with the agreed fact ("you already check your phone ~90×/day — you've seen the number"), step to "each check costs ~20 min of refocus," then to the claim "so reclaiming attention is worth more than any productivity hack." Negative: opening with "social media is destroying your brain" (contradicts a belief, triggers defense).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Продукт для сна: начать с принятого факта («вы знаете, что кофе после 16:00 мешает уснуть»), шаг — «свет экрана действует похоже, только незаметно», затем claim — «поэтому очки с фильтром вечером важнее, чем ещё одна таблетка мелатонина». Плохо: сразу «вы всё делаете неправильно» — атака на убеждение.
- **Book ref:** Belief is laid down in childhood and defended for life; the ad's job is not to change the prospect's "facts" but to *extend* them — building a comfortable succession of accepted steps from today's beliefs to the claim.

### [BTA:09] Redefinition — Dissolve Objections  (ch 10)
- **Essence:** Many products carry built-in handicaps that repel the prospect. Three drawback categories — **too complicated** (hard to use), **not important enough** (market feels too narrow/trivial), **too expensive** — met by three redefinitions: **Simplification**, **Escalation** (raise the perceived importance/stakes), **Price Reduction** (reframe the cost). The sharpest form is a *flip-flop* (concept-judo): turn the liability itself into the asset, ideally before the prospect notices it's a drawback.
- **Copy move:** Name the product's biggest objection, pick the matching redefinition, and re-frame: "it's *this*, not *that*." Where possible, invert the very weakness into the proof of the benefit.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form | email — objection-handling blocks; pricing sections.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies whenever a known drawback suppresses the sale. Fails if the redefinition is dishonest or transparently strained — the prospect feels the spin and trust collapses.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Flip-flop on price: a $2,000 course reframed — "expensive because we cap cohorts at 20 and refund anyone who doesn't ship" — the high price *becomes* the proof of quality/scarcity. Simplification: "setup is one paste of a code snippet." Negative: "yes it's pricey but worth it" (concedes the objection without redefining it).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Flip-flop: «долгая доставка 3 недели» переопределяется в актив — «мебель едет 3 недели, потому что её собирают под ваш размер вручную, а не достают со склада» (недостаток → доказательство кастомности). Escalation: «это не просто блокнот — это система, которая решает, где утекает ваш день».
- **Book ref:** Lifebuoy's off-putting medicinal odor was flipped into the asset via the "B.O." campaign — the strong smell became the visible proof of odor-destroying power you could smell through the wrapper.

### [BTA:10] Mechanization — Prove the Mechanism  (ch 11)
- **Essence:** After the prospect *wants* the promise, they ask "**How does it work?**" Mechanization is verbal proof (classic "reason-why" copy) that demonstrates in words *how* the product delivers the result. The question is never whether to include mechanism but *how much* — determined by awareness/sophistication. Three stages: **Name** the mechanism (when the market already understands it — just label it and win on price/features), **Describe** it (when it's unfamiliar or the market is tired of identical claims), **Feature** it (make the mechanism the headline/hero — the Stage-3 sophistication move [BTA:17]).
- **Copy move:** Match mechanism depth to the market: name-only for aware/simple markets; full description when belief is thin or claims are exhausted; feature the mechanism up front when everyone shares the same promise and you need a fresh point of difference.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | short-form — proof sections; category with parity claims.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies when the promise is believed but the delivery is doubted, or the market is claims-exhausted (soph 3–4). Fails when over-described to an already-convinced market (bores them) or when the mechanism is fake/unintelligible.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Feature the mechanism: "Sleeps you faster because it raises core-to-skin heat transfer — the same trick hospitals use," then restate the full claim below. Name-only for an aware market: "…with the M4 chip." Negative: three paragraphs explaining SSL to buyers who already trust "bank-level security."
- **Few-shot (RU):** Feature: «Худеет не за счёт голода, а потому что блокирует усвоение части жиров — вот как именно…», затем полный claim. Name-only: «…на новом процессоре Snapdragon». Плохо: подробно объяснять устройство редуктора тому, кто уже верит бренду.
- **Book ref:** A camera ad in an aware market simply *names* its mechanisms (Electronic Light Setter, Push-Button Zomar Lens) in bold and competes on price; a tired or unfamiliar market instead demands the mechanism be described or featured.

### [BTA:11] Concentration — Destroy the Alternatives  (ch 12)
- **Essence:** Concentration destroys every *other* way the prospect could satisfy the same desire — not by mere attack, but by proving rival solutions ineffectual *while showing your product supplies what they lack*. Iron rule: never attack a weakness unless you supply the cure in the same breath, and never for your own gain alone — frame the attack as *in the prospect's service*, or it reads as biased disparagement and breeds skepticism. It's a space-heavy technique that combines Intensification, Gradualization and Mechanization at once.
- **Copy move:** Name the alternative the prospect would otherwise choose (competitor, DIY, doing nothing), prove its weakness *for their sake*, and immediately show your product removing that weakness with a mechanism. Pair every attack with a solution.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL — comparison/objection sections; switching campaigns.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies when a dominant alternative holds the prospect's loyalty. Fails when the attack is unpaired with a solution or is obviously self-serving — it evokes dislike, not doubt.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Willpower diets fail because they fight your hunger hormones — here's the mechanism — this works *with* them instead" (attack + cure + why). Negative: "Competitor X is garbage" with no demonstration of how you're better — reads as biased.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Бесплатные заметки теряют ваши данные при сбое — вот почему — а здесь всё шифруется и синхронизируется, вот механизм»: атака на альтернативу всегда в связке с решением и в интересах пользователя. Плохо: «конкурент — отстой» без доказательства своего преимущества.
- **Book ref:** The "fire injector" spark-plug ad destroys confidence in ordinary plugs by explaining the physics (burning electrode → widening gap → misfire → wasted gas) *and* proving its own mechanism removes exactly that flaw.

### [BTA:12] Camouflage — Borrow Believability  (ch 13)
- **Essence:** Instead of building belief from scratch, *borrow* it from wherever society already stores trust. Readers trust a publication/platform and let that trust spill from editorial onto the ads it carries; its familiar style even develops a "believability reflex" — its phraseology carries an aura of truth by itself. Three ways: match the medium's **format**, adopt its **editorial phraseology/tone**, and borrow from **trusted authorities/sources**. Minimize the reader's mental gear-shift from "content" to "ad."
- **Copy move:** Make the ad wear the trusted environment's clothes — its layout, headline-to-body transition, illustrations, and voice — so credibility transfers. (Modern analog: native/advertorial content that reads like the platform's organic posts.)
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email — native ads, advertorials, platform-native content, PR-style pieces.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies where a trusted medium's credibility can transfer. Fails / harms trust when it becomes deceptive impersonation — must respect disclosure and not defraud (ethical + legal boundary the modern advisor enforces).
- **Few-shot (EN):** A skincare ad written and laid out like the magazine's own "expert Q&A" column, borrowing its voice and structure so it reads as trusted editorial. Negative: a screaming "BUY NOW!!!" banner dropped into a calm long-read feed — maximum gear-shift, zero borrowed trust.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Промо-материал в Telegram-канале, оформленный как обычный авторский пост канала (тот же тон, верстка, эмодзи-разделители), а не как явный рекламный баннер — доверие к каналу переносится. Плохо: кричащий «КУПИ СЕЙЧАС!!!» посреди спокойной аналитической ленты.
- **Book ref:** The same book ad, "shotgunned" house-set across a dozen magazines, was only mildly successful; rebuilt feature-for-feature to match the *Wall Street Journal's* format, it became enormously successful — a single format change can add ~50% to readership.

### [BTA:13] Verbalization — 38 Ways to Strengthen the Headline  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** Once the appeal and headline *content* are chosen ([BTA:04]), Verbalization shapes *how* it's said. The bare claim is best when you're first ([BTA:17] stage 1); when competitive or complex, bind extra images to the claim through word choice. It does three jobs: **strengthen** (measure it, quantify, make vivid), **refresh** (twist, re-angle, narrate, challenge), **pull in** (question the reader, tease information, partly reveal the mechanism). Formulas polish an existing idea — they never generate one ([BTA:14]).
- **Copy move:** Take the chosen claim and run it through concrete devices — put a number on it, put a clock on it, ask a question, hint at a secret, turn it into a mini-story — to sharpen impact and draw the reader into the body. Invent your own variations; the list is open-ended.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email — headline, subject line, hook, ad copy.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies after the core idea exists. Fails when used *as* the idea — clever wording on a weak/wrong appeal is lipstick on the wrong claim.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Measure it: "Cut your AWS bill 41% in one afternoon" beats "Save on cloud costs." Question-pull: "Why do senior devs quietly hate your codebase?" Negative: a witty pun headline over an appeal nobody wants — verbalizing a dead idea.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Измерить: «Сократите отчёт с 3 часов до 8 минут» сильнее, чем «Экономьте время на отчётах». Вопрос-крючок: «Почему ваши подписчики отписываются на второй неделе?». Плохо: остроумный заголовок поверх приза, который никому не нужен.
- **Book ref:** "20,000 FILTER TRAPS in Viceroy," "I am 61 pounds lighter," "In two seconds, Bayer aspirin begins…" — the same claim measured, timed, or quantified lands far harder than its bare statement.

### [BTA:14] Three Levels of Creativity  (ch 5)
- **Essence:** Three ways copywriters reach a headline: (1) **Word-Substitute** — swap your product into a proven headline; shallowest, produces "Echo Ads" that only remind people of the original and ignore the unique product-market-timing fit; (2) **Formulas** — pour the idea into memorized rules; valid only for *verbalizing* an idea you already have ([BTA:13]), never for finding it; (3) **Analytical** — the deep method: derive the solution from *this* product, *this* market, *this* moment. Only the analytical level fits the unique problem; its solution can be used only once, so the effort must be repeated for every ad. Motivation research/customer insight is a *direction*, not a headline — the writer still has to transform the fact into an idea.
- **Copy move:** Refuse to reuse old winners or drop the claim into a template. For each product, re-run the analysis: what desire ([BTA:04]), what awareness/sophistication ([BTA:16]/[BTA:17]), what belief ([BTA:08]) — then build the one headline that fits only this situation. Treat research as a pointer to the answer, not the answer.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form | VSL — the meta-discipline behind every headline.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to every fresh product/market. Fails as soon as you "argue from your old files" — copying yourself drags talent down to file-cabinet mediocrity, and echo ads waste the unique fit.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Analytical: study why *this* audience distrusts *this* category *now*, then write the only headline that fits. Negative (echo): "Got Milk?" → "Got Leads?" borrowed onto an unrelated SaaS with no situational fit.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Аналитический уровень: разобрать, почему именно эта аудитория не доверяет именно этой категории именно сейчас, и написать заголовок под этот стык. Плохо (эхо): «Ведь ты этого достойна» → «Ведь твой бизнес этого достоин» — чужой шаблон без привязки к ситуации.
- **Book ref:** "I'd walk a mile for a cup of BLANK coffee" is a word-substitute echo ad — it borrows a famous line but ignores the product-market-timing relationship that gave the original its power.

### [BTA:15] The Final Touches  (ch 14)
- **Essence:** The polish that binds a draft into one unbroken pull toward purchase. **Verification** — offer authorities/proof to reassure the choice was wise. **Reinforcement** — make two claims do the work of four (layer/stack so each supports the next). **Interweaving** — blend emotion, image and logic into the *same* sentence rather than separate blocks. **Sensitivity / anticipation points** — feel the exact spot where the reader tires of one theme and demands another, and switch there (where many good ads break down). **Momentum** — draw the reader deeper paragraph by paragraph. **Mood** — deliberately pack the copy with the drama, excitement or sincerity the piece needs.
- **Copy move:** After the argument is built, pass through the draft as the *reader*: add proof at doubt-points, fuse feeling+picture+reason per sentence, find each anticipation point and answer it before the reader leaves, and tune the emotional mood end to end so momentum never breaks.
- **Applies to:** VSL | landing | email — final revision pass on long copy.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to any multi-section piece. Fails when techniques sit in isolated blocks (a proof wall here, an emotion wall there) so the reader stalls at the seams.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Interweave in one line: "The report that used to eat your Friday night (emotion) now finishes before your coffee cools (image), because it caches every query (logic)." Anticipation point: the instant a reader thinks "but is it secure?", the next line answers it. Negative: a dry feature list followed by a separate "and it feels amazing!" paragraph — no interweaving.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Сплетение в одной фразе: «Отчёт, который отнимал вечер пятницы (эмоция), теперь готов раньше, чем остынет кофе (образ), потому что кэширует каждый запрос (логика)». Точка ожидания: как только читатель подумал «а это безопасно?» — следующая строка отвечает. Плохо: сухой список функций, а потом отдельный абзац «и это круто!».
- **Book ref:** Schwartz warns of "anticipation points" — the several moments in an ad where the prospect loses interest in one theme and demands another; missing them is exactly where good ads break down and lose the reader.
