## Patterns

### [CASH:20] Readability engineering  (Secret #1: Psychology of Simplicity)
- **Pattern:** Copy that is effortless to read gets read; effortful copy gets abandoned. Lower reading friction with four prescriptions and target a high Flesch Reading Ease score (aim for ~60–70+, roughly 6th–8th-grade level). Readers give you only a fraction of their attention — make the words do zero work.
- **Skeleton (4 prescriptions):** (1) **Short simple words** — ~70–80% one-syllable; kill "$10 words" (tired not enervated). (2) **Short sentences** — one thought per sentence, ~11 words average. (3) **Short paragraphs** — the "short-short trick": pose a question/statement, then answer it in a 2–4 word next line; open with an ≤11-word paragraph; cap regular paragraphs at 4–5 short sentences. (4) **Personal pronouns** — flood with *you* and *I* (≥14 people-references per 100 words); turns mass communication into personal. Bonus: **paragraph leaders** (repeat "You learned…", "We guarantee…" to start successive paragraphs) set tempo and, via length-implies-strength, boost perceived credibility.
- **Applies to:** all — short-form | landing | email | VSL. The base layer under every other pattern.
- **Applies / fails when:** Universal. Don't over-apply into robotic staccato — vary sentence length so it reads naturally. Highly technical B2B audiences tolerate more jargon, but clarity still wins.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Want to make $10k a month? You can. Here's how." (FRES ~72, grade 6). Negative: "If you wish to acquire vast financial resources, please attend to the following privileged information proffered herein" (FRES ~34, college-level, dead on arrival).
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Хочешь зарабатывать больше? Можно. Сейчас покажу как.» — коротко, "ты", один смысл на предложение. Плохо: «В случае, если вы стремитесь к увеличению финансовых поступлений, обратите внимание на нижеизложенную информацию» — канцелярит, длинные слова, читатель уходит. (Russian has no exact Flesch analogue — apply the same mechanics: короткие слова, ~8–11 слов в предложении, обращение на «ты/вы».)
- **Book ref:** Whitman runs the same ice-cream offer as two paragraphs — the plain version scores 72 (movie-dialogue easy), the pompous version 34 (New York Review of Books) — and cites Flesch's "John loves Mary" (92) degrading to 32 as more words and vagueness pile on.

### [CASH:21] Benefit bombardment — feature → benefit  (Secret #2)
- **Pattern:** A *feature* is a component of the product; a *benefit* is what the prospect *gets* from it. The reader runs a nonstop WIIFM ("What's In It For Me?") loop — answer it by translating every feature into the value it delivers, and pile the benefits on. This is the wording-level execution of the Means-End Chain [CASH:07].
- **Skeleton (the "You benefit by…" drill):** For each feature, force-complete the sentence "You benefit by ___". If you can't, it's not selling. Format as `Feature → You benefit by → [terminal LF8/secondary want]`. Never let a feature stand alone; never lead with self-focused facts (your anniversary, new equipment, staff photos) unless they translate to a buyer benefit.
- **Applies to:** all — bullet blocks, spec sections, body copy.
- **Applies / fails when:** Universal. Fails when copy lists raw features ("multiple ink tanks, 500-sheet tray") and assumes the reader will infer value — they won't; they shout "BIG DEAL, what's in it for me?"
- **Few-shot (EN):** Printer: "Separate ink tanks → **you benefit by** replacing only the color that runs dry, so you stop overpaying for full cartridges." Negative: "Features individual ink tanks and a 500-sheet tray." (features, no WIIFM).
- **Few-shot (RU):** CRM: «Автосохранение переписки → **вы получаете** полную историю клиента под рукой, и ни один менеджер не сольёт сделку из-за забытой договорённости». Плохо: «В системе есть автосохранение переписки и 500 полей карточки» — перечень фич без выгоды.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's stage "Feature-Benefit Exercise" — every time the seller names a feature, the "prospect" throws up their hands and yells "BIG DEAL! What's in it for me?", forcing the seller to reframe as "You benefit by…"; the Rolls-Royce feature/benefit table (hand-selected leather → comfort in all climates) is his worked example.

### [CASH:22] Benefit-loaded headline  (Secret #3)
- **Pattern:** ~60% of readers read the headline and nothing else, and people are hit by hundreds to thousands of ads daily — so put your **single biggest benefit in the headline, always, no exceptions** (Ogilvy: a weak headline wastes 90% of the spend). The headline is the "ticket on the meat" — it must both promise the benefit and *select the audience*.
- **Skeleton:** Headline = biggest benefit + audience selector (+ specificity/proof where possible). Length rule: rule #1 = always the biggest benefit; rule #2 = between two equally strong headlines, the shorter wins (people grasp ~5–6 words at a glance; readership drops sharply past ~12 words) — but a well-crafted *long* headline beats a weak short one. Avoid vague "distinction/brighten your life" claims that create no mental movie and select no one.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email (subject line) | ad.
- **Applies / fails when:** Universal. Fails with clever-but-empty headlines (no benefit, no audience), and with amorphous adjectives that give the reader nothing to grasp.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Food Servers: New Workshop Shows How to Boost Your Tips 512% — or Your Money Back." Also the audience-selecting minimalist "Need Lamps?" Negative: "We'll Help You Brighten Up Your Life and Save Big!" (could be paint, windows, or antidepressants — selects no one).
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Бухгалтеры: закрывайте квартал за 2 дня вместо двух недель — или вернём деньги». Аудитория-селектор в минимализме: «Нужен сайт за неделю?». Плохо: «Сделаем вашу жизнь ярче и сэкономим ваши деньги!» — непонятно, кому и о чём.
- **Book ref:** Whitman rewrites weak headlines throughout — "Louise Taylor Designs Homes of Distinction" becomes "Award-Winning Designer Turns Your House Into a Gorgeous Model Home for Less Than You Dreamed" — and cites the Rudolph study of 2,500 Saturday Evening Post ads showing readership falls as headlines lengthen (steepest past 12 words).

### [CASH:23] Headline starter templates  (Secret #5: 22 headline starters)
- **Pattern:** A headline must do two things — grab attention AND motivate reading on. Caples' four qualities of a strong headline: **self-interest, news, curiosity, a quick/easy way.** These 22 tested opener words drop the biggest benefit ([CASH:22]) into a proven frame; swap in your own wording. Warn: curiosity headlines must still select the *right* eyeballs (a "SEX!" headline on a patio-furniture ad wastes the reader).
- **Skeleton (the 22 starters):** FREE… · NEW… · AT LAST… · THIS [invention/method]… · ANNOUNCING… · WARNING!… · JUST RELEASED… · NOW you can… · HERE'S how… · THESE [people/things]… · WHICH OF these…? · FINALLY… · LOOK!… · PRESENTING… · INTRODUCING… · HOW TO [benefit] in [time]… · AMAZING… · DO YOU [problem]? · WOULD YOU [trade/like]? · CAN YOU [be sure/do]? · IF YOU [have problem], [good news]… · STARTING TODAY you can…
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email subject | ad.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works as a fast idea-generator and A/B fodder. Fails when the starter is bolted onto a non-benefit ("NEW! Our company turns 10") or when curiosity/clever framing pulls the wrong audience.
- **Few-shot (EN):** HOW-TO: "How to Cut Your AWS Bill 40% in One Afternoon — Without Migrating a Thing." WARNING: "Warning: Your 'Free' Analytics Tool May Be Selling Your Users' Data." Negative: "New! Amazing! The Best Solution Ever!" (starters with no benefit and no audience).
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Наконец-то: CRM, которую менеджеры не саботируют на второй неделе». «Внимание, селлеры: как поднять выкуп на Wildberries на 18% без снижения цены». Плохо: «Новинка! Уникально! Лучшее предложение!» — стартеры без выгоды и без адресата.
- **Book ref:** Whitman lists all 22 with worked examples (e.g. "Here's How a 95-Pound Granny Made a 275-Pound Psychopathic Killer Cry Like a Baby") and frames them under Caples' four headline qualities, stressing you want the *right* eyeballs, not merely many.

### [CASH:24] Body-copy lead hooks  (Secrets #6, #25, #13)
- **Pattern:** After the headline hooks them, the *first sentence* must keep the momentum without jarring them out of the reading trance. Two toolkits: **12 ways to open body copy** and **physical "grabbers."** Plus the **question / open-loop** device (Secret #13): a question installs an "open loop" the brain wants to close, so it keeps reading.
- **Skeleton — 12 opening moves (from one headline):** (1) continue the headline's thought; (2) ask a question; (3) quote a respected authority; (4) give a free taste (a usable tip); (5) challenge them to prove it works; (6) start with a story of skepticism; (7) tell what others are saying (bandwagon/testimonial); (8) play reporter (dateline news style); (9) get personal with *you, you, you*; (10) tell a dramatic story; (11) give super-detailed specs; (12) lure with a very short first sentence ("Don't you hate it?"). **Grabbers (direct mail/physical):** staple a real object to the top — a $1 bill ("Why? To make a point. I'll show you how to save 100 of these…"), a bad wedding photo (for a photographer), sand (beach realtor), an NSF check (collections). Curiosity + mild reciprocity forces the read.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL | direct mail — the transition from hook to body.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works to sustain attention right after the headline. Fails when the lead restarts with corporate throat-clearing ("For 23 years we have proudly…") and loses the momentum the headline built.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Open-loop + short sentence: "There's one number on your dashboard that quietly decides whether you're profitable. Most founders never look at it. Here it is —" Negative: "Founded in 2019, our company has grown to serve clients across many industries." (kills the trance).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Драматичная история + вопрос: «В 23:40 упал прод. К утру мы потеряли 400 000 ₽ и трёх клиентов. Знаешь, что нас спасло бы за 9 минут?» Плохо: «Наша компания основана в 2019 году и специализируется на широком спектре услуг» — обнуляет импульс заголовка.
- **Book ref:** Whitman spins one headline ("Psychologist's Study Reveals Speaking Patterns That Put Rude Salespeople in Their Place") into all 12 openers, and shows the "$1 bill grabber" letter template ("As you can see, I've attached a crisp one-dollar bill… Why? To make a point.") reused across carpet, day-spa, and network-marketing pitches.

### [CASH:25] Extreme specificity  (Secret #9)
- **Pattern:** Specific claims out-believe and out-sell generic ones. Exact numbers, named ingredients, and described processes create mental movies, signal quality, and (via length-implies-strength [CASH:19]) make you look more complete and successful — *even if competitors do the exact same thing but never say it*. "The one who says it, wins."
- **Skeleton:** Replace every vague adjective with a concrete detail. "Better ingredients" → "buffalo-milk mozzarella placed in chunks (shredded releases moisture), San Marzano tomatoes, coal-fired oven imported from Italy." Quantify inventory/results ("343 kinds of fasteners, 354,000 bolts in daily stock"). Educate the prospect on your process — the story they didn't know.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | long-form email | ad body — anywhere you describe the product.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works when the specifics are true and relevant. Fails/backfires if fabricated (a savvy buyer checks) or if specificity buries the benefit under trivia — keep it tied to why it matters to them.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Our onboarding takes 11 minutes: 4 to import, 3 to map fields, 4 to send your first campaign — measured across 1,200 new accounts last quarter." Negative: "Fast, easy setup with great support." (generic, forgettable, unbelievable).
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Собираем кухню за 6 дней: замер в день обращения, распил на станке с точностью 0,1 мм, 5 лет гарантии на фурнитуру Blum.» Плохо: «Качественные кухни на заказ, быстро и недорого» — обобщённо, не создаёт картинки, не убеждает.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's Handyman Jack's ad ("343 kinds of fasteners, 28 types of nails… 354,000 bolts") beats the generic Paulson's hardware ad; his pizza-shop cold-call experiment shows the shops that gave concrete details (San Marzano tomatoes, coal oven, four generations) would win the group over the ones that mumbled "we use better ingredients."

### [CASH:26] Scarcity & urgency  (Secret #4)
- **Pattern:** People resist changing their current state (inertia), and "later" never comes — so the ad must make them act *when the offer is in front of them*. Scarcity + a deadline supplies the fear-of-loss push. The *absence* of a deadline silently says "always available → no need to buy now." Executes CLARCCS scarcity ([CASH:12]) and battles inertia ([CASH:32]).
- **Skeleton:** Attach either a **hard deadline** (specific date/time: "Offer Expires May 15", "Good Only Before 4 p.m.") or a **soft deadline** (limited quantity/exclusivity: "Supplies Strictly Limited", "First 50 Callers", "No Rain Checks"). Scarcity beats a plain deadline because it adds *exclusivity* on top of urgency. Never run a persuasive offer without one.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email — near the CTA especially.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works when the constraint is real/credible. Fails and corrodes trust when it's a perpetual fake ("ends tonight!" every night) — hollow scarcity trains buyers to ignore you.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Founding-member pricing locks at $29/mo — but only for the first 100 sign-ups, then it's $49 forever." Negative: a countdown timer that silently resets every visit (exposed = credibility gone).
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Цена для первых 100 участников — 4 900 ₽, дальше 8 900 ₽ навсегда. Осталось 23 места.» Плохо: баннер «только сегодня», который висит «только сегодня» третий месяц — читатель перестаёт верить любым дедлайнам.
- **Book ref:** Whitman contrasts the deadly salesperson ("take your time, it'll be available later") with a list of deadline phrases, arguing that if every salesperson removed urgency it would "spell economic collapse" — the deadline, hard or soft, is what converts interest into action-now.

### [CASH:27] Social-proof engineering  (Secret #15)
- **Pattern:** People believe testimonials, full stop — across every market. Social proof is the credibility layer that makes benefit claims believable; volume amplifies it (ties to length-implies-strength [CASH:19] and bandwagon [CASH:06]). The reason more businesses don't use it is simply that they don't *ask*.
- **Skeleton (harvest + deploy):** Ask customers directly ("we want to make you famous"), prompt with specific questions (results? vs. alternatives? would you recommend? would you buy again?), get a signed release (consideration = free copies or $1), and pay return postage. Deploy: name + city + photo when possible (more specific = more believable), and stack *many* — dozens of testimonials/photos beat one. Use quantified/result-based quotes over vague praise.
- **Applies to:** all — landing, VSL, email, sales pages especially.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works when testimonials are real, specific, and attributed. Fails/illegal if fabricated; weak when anonymous or generic ("great service!"). Best proof names a concrete result the prospect wants.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "'Cut our churn from 7% to 2.9% in one quarter.' — María González, Head of Growth, Acme (photo)" ×30 on the page. Negative: "Our customers love us!" with no names, numbers, or faces.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «"За 2 месяца выручка кофейни выросла на 34%, очередь по утрам теперь до двери." — Игорь Смирнов, владелец, Казань (фото)» — и таких 25 штук стеной. Плохо: «Нас все хвалят, клиенты довольны» — без имён, цифр и лиц не работает.
- **Book ref:** Whitman gives the exact testimonial-request letter and legal-release wording (the "$1 as consideration" trick), noting testimonials have sold since Pond's used the first in 1926, and that the only reason businesses skip them is laziness about asking.

### [CASH:28] Mental movies + PVAs  (Secrets #17, #18)
- **Pattern:** Facts alone don't sell — the buyer must *demonstrate the product inside their head* first ("the first use is in the mind"). Do this by (a) **PVAs — powerful visual adjectives** that paint bright, high-impact pictures, and (b) engineering **internal representations (IRs)** across the five senses **V-A-K-O-G** (Visual, Auditory, Kinesthetic, Olfactory, Gustatory). Sharpen a fuzzy mental image into a loud, colorful, sensory experience and you occupy more of the brain → more persuasion → action. Directly executes examples-over-stats [CASH:14] and vicarious LF8 fulfilment [CASH:01].
- **Skeleton:** Upgrade flat lines with PVAs: "juicy red apples" → "mouth-watering, sugar-sweet, hand-picked apples." Then tour the reader through *using* the product, layering V-A-K-O-G ("sparkles like a Caribbean lagoon [V], chuga-chuga hum [A], relax and sip a piña colada [K], smells fresher [O]"). Coin a sticky mental hook ("The Orange-Juicy Way to Wash"). Use as many sense-modes as the product allows (a printer can't do taste, but can do the feel of cottony paper).
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email | long-form — needs room to build the movie.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works for almost anything; the more sensory the product (food, travel, comfort), the bigger the lift. Fails when copy stays at dry fact-level ("$250, 2-year warranty, effective") — facts create few IRs and don't prime the brain to buy. Don't overreach into senses the product can't credibly evoke.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Coffee subscription: "The first pour hits your nose before it hits the cup — dark cocoa, a little orange peel — and that first sip is thick enough to chew." Negative: "Freshly roasted specialty coffee, shipped monthly." (accurate, zero mental movie).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Баня под ключ: «Заходишь — кедровый пар обволакивает плечи, каменка потрескивает, ковш воды на камни — и по коже прокатывается та самая волна жара, ради которой всё и затевалось». Плохо: «Строим бани из бруса, парилка, комната отдыха, гарантия» — факты есть, кино в голове нет.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's "Squidly Diddly" pool-cleaner letter sells the identical $250 product the dry spec version couldn't — by touring the reader through the experience with all five VAKOG modes; his "bath soap" argument with George insists that describing "sun-drenched orange fields" and "Sweet Orange-Cream Bubbles" is what separates selling from merely listing.

### [CASH:29] Unique Selling Proposition  (Secret #20)
- **Pattern:** When buyers can't tell you from competitors, they have no reason to *prefer* you (you become interchangeable salt/sugar). A USP gives them one. Two moves: (a) *say* the differentiating things competitors leave unsaid (proactively toot your horn), and (b) craft a "specifically unique" positioning/hook nobody else in your category is using.
- **Skeleton:** Don't be the generic version — reposition: "not just a hardware store → The Hardware Superstore"; "not just a printer → The Business Image Maker." For offers, make the hook *specifically unique*: "19-Year-Old College Kid Discovers Clever Way to Get People to PayPal You Money" beats "Work From Home and Turn Email Into Profits." Rule: if the USP isn't yet true, *make* it true (hire/dress/systematize to back the claim) — positioning must be congruent with reality or you educate buyers away.
- **Applies to:** all — brand line, headline, offer framing.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works when the claimed difference is real and specific. Fails when it's a hollow tagline the product can't back (word spreads), or when the "difference" is generic ("quality service").
- **Few-shot (EN):** Not "freelance copywriter" → "The Only Copywriter in Austin Who Guarantees a 2× Response Lift or Rewrites Free." Negative: "Professional copywriting services, competitive rates." (interchangeable, no reason to prefer).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Не «студия ремонта» → «Ремонт с фиксированной ценой в договоре: ни рубля сверх сметы — или доплачиваем сами». Плохо: «Качественный ремонт квартир под ключ, доступные цены» — как у всех, предпочесть не за что.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's Nifty Fifty's diner survives where a dance studio, gym, and video store failed on the same spot — because it proactively tells you the beef is ground fresh daily, fries hand-cut, onion rings hand-battered (visible through the kitchen window); and his printer client rebrands from "printer" to "The Business Image Maker," backing it with free reports and clinics.

### [CASH:30] Authority positioning  (Secret #22)
- **Pattern:** Authority figures are believed with little scrutiny (peripheral route + bandwagon + length-implies-strength stack, [CASH:12]/[CASH:19]). You can *adopt* the authority position in your field and wear that coat of influence — assuming you genuinely know the subject.
- **Skeleton:** (1) Regard your knowledge as valuable. (2) Publish it in every form: a professional headshot on *all* materials; 3–10-page free reports/PDFs on what you already know ("How to Get Wedding Invitations Printed Without Getting Ripped Off"); an e-zine/blog; editorial-style Q&A "columns" you write yourself (you make the questions and answers) with your headshot and contact at the bottom; seminars, workshops, a book, radio/TV. Each report ends with a coupon so a primed reader converts. Repetition of face + name builds fame.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | long-form | content marketing — trust-building, lead magnets.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works when you can genuinely deliver expertise and are willing to publish. Fails if you claim authority with no substance (exposed on contact) or never distribute the content.
- **Few-shot (EN):** A bookkeeper publishes "The 7 Deductions Freelancers Miss (That Cost Them $3,400/yr)" as a free PDF with headshot + a consult coupon; runs a monthly "Ask the Bookkeeper" column locally. Negative: calling yourself "the #1 expert" with zero published proof.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Нутрициолог выпускает бесплатный гайд «11 анализов, которые терапевт вам не назначит» с фото и промокодом на консультацию; ведёт рубрику «Спросите нутрициолога». Плохо: «Я — эксперт №1 по питанию» без единой публикации за спиной.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's step-by-step for a printer — headshot on everything, self-published "Special Report" ("Printing Expert Reveals How to Make Your Resume 325% More Effective"), free library clinics, self-written newspaper Q&A columns — turns "just another printer" into the local go-to expert while competitors do nothing.

### [CASH:31] Guarantees / risk reversal  (Secret #33)
- **Pattern:** Every buyer's mind is a battleground between *skepticism* and *desire to believe*; a strong guarantee tips the scale toward belief by absorbing the risk. It reduces pre-purchase stress (which rises with price), and — counterintuitively — *longer* guarantees produce *fewer* returns than short ones, because short deadlines create a "beat-the-clock" mindset while long ones breed genuine confidence.
- **Skeleton:** Offer the **longest, strongest guarantee in your category** (1 year, lifetime — not 30 days). Make it prominent, not buried: certificate border, your signature, repeated across ad/site/brochure. Weaponize it against competitors: "Why do our competitors guarantee theirs for only 90 days? Do they know something they're not telling you?" Consider over-guaranteeing (double-your-money-back) when trust is the barrier.
- **Applies to:** all — landing, VSL, checkout, especially higher-priced offers.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works whenever fear-of-loss blocks the sale. The rare failure is a product with a genuinely high abuse rate on a consumable, but even there abuse is usually tiny; the bigger risk is a *weak/hidden* guarantee that signals your own doubt.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Use it for a full year. If your reply rate doesn't beat your current control, email us and we refund every cent — keep the templates." Negative: "30-day returns, restocking fee applies, see terms." (signals doubt, creates deadline pressure).
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Пользуйся год. Если выручка не вырастет — вернём всю оплату до копейки, а материалы оставь себе.» Плохо: «Возврат в течение 14 дней, товар должен быть в упаковке, подробности в оферте» — сигналит неуверенность и включает "успей вернуть".
- **Book ref:** Whitman cites Hormel's Depression-era "Double Your Money Back" vegetable-soup ad — execs feared mass abuse, but only 12 people claimed it and the guarantee drove the sale; he prescribes flaunting the longest guarantee in your industry to shift the skepticism-vs-belief scale.

### [CASH:32] CTA & inertia-breaking  (Secret #19)
- **Pattern:** An ad that only *informs* is a salesperson who can't close — fire it. People are lazy and hypersensitive to any hassle ("I'd rather not get involved"), so getting action takes two steps: **(1) make it easy to act, then (2) explicitly ask for the action.** "Grease the slide."
- **Skeleton:** *Make it easy:* pre-fill the order form (name/address), postage-paid reply, toll-free/one-click ordering, every payment method (cards, PayPal, C.O.D., plans), fast + multiple shipping options, a strong guarantee ([CASH:31]) to quell fear of loss, a payment plan ("just $14.99×3") to dull spending pain, and step-by-step "how to order" with address/hours/directions. Say "It's Easy to ___" in the exact variant that fits ("It's Easy to Get a Free Estimate"). *Ask:* give one clear, direct instruction to act now, paired with a deadline ([CASH:26]).
- **Applies to:** all — the close of every ad, landing, email.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works whenever there's friction to remove. Fails when copy builds desire then trails off without a specific ask, or when the buying process has hidden steps/effort the reader anticipates and avoids.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Click below, we've pre-filled everything — confirm your email and you're in. Pay monthly or annually, cancel in one click, 60-day money-back. Founding price ends Friday." Negative: "Learn more about our solutions." (informs, no easy path, no ask).
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Нажми кнопку — данные уже подставлены, подтверди почту, и готово. Оплата картой, СБП или частями; отмена в один клик; возврат 30 дней. Цена для первых 100 — до пятницы.» Плохо: «Узнайте больше о наших решениях» — информирует, но не ведёт к действию и ничего не просит.
- **Book ref:** Whitman opens his seminar impersonating the deadly salesman ("no need to rush, the product will be here later"), then argues advertising is "selling in print" — an ad that doesn't move people to act is as worthless as a rep who can't close; the fix is removing every roadblock and directly asking.

### [CASH:33] Advertorial camouflage — make it not look like an ad  (Secrets #14, #23, #29)
- **Pattern:** "The less an advertisement looks like an advertisement… the more readers stop, look, and read" (Ogilvy). People skip things that announce themselves as solicitations. Disguise the sell inside a format the reader already trusts: a personal letter, a survey, or a news story. Editorial-style ads reportedly lift readership 50%+ (Ogilvy), 80% (Caples), even 5–6× (Benson).
- **Skeleton — three camouflages:** (1) **Granny Rule (personal mail):** make direct mail look like a letter from a person, not a corporation — plain envelope, real stamp, handwritten-style address (no mailing labels), warm conversational tone, personal teaser ("Mirko: Please read before 9:30 tonight"), a "Personal Trial Certificate" instead of an "Order Form," a reply envelope. (2) **Survey-in-disguise:** send a genuine 5–6-question survey, then at the bottom make an offer available "only to survey respondents" as a thank-you — you get feedback *and* sales, and the postage-paid return envelope makes ordering frictionless. (3) **Editorial energizer:** write the ad as a news article — same typeface, column width, leading, and a reporter's headline ("Local Hypnotist Announces New Way to Quit Smoking in 48 Hours"); work in quotes; keep the tone objective (never hypey or you break the spell); read the publication's real stories first to match voice; offer a free report for response.
- **Applies to:** direct mail | email | native/advertorial placements | landing pages styled as articles.
- **Applies / fails when:** Works to win attention and trust. Fails/backfires if the disguise is deceptive about a material fact, if the "news" tone slips into obvious hype, or where disclosure rules require an "Advertisement" label — keep it honest, just un-ad-like.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Editorial: "New Data: Remote Teams Using [Tool] Ship 22% Faster, Study Finds" written in plain reportorial style with a customer quote and a "download the full report" CTA. Negative: the same page screaming "BUY NOW!! LIMITED OFFER!!" in ad-banner styling — instantly skipped.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Editorial: «Исследование: пекарни, перешедшие на предзаказ через бота, теряют на 30% меньше выручки в непроданной выпечке» — статейным тоном, с цитатой владельца и ссылкой на разбор. Плохо: та же страница в виде кричащего баннера «КУПИ СЕЙЧАС! АКЦИЯ!» — читатель пролистывает не глядя.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's "Granny Rule" prescribes making mail look hand-made and personal; his auto-detailer "survey" letter attaches a respondents-only half-price offer; and his hypnotist "editorial energizer" is set to mimic the newspaper's own articles — all three hide the sell inside a trusted format.

### [CASH:34] Price & value framing  (Secret #38 + value/buying hot-lists)
- **Pattern:** *How* you present price changes willingness to pay independent of the number itself. Two regimes: **odd/fractional pricing** signals value (buyer mentally truncates the last digits and reads "lowest possible price"), while **prestige/round pricing** signals quality. Beyond the digit, value is a *frame* you construct with the 9 value-conveyors and lower friction with the 13 buying-easers.
- **Skeleton:** **Digit choice:** for value/discount, end in **9** (best), or 79/88/98 (also convey value) — *not* 95, 49, 50, 90; ~60% of retail prices end in 9. For premium/quality, use round whole numbers ($1,000 > $999.95); a $50 dress is perceived as higher quality than the same dress at $49.99. **9 ways to convey value:** scream "Sale!"; give a coupon; diminish ("less than a coffee a day"); explain the low price ("we over-ordered"); amortize ("just $1.25/day"); state worth vs. cost; say what others paid; add scarcity/deadline; use psychological pricing. **13 buying-easers:** full address/phone/directions, "It's Easy to Order," accept every channel (phone/mail/online/fax) and every payment (cards/checks), toll-free number, long strong guarantee, and **installments for anything >$15 ("3 easy payments of $10.99" → +15% response)**.
- **Applies to:** all — pricing tables, checkout, offers.
- **Applies / fails when:** Value framing fits mass/discount positioning; prestige rounding fits luxury — mixing them (a "$9.99" luxury watch) undercuts the brand. Amortize/diminish only when the math is honest.
- **Few-shot (EN):** SaaS: "$49/mo — or just $1.63 a day, less than your coffee. Pay yearly and it's $470 (2 months free)." Luxury coaching: "$12,000. Twelve seats." Negative: pricing a premium mastermind at "$11,997" (fractional digits read cheap, clashing with the positioning).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Онлайн-сервис: «990 ₽/мес — это 33 ₽ в день, дешевле проезда. При оплате за год — 9 900 ₽, два месяца в подарок.» Премиум-наставничество: «300 000 ₽. Восемь мест.» Плохо: премиум-программа за «299 990 ₽» — дробные цифры читаются как дешёвка и спорят с позиционированием.
- **Book ref:** Whitman cites Schindler & Kibarian's catalog test (99-ending prices +8% sales over 00) and the Rutgers dress study ($50 perceived higher-quality than $49.99), and lists the 9 value-conveyors and 13 buying-easers (installments on >$15 items lift response ~15%).

### [CASH:35] The Cleverectomy — clarity over cleverness  (Secret #41)
- **Pattern:** Advertising is selling, not entertainment. "Clever" ≠ "effective." A cute pun/twist headline that hides what you sell loses the ~60% who read only the headline — including real prospects who simply couldn't tell it was for them. Being creative for creativity's sake is a costly misunderstanding of the job.
- **Copy move:** Cut anything that makes the reader *decode* instead of *understand*. Prefer the plain, benefit-loaded, specific, offer-carrying headline over the witty one. Interesting ≠ clever: copy should be interesting, but it earns attention with benefits, facts, an offer, and reassurance — not wordplay. When tempted by a clever line, ask "what happens to the prospect who reads only this and doesn't 'get it'?" — you lost them.
- **Applies to:** all — the governing discipline over every other pattern; the wording-side twin of Message Organization [CASH:13].
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to direct-response selling. The rare exception is pure brand/awareness work with a different goal — but even there, clarity rarely hurts. Fails whenever the joke costs comprehension.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Clear + selling: "Custom Website Built by a Marketing Pro in 24 Hours for $199." Clever-but-dead: "Don't Get Caught in a Sticky Web" (doesn't say what's sold — 60% gone). Negative pattern: any headline that reads like a riddle.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Ясно и продающе: «Сайт под ключ от маркетолога за 24 часа — 199 $». Красиво, но мимо: «Не запутайтесь в сетях интернета» (непонятно, что продают — 60% ушли). Плохой паттерн: заголовок-ребус, который надо разгадывать.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's verbatim-style debate with a web-designer client who wants "Don't Get Caught in a Sticky Web" over the plain "Powerful Web Pages in 24 Hours by a Marketing Expert for $199" — and Rosser Reeves' rebuke ("do you want fine writing, or the sales curve to stop moving down?") drive the point: it's not clever to be clever.

### [CASH:36] Visual attention & media engineering  (Secrets #7,8,10,11,12,16,21,24,34,35,37,39,40)
- **Pattern:** Layout, type, imagery, color, size, and placement decide whether copy is *seen and read* before a single word persuades. These are tested, mostly counterintuitive rules — apply them as defaults, override only with a reason. Operational table below (each row cites its secret).

| Lever | Rule | Secret |
|-------|------|--------|
| **Shape** | Break the square/rectangle sea — a circular-bordered ad stands out on a crowded page | #7 |
| **Reverse type** | Don't print white-on-dark for body/small type (slows reading ~4→6 wps, less legible); OK only for large, few-word headlines | #8 |
| **Layout (Ogilvy)** | Big photo top ⅔ → headline directly *under* photo → drop-cap body → logo lower-right. Eye hits picture then drops. "Reverse Ogilvy" flips the ratio | #10 |
| **Captions** | Up to 2× more people read captions than body — never run an image without a selling caption under it | #10 |
| **Drop cap** | Starting body copy with a drop-initial lifts readership ~13% | #10 |
| **Typeface (print)** | Serif body copy reads far easier than sans-serif; match the font's personality to the product | #11 |
| **Typeface (screen)** | Arial ≥12pt, Verdana for smaller (rarely <10pt), Georgia for a formal look, 14pt for older readers | #11 |
| **Case** | Set headlines in Initial Caps, not ALL CAPS (all-caps slows reading ~12–19%; eye reads by word outline) | #11 |
| **Pro design** | Don't design your own materials — image wins/loses sales; hire a designer (or a design student on a budget) | #12 |
| **Face (Guillotine)** | A head/face looking at the reader is the top eyeball-grabber; adds warmth, trust, and fame through repetition. Pair with a quote headline | #16 |
| **Half-page island** | Buy a "half-page island" to visually dominate a full page (only news beside it) for less than a full page — length-implies-strength halo | #21 |
| **Pictures** | Visuals lift "Noted" 30–55% and "Read Most" up to 70%; photos beat illustrations; show the product (+13%), and product *in use* (+13% more) with real context | #24 |
| **Best photo subjects** | Babies, mothers+babies, animals, groups, sports, celebrities, food — they tap the Life-Force 8 | #24 |
| **Size** | Attention ≈ √area: to *double* attention you must *quadruple* size — bigger ads pay off less than proportionally | #34 |
| **Inside position** | Front/middle/back and left/right page barely matter — the ad's offer & execution decide | #35 |
| **Premium positions** | Cover slots do pay: inside-front +29%, opposite-contents +25%, back cover +22%, inside-back +6% | #36 |
| **Color preference** | Ranked liking: blue > red > green > violet > orange > yellow; preference shifts bluer with age | #37 |
| **Paper/ink** | Best readability: black ink on white or yellow; worst: red on green. Color lifts readership more than size does (4-color vs B&W > 1- vs 2-page) | #37 |
| **Color perception** | Color alters perceived weight, taste, and category (Canada Dry red→green +25% sales — red read as "cola") — pick color deliberately | #39 |
| **White-wrap** | Isolate a smaller ad in white space (add ≤~60% area, ~20% is the sweet spot) to out-pull a bigger cluttered ad | #40 |
- **Applies to:** print ads | landing pages | emails | any visual layout. Screen-specific rows override print rows online.
- **Applies / fails when:** Defaults for direct-response. Some rows are print-era (island, position) — translate the *principle* (isolation, contrast, premium real estate) to digital. Fails when applied dogmatically against a tested result — always A/B when stakes are high.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Landing page hero: one big product-in-use photo, benefit headline *under* it, serif-or-clean body starting with a visual emphasis, generous white space around the CTA, blue/black palette. Negative: white body text on a dark hero with an ALL-CAPS clever headline and no product shown.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Первый экран лендинга: крупное фото продукта в использовании, под ним заголовок-выгода, воздух вокруг кнопки, сине-чёрная палитра, подпись под фото. Плохо: белый текст на тёмном фоне, заголовок КАПСОМ и без изображения продукта.
- **Book ref:** Whitman compiles decades of Starch/Ogilvy/Poffenberger research: the two-thirds Ogilvy layout with captioned photo, the reverse-type legibility experiments (Holmes, Starch, Paterson-Tinker), the √area size rule, and Canada Dry's red→green can lifting sales 25%.

### [CASH:37] Long-copy doctrine & multi-ad  (Secrets #26, #32)
- **Pattern:** A century of split-test data says **well-written long copy outsells short copy** — no qualifications, online included ("copy can never be too long, only too boring"). Long copy also *satisfies both buyer types*: Ms. Long gets every detail she needs; Mr. Short stops and buys the moment he's convinced. Short copy fails Ms. Long. Separately, **frequency/multi-placement** compounds attention (1+1=3): one insertion reaches only a fraction of readers.
- **Skeleton:** Write like a two-hour salesperson, not a ten-minute one — restate each benefit from multiple angles, hit both emotion (peripheral) and logic (central), stack benefits + pictures + testimonials + guarantee until the case is complete. Don't pad; keep every line interesting and relevant. For reach: run **multiple ads in one issue** or a sequence — Whitman's 10 multi-ad formations (e.g. three sequential right-hand pages, double-page spread, four-corner "checkerboard") outperform a lone insertion.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email | sales pages | media planning. (Length register set by involvement — see [CASH:11].)
- **Applies / fails when:** Long copy wins when there's genuinely more to say and it stays interesting. The only reason for short copy is "when there isn't much to say." Fails when length is boring padding, or when a truly trivial low-involvement product gets over-argued.
- **Few-shot (EN):** A $2k course page: full story + methodology + 30 testimonials + FAQ + guarantee + multiple CTAs down the page — buyers convinced at any depth can act. Negative: a $2k offer sold in three sentences and a "Buy" button (Ms. Long leaves unconvinced).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Лендинг курса за 120 000 ₽: полная история, методология, 30 отзывов, разбор возражений, гарантия, кнопки покупки по всей длине — убеждённый на любой глубине жмёт сразу. Плохо: тот же курс «в три предложения и кнопка» — вдумчивый покупатель уходит неубеждённым.
- **Book ref:** Whitman marshals Ogilvy, Hopkins, Caples, Schwab ("a salesman uses enough words to get your emotions and reasoning flowing") plus MarketingExperiments and Online-Learning.com (quadrupled homepage copy → +20% enrollments) to bury the long-vs-short debate, and lists Starch's 10 multi-ad formations for compounding frequency.

### [CASH:38] AIDA skeleton + killer-ad checklist  (Secret #14 + closing hot-lists)
- **Pattern:** The master structure for ordering a sales message: **Attention → Interest → Desire → Action**, with **Proof** inserted before Action in Whitman's expanded form (Attention → Interest → Desire → Proof → Action). Each *component* of a mailing (envelope/OSE, letter, brochure, order form) must first do job #1: get attention. Use the 46-point checklist as final QA.
- **Skeleton — the flow:** grab **Attention** (benefit headline [CASH:22] / grabber [CASH:24]); build **Interest** (lead hook, relevance); build **Desire** (benefits [CASH:21], mental movies [CASH:28], means-end value); give **Proof** (evidence [CASH:18], social proof [CASH:27], guarantee [CASH:31]); drive **Action** (easy + explicit ask + deadline [CASH:32]/[CASH:26]). For direct mail, tease the envelope non-specifically ("Personal letter for:"), open the letter with a 7-words-or-less attention grab, never a résumé of your company.
- **46-point checklist (condensed QA):** *Headline* — biggest benefit? emotional grabber? one of the 22 starters? bigger/bolder than body? makes an offer? initial caps? in quotes (+25% reading)? *First sentence* — flows from headline? straight into reader benefits (not company brag)? forces sentence two? "you" in the first few words? *Body* — reader-benefit focused? says why you over competitors? AIDA+proof order? one product at a time? selling subheads? PVAs? believable? emotional? extreme specificity? short words/sentences? serif (print)/sans (web)? explicit simple instruction to act? asks for the sale? deadline? bullets for many benefits? testimonials? name/phone big? logo? *Layout* — pro designer? big bold headline broken at sensible words? clear focus/eye-path? white space + white-wrap? indented paragraphs? few elements? relevant art? ≤2–3 typefaces? a face looking at the reader?
- **Applies to:** all — the assembly template and the pre-launch checklist.
- **Applies / fails when:** AIDA is the reliable default order for direct-response. Fails when a stage is skipped (desire with no proof → disbelief; proof with no ask → no action) or when the opening spends its attention budget on the company instead of the reader.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Sales page skeleton: benefit headline → problem-agitation lead → benefit stack + mental movie → testimonials + guarantee → pre-filled CTA with deadline. Negative: "About Us → Our History → Our Values → Contact" (no attention grab, no desire, no ask).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Скелет продающей страницы: заголовок-выгода → лид с проблемой и её обострением → блок выгод + «кино в голове» → отзывы + гарантия → CTA с подставленными данными и дедлайном. Плохо: «О нас → Наша история → Наши ценности → Контакты» — ни захвата внимания, ни желания, ни призыва.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's "cure for cancer" letter to the FDA shows each component grabbing attention first (a 7-word opener beats a career résumé), and his closing 46-point "Killer Ad" checklist plus the AIDA-plus-proof progression give the assemble-and-verify template.
