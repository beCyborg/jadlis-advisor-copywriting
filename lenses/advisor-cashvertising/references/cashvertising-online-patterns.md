# Cashvertising Online — Drew Eric Whitman  ·  prefix [CSHO]
> Applies offline direct-response psychology to social feeds, ads, email, and web pages: attention is rented on borrowed platforms, so copy must earn a share/click inside a dopamine-driven scroll. Use this lens when the deliverable is short-form social copy, a paid social ad, an email subject line, or a conversion-focused web page — not for long-form print/VSL (that is core Cashvertising territory).

## Principles

### [CSHO:01] Dopamine Slot-Machine Content  (ch 1–2)
- **Essence:** Social feeds are engineered as variable-reward machines (endless scroll, intermittent dopamine hits, FOMO, social validation, the Ovsiankina "must-finish" urge). Your ad competes inside that machine, so it must survive a distracted, dopamine-seeking scroll — attention is the first sale, before any product sale.
- **Copy move:** Design each unit against the Fogg Behavior Model (MAP = Motivation × Ability × Prompt). Supply Motivation with a Life-Force desire (not a feature); collapse Ability by stripping steps between click and conversion (one focused page, one field, one button); deliver an explicit Prompt (a literal instruction to act now). Never route a hot lead into a multi-page site or a long form — you dissipate the impulse the feed just handed you.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email
- **Applies / fails when:** Governs paid-social and organic feed content where the reader is mid-scroll and un-committed. Fails as a lens for high-consideration B2B where the "impulse close" is inappropriate — there Ability still matters but the single-step close does not.
- **Few-shot (EN):** (a) Ad → one lawn-mowing page → "Book your first cut for $29 — pick a date below" (one calendar field, pay inline). (b) Prompt made literal: "Tap *Get My Free Sample* — takes 8 seconds." Negative: ad → homepage listing 9 services + a 12-field quote form → the motivated click evaporates before conversion.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Реклама клинингового сервиса в Telegram Ads → одна посадочная «Уборка 2-комнатной за 2490 ₽ — выберите дату», один шаг оплаты. Прямой prompt: «Нажмите *Записаться* — 10 секунд». Антипример: клик ведёт на сайт-визитку с меню из 8 услуг и формой на 12 полей — импульс гаснет.
- **Book ref:** Whitman frames platforms as intentionally addictive (a former Facebook exec's "addictive as cigarettes" admission) and lists seven built-in hook mechanisms; the landscaper example shows collapsing Ability to catch the buyer at peak "I hate mowing this" interest.

### [CSHO:02] The Table Test — Instant Clarity  (ch 2)
- **Essence:** Imagine your ad lying on a table; a stranger glances at it in passing. Could a bystander then say what it sells and what the offer is? If the meaning does not transmit instantly, the ad fails — cleverness that must be decoded is a liability in a feed.
- **Copy move:** Run every headline/subject line through the table test: does it name the product, the offer, and a core Life-Force desire at a glance? Replace "clever" or season-announcing ("Spring is perfect for planting!") lines with a desire-loaded promise ("How to have the most beautiful yard on your street"). Pair one Life-Force 8 driver with one Secondary Want, then state the benefit, not the feature — and generate emotion, since purchases are made emotionally and justified with logic.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email
- **Applies / fails when:** Universal clarity gate for any headline/thumbnail/subject. The "instant" bar is highest on social where dwell time is ~1 second; on a landing page a curiosity-gap headline can defer full clarity slightly, but never to the point a passerby is confused about the category.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Pass: "Getting married? 8 photography mistakes that ruin wedding photos forever." Fail (table-test miss): "FIND. MEET. LOVE." — a bystander cannot tell it sells wedding photography. Rewrite a fire-extinguisher ad from "Fire can strike anytime!" to "This $29 extinguisher can save your family from a house fire" — feature → what people actually want.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Проходит: «Ремонт квартиры? 7 ошибок подрядчиков, из-за которых переделывают всё заново». Не проходит: «Создаём пространство мечты» — прохожий не поймёт, что продают. Переписать «Весна — время сажать!» → «Как сделать самый красивый газон на улице».
- **Book ref:** Whitman's seminar test — a passerby asked "what was that ad selling?" — exposes business-card ads that only announce a business exists; his rewrites insert one LF8 + one SW9 to make the promise land instantly.

### [CSHO:03] Inoculation Remarketing (PIR)  (ch 3)
- **Essence:** Before a prospect leaves to comparison-shop, plant the specific weaknesses they'll find in competitors, framed as consumer-protection advice. This installs a "self-talk critic" that pre-discredits rival copy — the buyer carries your judging criteria into every other tab. Based on inoculation theory: a small dose of the counter-argument builds resistance to it.
- **Copy move:** Mine competitors' 1–3-star reviews for the top real complaints. In your copy, teach those complaints as buyer-beware facts ("many use inferior glass that cracks"), then assert your product avoids them, then attack with the "Why don't they ___? / Why do they ___?" question frame. Reinforce with a comparison chart (heuristic shortcut) and solicit specific-issue testimonials by asking customers pointed questions ("what did you think of our lack of contracts?"). Optionally publish a consumer-advocate free report ("12 things to watch out for before you buy X"). Rule: every claim must be literally true.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL
- **Applies / fails when:** Best in considered purchases where buyers research competitors before deciding (physical goods on marketplaces, services, courses). Fails on pure impulse buys (no research phase to intercept) and backfires if the negatives are exaggerated or unverifiable — a false "beware" claim destroys the credibility the tactic depends on. Avoid naming competitors directly where it invites legal/defamation risk; attack the category practice, not the brand.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Selling a standing desk: "Before you compare, know this — most sub-$400 desks use single-motor lifts that wobble above 40 inches and are rated for only 5,000 cycles. Ours is dual-motor, rated 20,000 cycles. Ask any seller for their cycle rating — if they don't publish it, wonder why." Negative: "We're the best standing desk, buy now!" — no inoculation, so the buyer leaves with no criteria and forgets you.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Продаёте онлайн-курс по Python: «Прежде чем сравнивать — проверьте: у большинства курсов записанные в 2019 лекции и куратор отвечает раз в неделю. Спросите любую школу: когда обновляли модуль по async и за сколько часов отвечает ментор? Не отвечают прямо — вопрос закрыт». Плюс сравнительная таблица «мы / типичная школа». Антипример: «У нас лучший курс, записывайтесь» — критериев не дал, ушли и забыли.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's French-press example teaches borosilicate-glass and stainless-filter distinctions from rival reviews, then a karate-school example inoculates against belt-test fees, lock-in contracts, and dirty dojos — turning the seller into a consumer advocate.

### [CSHO:04] Swipe-the-Winners Modeling  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** Proven high-performers are visible teachers — the psychological elements that made an ad go viral are on the surface if you know what to look for. Reverse-engineer the mechanism (high-arousal emotion, novelty, demonstration, target-selection), not the surface words. On social, virality is bonus untargeted reach layered on top of an ad that must still sell.
- **Copy move:** Audit winning ads for the transferable levers and install them: (1) high-arousal emotion (awe, anger, fear, humor, surprise) in the headline — test how the line makes you *feel*, not think; (2) novelty framing ("new / unusual / genius") to trigger the dopamine-novelty response; (3) video *demonstration* (VAKOG proof — show it working) since seeing dissolves doubt; (4) a target-selecting opener ("Do you like pancakes?", "Attention dog lovers!") to pre-qualify clickers; (5) a human-spokesperson, first-person, pronoun-heavy voice over corporate tone; (6) an explicit tag/share prompt. Reaction-video = testimonial showing many first-uses; it answers the pre-purchase self-talk "will *I* like it?".
- **Applies to:** short-form | VSL
- **Applies / fails when:** Governs paid/organic social creative where sharing is one click and reach compounds. High-arousal and casual-voice tactics fit consumer/impulse categories; the intentional-misspelling and cartoonish tone backfire for finance, legal, medical, or premium brands that need gravitas. Virality without a selling core = views, not sales.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Target-select + emotion + demo: "Hate untangling cables every morning? Watch this $12 clip end it in 4 seconds" over a tight before/after clip, ending "Tag the messy-desk friend." Negative: "We offer premium cable-management solutions for the modern workspace" — corporate, emotionless, no demo, un-shareable.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Ненавидите, когда провода в клубке?» + короткое видео до/после + «Отметьте того, у кого вечный бардак на столе». Антипример: «Предлагаем премиальные решения для организации кабельного пространства» — корпоративно, без эмоции и демонстрации, репостить нечего.
- **Book ref:** Whitman dissects six Facebook winners (Squatty Potty, salad cutter, bed fan, Zungle glasses, KABOOST, pancake flipper), extracting shared engines: high-arousal emotion, novelty, video demonstration, and target-selecting first lines — while noting the bed-fan's vague copy left buyers unsure what it even sold.

### [CSHO:05] Peer-Recommendation Proof  (ch 5, Secret #1)
- **Essence:** Buying is fear-driven; buyers want someone to "jump off the cliff" first. Reviews, ratings, follower counts, expert/press mentions, and share counts let strangers go first so the prospect can follow safely — social proof is a "bandwagon of consumer security" that trades on Length-Equals-Strength (a large volume of proof reads as "there must be something to it").
- **Copy move:** Load proof onto the *main* selling page, never a separate testimonials tab. Use five channels — experts, buyers, influencers, press, and share/like counts. Prefer specific, mildly-worded reviews (with full name + city) over hype ("used 4 others, this beat them all" > "OMG life-changing") because ~93% distrust obviously-hyped reviews. Show follower/customer counts even pre-sale. Respond publicly to *every* review, positive and negative — a gracious reply to a complaint is itself an ad that signals you care. Manufacture press with a survey/stat-driven release. Cap it with the longest guarantee you can offer.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL
- **Applies / fails when:** Universal, strongest lever in the arsenal. Fails when proof is thin-and-hyped (triggers fake-review suspicion) or siloed on a page nobody visits. Overstated star claims ("100% 5-star!" on 6 reviews) can read as manipulation — keep it credible and specific.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "1,400+ verified buyers, 4.8★. 'I tried three other posture correctors — this is the only one I still wear at month six.' — Dana R., Austin TX." Negative: burying every review under a "Reviews" tab while the sales page runs pure claims — the anxious buyer leaves to find proof elsewhere and lands on a competitor's page.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Более 3 200 отзывов, 4.9★ на Ozon. "Брала три разных — этот единственный, которым реально пользуюсь каждый день." — Марина, Казань». Плюс ответ продавца на каждый отзыв, даже негативный. Антипример: отзывы спрятаны на отдельной вкладке, на продающей странице одни обещания — тревожный покупатель уходит искать пруфы к конкуренту.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's stalled-traffic-light metaphor (everyone waits for the first car to move) and the "Paralyzer" tear-gas kit with ~30 photocopied customer letters show how sheer volume of credible proof erodes the "I'll get ripped off" armor.

### [CSHO:06] Online Color Psychology  (ch 5, Secret #2)
- **Essence:** Color drives a large share of the sub-90-second first impression and carries semi-innate associations (blue = trust/security, red = urgency/speed/danger, black = high-tech/quality, yellow-orange = fun/cheap), but exact preference is personal and non-transferable — studies inform, they don't dictate.
- **Copy move:** Choose a base palette by the emotional job (blue for trust-heavy offers like finance/SaaS; red/orange for urgency and CTAs; black for premium/tech), then A/B test your control against one color variant — let the market arbitrate. Don't fight strong category conventions blindly (no pink men's cologne, no green teeth-whitener) but do test counterintuitive contrast for standout. Treat color as a testable variable, not a taste decision.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form
- **Applies / fails when:** Matters most for buttons, logos, and page palette where first-impression and click behavior are measurable. Fails as a deterministic rule — no color "makes" someone buy across audiences; without a split test you're guessing. Life-Force-8 desire always overrides color (people buy the life-saving product in any color).
- **Few-shot (EN):** Trust-heavy fintech landing: blue frame + orange CTA (contrast for the click), then split-test orange vs red button. Negative: "our brand color is teal, so every button is teal on teal" — the CTA disappears and no test ever ran.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Лендинг страхового сервиса: синий фон доверия + оранжевая кнопка (контраст под клик), затем сплит оранжевая vs красная. Антипример: «фирменный цвет мятный, поэтому кнопка мятная на мятном фоне» — CTA сливается, а тест так и не запустили.
- **Book ref:** Whitman cites the Hallock study's color-emotion map and Singh's finding that 62–90% of a snap product judgment rests on color, then undercuts determinism with Singh's own caveat that color perception is individual — landing on "test it."

### [CSHO:07] High-Response CTA Design  (ch 5, Secret #3)
- **Essence:** The CTA is the salesperson finally asking for the order; online, price/how-to-buy is hidden below scroll, so you must ask early, often, specifically, and in the buyer's own voice. First-person, personalized, benefit-loaded buttons with reduced surrounding clutter measurably outperform generic ones.
- **Copy move:** Place a Quick-Buy button right after the headline (satisfies hot buyers who already researched) AND repeat CTAs mid-page and at the end — never ask only once. Write in first person ("Start MY free trial" beat "YOUR" by ~90%) and personalize ("YES, Reid! I want fresh juices!"). Add a *second deck*: bold command line ("Click Now to Order") + a smaller benefit subhead ("Enjoy pure water for pennies"). Prefer button-styled CTAs, add a directional arrow, place your product image beside the button, strip nearby clutter, and rotate the CTA element periodically to fight banner-blindness. Personalized CTAs converted ~202% better across 330k tested.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | short-form
- **Applies / fails when:** Governs any page/email with a conversion action. The "ask early" tactic assumes headline+copy are strong enough to reach the CTA; a single-CTA email can beat multi-CTA when the goal is one focused action, so test count. Fails if the button hides price nowhere and the page never restates the offer.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Two-deck, first-person, arrow: "**▶ Start MY 14-Day Free Trial** — cancel anytime, keep the templates" repeated after the headline, mid-page, and at close. Negative: one grey "Submit" link buried at the very bottom of a long page — hot buyers never scroll to it.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Двухдечный CTA от первого лица: «**▶ Начать МОЙ бесплатный период на 14 дней** — отмена в любой момент, шаблоны остаются», повтор после заголовка, в середине и в конце. Антипример: одна серая ссылка «Отправить» в самом низу длинной страницы — горячий покупатель до неё не доскроллит.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's "Quick-Buy button after the headline" test lifted clicks-to-pricing ~47%; AWeber's button-vs-link saga shows novelty wears off, arguing for rotating the CTA element rather than fixing one forever.

### [CSHO:08] Influencer Outreach Psychology  (ch 5, Secret #4)
- **Essence:** With ad-blocking rising, borrowed audiences via influencers bypass the blockers — and nano/micro creators (1k–100k) convert far better per-follower than megas, who are drowning in pitches. The outreach email is itself a sales letter where the influencer is the prospect.
- **Copy move:** Start with nanos/micros, scale up as profit allows. Email subject line = first name only ("Hi Steve") to earn the open — never tease the offer in the subject. Keep the body short and 100% personal (quote their actual post), butter the ego, and ask for nothing but a reply — offer a free sample to trigger reciprocity, not a deal. One tiny ask: "hit Reply with your shipping address." Mail many simultaneously (numbers game), be persistent, stay humble-but-confident. Send Tue/Wed/Thu ~10am.
- **Applies to:** email | short-form
- **Applies / fails when:** For earned/seeded amplification, especially where ad-blocking erodes paid reach. Fails on irrelevant match (beauty influencer for a pliers brand) and on ego-heavy megas without deep pockets. A pushy "will you promote me?" first email kills the reciprocity play — the first email only opens a line.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Subject: "Hi Maya". Body: "Loved your reel on messy cable desks — the binder-clip hack made me laugh. I make MagClips (a magnetic version); can I mail you a free set? Just hit Reply with an address." Negative: Subject "PARTNERSHIP OPPORTUNITY — 20% commission!!" → filed as spam, never opened.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Тема: «Привет, Аня». Текст: «Обожаю твой рилс про провода на столе — лайфхак с зажимом огонь. Делаю магнитную версию MagClips, можно прислать бесплатный набор? Просто ответь адресом». Антипример: тема «ПРЕДЛОЖЕНИЕ О СОТРУДНИЧЕСТВЕ — 20% комиссии!!» → улетело в спам, не открыто.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's Godzilla-Pretzel outreach template asks only for a shipping address to send free samples — no review requested — deliberately triggering the Law of Reciprocity before any ask.

### [CSHO:09] Endowment Effect  (ch 5, Secret #5)
- **Essence:** People value a thing more once they feel they own it (owners demanded ~2× what buyers would pay for the same mug), because losing feels worse than gaining. Manufacture pre-purchase ownership and the prospect assigns higher value and perceives your price as lower.
- **Copy move:** Five ownership triggers — (1) free trials (let them live with it until returning it feels like a loss); (2) personalization (name-based accounts, "your dashboard", city references); (3) giveaways (a dated coupon/gift certificate they "hold" and re-experience until redeemed); (4) freemium (a free tier they invest effort into, then upsell once switching feels costly); (5) solicit their opinion (feedback deepens investment and yields testimonials). Design the free experience so quitting = a felt loss.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL
- **Applies / fails when:** Strong for SaaS, subscriptions, apps, and any product that can be sampled or personalized. Fails for pure one-shot commodity buys with no trial surface; a free tier too generous cannibalizes upgrades, so gate the premium value deliberately.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Build your free workspace in 2 minutes — name it, invite your team, import your first project." After hours of setup, the upgrade prompt lands on an owner, not a shopper. Negative: "14-day trial" that starts blank and demands a credit card up front — no ownership forms before the wall.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Соберите своё бесплатное пространство за 2 минуты — назовите, пригласите команду, загрузите первый проект». После часа настройки предложение апгрейда падает на «владельца», а не на «покупателя». Антипример: «пробный период 14 дней», который стартует с пустого экрана и требует карту сразу — чувство владения не успевает возникнуть.
- **Book ref:** Whitman recounts the Xerox "leave the machine and tell me what you think" trial that made the agency refuse to give it back, and his own portfolio-platform freemium creep that made upgrading "the next natural step."

### [CSHO:10] Image & Photo Psychology  (ch 5, Secret #7)
- **Essence:** Humans are wired to read faces for information from infancy, so the human face is the single highest-attention image, and posts with any image massively outperform text-only. But not all images are equal: a large JMR study of thousands of posts found the *type* of image predictably raises or lowers engagement.
- **Copy move:** Operational image rules — (1) always include an image, every post; (2) never hyperlink the image — show it inline so nothing must be clicked; (3) crank saturation/brightness (colorful images pop and share more); (4) include a human face (any face — model, average, odd — lifts likes ~39% / shares ~80% on text-image platforms); (5) prefer *professional*, informative photos over selfies and amateur shots (which read as private and don't get shared); (6) scrap screenshots; (7) enforce image-text fit — the picture must match the message or it creates confusion; (8) make banner ads deliberately obtrusive (bold color, animation) — retargeted banners work best with a price incentive. Text side: keep posts positive (negativity lifts shares but kills likes), informative, tied to high-emotion news, and packed with powerful visual adjectives.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing
- **Applies / fails when:** Governs any visual social post or ad. Face-lift finding is strong on text-image feeds (Twitter/X, Facebook) but washes out on Instagram where nearly every post already has a face. "Professional over amateur" is about *shareability of brand posts* — raw/authentic UGC can still out-convert in ad creative, so don't over-polish paid social.
- **Few-shot (EN):** A bright, professionally-lit photo of a smiling person holding the product, shown inline, caption tied to a trending story. Negative: a low-res screenshot of your dashboard, hyperlinked so users must click to see it, with a corporate caption — low engagement on every axis.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Яркое, профессионально снятое фото улыбающегося человека с продуктом в руках, встроенное прямо в пост, подпись цепляет актуальную новость. Антипример: тусклый скриншот интерфейса, спрятанный за ссылкой, с корпоративной подписью — низкий отклик по всем метрикам.
- **Book ref:** Whitman invokes Caples ("the human face gets the most attention") and the Visual-Cliff study, then codifies Li & Xie's JMR analysis (18k+ airline/SUV tweets, 2k Instagram posts) into ~13 always/never image-and-text rules.

### [CSHO:11] Frequency Illusion / Omnipresence  (ch 5, Secret #8)
- **Essence:** Once someone notices a brand, they start seeing it everywhere (Baader-Meinhof / frequency illusion). You can manufacture the *impression* of market dominance far cheaper than actual dominance by making your presence consistent and repeatedly re-encountered.
- **Copy move:** Four levers — (1) textual/graphic consistency: every ad looks like the next in a series (same layout, 2–3 fonts, same image style, one palette) so recognition snowballs; (2) front-loaded flighting: launch heavy, then taper — memory fragments survive ~3 weeks to 3 months, so intermittent bursts sustain recall on a budget; (3) pulsing: continuous base with planned frequency spikes; (4) retargeting: pixel- or list-based, the single highest-ROI channel (retargeted visitors far likelier to convert). Use these for awareness/top-of-mind, not for the immediate order — to get today's sale you still need a direct-response ad running today.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing
- **Applies / fails when:** Best for brand-recognition and re-engagement across a sustained campaign. Awareness ≠ conversion — flighting/pulsing keep you top-of-mind but don't ring the register alone; pairing with a live direct-response offer is required. Consistency fails if every post looks designed by a different team.
- **Few-shot (EN):** A month-one ad blitz in one template (same header position, two fonts, brand teal), then a lighter cadence, with a retargeting pixel re-showing the exact product to site visitors. Negative: five ads in five unrelated styles run once each — no recognition ever compounds.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Первый месяц — плотная серия объявлений в одном шаблоне (та же сетка, два шрифта, фирменный цвет), затем более редкий ритм плюс ретаргетинг-пиксель, догоняющий тем же товаром посетителей сайта. Антипример: пять объявлений в пяти разных стилях, каждое показано один раз — узнаваемость не накапливается.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's "old man Joe" suddenly seeing his new Nissan everywhere illustrates the illusion, then he maps flighting, six pulsing patterns, and retargeting stats onto engineering perceived ubiquity.

### [CSHO:12] Polarization Positioning  (ch 5, Secret #10)
- **Essence:** A controversial stance splits the audience into supporters and detractors — and the supporters bond harder to you because they've now emotionally self-identified with your cause. Being loved by a segment beats being ignored by everyone.
- **Copy move:** Rail against something your audience *already* dislikes in your industry (dishonest practices, rip-off norms) — you don't invent the grievance, you name it. Post a strong emotional statement that frames you as the consumer's advocate, which self-selects your tribe, then market to that aroused, aligned segment. Keep the target a *practice*, not an identifiable innocent, to cap the risk. Emotion + advocacy = high like/share.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing
- **Applies / fails when:** Powerful for challenger brands and personal brands that want a devoted core. Fails/backfires if you offend the actual buyers rather than the shared enemy, or in categories requiring neutral gravitas (some B2B, regulated finance/health). Overreach that alienates your own market is the failure mode.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Sick of 'artisan' bakeries that thaw frozen dough at 5am? We mill our own flour daily. Come taste real bread." Rallies bread purists against a real industry practice. Negative: attacking a named competitor personally or mocking your own potential customers ("if you buy supermarket bread you have no taste") — you insult the buyer.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Достали "крафтовые" пекарни, которые в 5 утра размораживают заводское тесто? Мы мелем муку сами каждый день. Приходите за настоящим хлебом». Сплотит ценителей против реальной практики. Антипример: личная атака на конкретного конкурента или насмешка над самими покупателями («берёте хлеб в супермаркете — значит, нет вкуса») — оскорбляете клиента.
- **Book ref:** Whitman cites Trump and Musk as polarization masters and gives pizza-shop, web-designer, and café example posts that attack an industry practice (frozen dough, template sites, overpriced lattes) while framing the seller as the customer's ally.

### [CSHO:13] Ethical Bribes / Incentives  (ch 5, Secret #11)
- **Essence:** Most people won't hand over contact info or attention for free — an incentive ("ethical bribe") lifts engagement, and, crucially, engaging *because* of the incentive still transfers attention and buying-intent to the brand ("value-exchange advertising"). Incentives also drive traffic and referrals.
- **Copy move:** Offer a value-exchange: give something wanted in return for attention/contact/action. A study found ~48% engaged because of the incentive yet still attended to the brand, and incentives lifted intent-to-buy-over-competitor ~32%. Choose the incentive to fit ticket size — sneak previews, how-to guides, free seminars, charity donations (help buyers rationalize), discount coupons, BOGO (tests beat "50% off"), free memberships, loyalty programs, free returns, gift wrapping, free consultations, event tickets, gift cards. Frame it as a gift, not a markdown.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | short-form
- **Applies / fails when:** For lead capture, first-purchase conversion, and referral. Reserve expensive incentives for high-ticket items; a bribe that dwarfs margin or attracts pure deal-seekers who never buy again is the failure mode. Works only when the incentive is genuinely wanted by the target.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Get our 12-page 'Choosing a Contractor' guide free — plus $50 off your first project when you book." Value-exchange for the email and a nudge to buy. Negative: "Sign up for our newsletter!" with no reason given — no exchange, few signups.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Заберите бесплатный гайд "Как выбрать подрядчика" на 12 страниц — плюс 3000 ₽ на первый заказ при бронировании». Обмен ценности за email и толчок к покупке. Антипример: «Подпишитесь на нашу рассылку!» без причины — обмена нет, подписок мало.
- **Book ref:** Whitman cites the SocialVibe/Infillion study of 30,000+ consumers ("value-exchange brand advertising") and lists 13 incentive types, noting tests show BOGO outperforms an equivalent "50% off."

### [CSHO:14] Illusory Truth & Repetition  (ch 5, Secret #16)
- **Essence:** Repeated claims feel truer regardless of actual truth (Illusory Truth Effect) because repetition raises *processing fluency* — the brain treats easy-to-process as likely-true. Anything that eases processing (repetition, a legible font, simpler words) also raises believability.
- **Copy move:** Repeat your core claim across exposures — the biggest believability jump comes on the *second* encounter, and positive attitude climbs up to ~10 exposures before wear-out. Boost fluency further: switch to an easy-to-read font, use simple short-sentence language (people even judged a task as faster when its instructions were in an easy font). Don't fear "over-running" your ad — you tire of it long before most of the audience has seen it even once; each repetition also raises perceived product quality.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to any claim you want believed and any campaign with repeated exposure. Ethical only with true claims (the effect works on falsehoods too — that's the caution). Wear-out past ~10 exposures flips attitude negative, so rotate creative. Fluency gains fail if you chase clever-but-hard-to-read fonts.
- **Few-shot (EN):** One benefit line ("cuts prep time in half") repeated in the headline, mid-copy, the CTA subhead, and every retargeting touch, set in clean Arial. Negative: a different fancy claim in an ornate font each exposure — nothing accrues believability and the font slows reading.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Одна выгода («готовка вдвое быстрее») повторяется в заголовке, в теле, в подписи под кнопкой и в каждом касании ретаргетинга, набрана чистым читаемым шрифтом. Антипример: каждое касание — новый вычурный claim декоративным шрифтом: доверие не накапливается, а шрифт тормозит чтение.
- **Book ref:** Whitman cites Hassan & Barber's repetition study (truth rating rose with exposures, biggest jump at the second) and the Arial-vs-hard-font finding where the same exercise was judged to take half as long when easier to read.

### [CSHO:15] Killer Slogan Formula  (ch 5, Secret #17)
- **Essence:** Memorable slogans aren't luck — a study of famous slogans isolated the qualities that drive liking and recall, and the dominant, most-transferable lever is brevity: the top-recalled slogans average ~4 words.
- **Copy move:** Keep the slogan to ~7 words max (billboard rule), ideally ~4. Build in research-backed likability devices used sparingly — a clear benefit or promise, rhythm, and easy poetic hooks (light rhyme/alliteration) — but never let cleverness obscure meaning. Don't rely on age of slogan, big budget, or gimmickry (those didn't predict liking); younger audiences like slogans more, so calibrate to your demographic. Pair a sticky slogan with repetition to ride the Illusory Truth Effect ([CSHO:14]).
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing
- **Applies / fails when:** For brand taglines, hooks, and repeated campaign lines. A long, clever-but-cryptic slogan fails both recall and the table test ([CSHO:02]); brevity without a benefit or hook is forgettable. Slogans build memory/affinity, not the direct sale.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Sleep cooler tonight." (3 words, benefit, sticky) beats "Innovative thermoregulation for your optimal nocturnal recovery experience." (long, cryptic, unmemorable).
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Спи прохладнее.» (2 слова, выгода, липко) бьёт «Инновационная терморегуляция для оптимального ночного восстановления» (длинно, туманно, не запоминается).
- **Book ref:** Whitman reports a two-phase slogan study (241 → 150 slogans, 595 respondents) and notes the ten most-liked/recalled averaged 3.9 words, with age of slogan, budget, gender, and income failing to predict liking.

### [CSHO:16] Self-Referencing Headlines  (ch 5, Secret #18)
- **Essence:** The headline is "the ad for your ad" (Ogilvy: 5× as many read the headline as the body). Turning a declarative headline into a *question* that includes "you/your" (a self-referencing cue) dramatically raises click-through by forcing personal relevance and a reflexive mental response.
- **Copy move:** Take your best declarative headline, convert it to a question, and insert "you/your." A question triggers a habitual answering reflex; the self-reference makes the message feel personally relevant vs. generic. Write all copy to one person, not a crowd — use "you," imagine a specific reader. Test the question-with-cue variant against your declarative control.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email
- **Applies / fails when:** For headlines, subject lines, thumbnails, ad hooks. Question headlines lifted readership in nearly all tested cases (mean ~+257% with self-reference on products). Weakest where the product is a pure commodity the buyer already intends to buy (one tested item showed no lift), and a question that's answerable "no" instantly can backfire — keep the implied answer a curiosity pull, not a dismissable yes/no.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Do You Know the 3 Mistakes That Kill Your Sleep?" beats declarative "3 Mistakes That Ruin Sleep." Negative: "Would You Like Our Newsletter?" — a question with an easy "no" and zero relevance.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Знаете 3 ошибки, которые крадут ваш сон?» бьёт утвердительное «3 ошибки, которые портят сон». Антипример: «Хотите нашу рассылку?» — вопрос с лёгким «нет» и нулевой релевантностью.
- **Book ref:** Whitman cites Lai & Farbrot's Twitter/FINN experiments where question headlines with self-referencing cues beat declarative controls by a mean of ~137%–257%, echoing Daniel Starch's 1914 note that the interrogatory form heightens interest.

### [CSHO:17] Online Credibility Signals  (ch 5, Secrets #19, #21, #22)
- **Essence:** Belief is what converts reading into buying, and online belief is built from cheap, fast signals — and, counterintuitively, site *design* is the single most-cited credibility cue (~46% of judgments), outweighing "substantive" factors for non-expert buyers. Experts (health/finance audiences) invert this and weigh substance.
- **Copy move:** Apply Stanford's ten web-credibility guidelines: make it easy/useful; tout experience, credentials, awards; keep content fresh; state your honesty and add real employee bios; make contact effortless (email, phone, socials visible); back every claim with sourced proof; avoid hype; eliminate spelling/grammar/link errors; convey solidity (photos of real people, HQ, physical address); and — highest leverage — use professional design (crowdsource via 99designs/DesignCrowd rather than DIY templates). For 50+ audiences, use sans-serif at 14pt (Arial/Verdana) for legibility, since illegible = invisible and low legibility also lowers believability ([CSHO:14]).
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL
- **Applies / fails when:** Governs any site/page where the buyer must trust you before paying. Design-first weighting holds for general consumer audiences; for health, finance, and expert buyers, substance/accuracy/sources dominate — lead with evidence there. Hype and errors are the fast credibility killers.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Professionally designed page, founder photo + bio, "reviewed by [named sources]," phone/chat visible, zero typos, sans-serif 14pt for a retiree audience. Negative: a template site with a stock "team" photo, unsourced "#1 in the world!" claims, and a broken contact form — instant distrust.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Профессионально свёрстанная страница, фото и био основателя, «проверено [названные источники]», телефон и чат на виду, ноль опечаток, для аудитории 50+ — sans-serif 14pt. Антипример: шаблонный сайт со стоковым фото «команды», бездоказательное «№1 в мире!» и нерабочая форма контакта — мгновенное недоверие.
- **Book ref:** Whitman's "sloppy stranger vs. beloved millionaire offering the same $10-for-$1,000 deal" frames credibility, then cites Stanford's Web Credibility Project (4,500 participants, ten guidelines) and Fogg's finding that design was mentioned in 46.1% of credibility judgments.

### [CSHO:18] Online Pricing Psychology  (ch 5, Secrets #20, #24, #25)
- **Essence:** How you *express and frame* a price reshapes perceived value independent of the number itself — via discount framing, decoys, anchors, typography, and benefit-stacking. The buyer weighs Skepticism against Desire-to-Believe; pricing presentation and stacked benefits tip that scale.
- **Copy move:** Operational pricing levers — (1) **Rule of 100 discount framing:** under $100 show percent-off, over $100 show dollars-off — always express whichever number is larger; (2) **Decoy effect:** add a deliberately overpriced option to make your target (middle) option look affordable and steer buyers up (weaker when buyers have a fixed brand preference or the product needs tasting/handling); (3) **Anchoring:** flank your target price with much pricier options / tiers so it reads as a bargain and supplies a spend-rationalization; (4) **Fewer syllables:** write $1295 not $1,295.00 (shorter reads as smaller); (5) drop the currency symbol where possible (menu study: diners spent more on symbol-free numerals); (6) show a lone price in *larger* font for confidence, but a sale price beside an anchor in *smaller* font to read as cheaper; (7) **red price for a male audience** reads as more of a bargain; (8) **stack benefits** until perceived value exceeds the price. Test everything.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL
- **Applies / fails when:** For pricing tables, offers, checkout, menus. Decoy/anchor need genuine indifference and clear numeric/graphic comparison; brand-loyal or expert buyers resist. Manipulative-feeling anchors on trust-sensitive audiences can backfire — keep options real. Gendered red-price effect washes out for female audiences.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Three tiers — $429 Pro (decoy), $119 Standard (target, larger price, benefits stacked), $56 Basic — with "Save $63" (dollars, since >$100) on the target. Negative: a single lone $119 in tiny grey type with no anchor and no benefits — reads as expensive and unjustified.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Три тарифа — 34 900 ₽ Pro (приманка), 9 900 ₽ Standard (цель, цена крупнее, выгоды сложены стопкой), 4 900 ₽ Basic — на целевом «Экономия 5 000 ₽» (в рублях, т.к. >100$-эквивалент). Антипример: одинокие 9 900 ₽ мелким серым шрифтом без якоря и без выгод — читается как дорого и необоснованно.
- **Book ref:** Whitman cites González's "Rule of 100" discount study, Ariely's *Economist* decoy experiment (adding a same-price Print-Only option pushed 84% to the combo), and seven psychological-pricing studies (syllables, red-for-men, font size, dropped symbols, anchoring, benefit-stacking).

### [CSHO:19] Anxiety & Uncertainty Reduction  (ch 5, Secrets #6, #23; checklist #13)
- **Essence:** Fear sits on the far side of Desire — buyers hesitate over risk, waiting, and confusion. Making support *visibly available* (the Buffer Effect: mere presence of help reduces stress even unused) and reducing on-page uncertainty (progress feedback, numbered steps, speed) converts hesitation into action.
- **Copy move:** Buffer side — give customer support star treatment, not a buried FAQ line: searchable FAQ, short (<1 min) how-to videos, monitored social channels with fast replies (13% expect a reply within 1 hour, 76% within a day), live chat (recovers hesitating carts), omni-channel human support, and real photos of the support team with a quoted line. Uncertainty side — keep pages loading under ~2–3 seconds (40% leave after 3s); when waits are unavoidable, show a progress bar / system feedback (progress bar raised tolerable wait ~13s → ~38s); number the checkout steps with a progress indicator (up to +8.6% completion); split long forms across pages to cut perceived effort; ask buyers to explicitly *reject* an upsell ("[ ] No thanks, I'll pass on 60% off") to trigger loss aversion; be mobile-first with a visible guest-checkout. Operational persuasive-site checklist (Secret #13, cross-refs): reviews on the main page [CSHO:05] · authority endorsements [CSHO:05/17] · real scarcity/FOMO (countdown, recent-sale pops) · mobile + <3s load · top-load the biggest benefit above the fold [CSHO:02] · bulletize lists · standard navbar, no drop-downs · anchor the preferred tier [CSHO:18] · high-contrast (not color-dependent) CTA [CSHO:06] · scatter CTAs [CSHO:07] · gaze/point a face at the CTA [CSHO:10] · number steps · reject-the-CTA · limit options.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL
- **Applies / fails when:** For conversion pages, checkout, and post-sale retention. Strongest where perceived risk is the blocker (new brand, higher ticket, SaaS). Fake scarcity or spoofed recent-sale pop-ups destroy trust; a progress bar helps only if the wait then resolves — chronic slowness still loses the sale.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Checkout: "Step 2 of 3" progress bar, live-chat bubble with a real rep's photo, guest checkout up top, "ships by Fri, arrives Mon," 60-day returns stated. Negative: a spinner with no progress, a 5-field form on one page, support hidden in a footer FAQ — anxious buyers bail.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Чекаут: индикатор «Шаг 2 из 3», всплывающий чат с реальным фото оператора, гостевой заказ сверху, «отправим в пятницу, доставим в понедельник», «возврат 60 дней». Антипример: бесконечный спиннер без прогресса, форма из 5 полей на одной странице, поддержка спрятана в футере — тревожный покупатель уходит.
- **Book ref:** Whitman ties the Nuckolls buffer-effect pregnancy study (91% complications under low support vs. 33% under high) to visible customer support, and Nah's TWT experiment where a progress bar nearly tripled users' patience.

### [CSHO:20] Cart-Abandonment Recovery  (ch 5, Secret #26)
- **Essence:** ~69% of ready-to-buy shoppers vanish at checkout. Excluding the "just browsing" majority, abandonment has identifiable, fixable causes — surprise costs, forced accounts, slow delivery, distrust, and checkout friction — each recoverable.
- **Copy move:** Attack the top eight causes (Baymard): surface all costs early (surprise shipping/tax = the #1 killer at ~48%); offer a prominent guest checkout beside the login (forced accounts lose ~24%); show an *estimated delivery date*, not just "shipping speed"; add payment-trust signals (badges, secured icons); shorten checkout and simplify passwords (≥6 lowercase letters is enough); show the running order total; let buyers edit order data inline at review; make accordion steps respect the Back button; mark both required (red asterisk) and optional fields; state a generous return policy; fix all site errors. Then recover leavers with an email/retargeting sequence — a "you left this in your cart" subject line hits up to ~48% opens ([CSHO:21]).
- **Applies to:** landing | email
- **Applies / fails when:** For any e-commerce/checkout flow and post-abandon follow-up. Fixes address structural friction and trust; they won't recover the ~59% who simply "weren't ready to buy" — for those, retargeting + nurture, not checkout tweaks. Aggressive account-creation walls remain the self-inflicted failure.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Cart page shows "$0 shipping · arrives Mon Oct 14 · guest checkout ✓ · Visa/PayPal secured," then a 24-hour "Your cart's still here — here's 10% to finish" email. Negative: total shipping revealed only on the final step + a mandatory account signup — the classic two-thirds walk.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Страница корзины: «Доставка 0 ₽ · привезём в пн 14 окт · оформление без регистрации ✓ · оплата картой/СБП защищена», затем письмо через 24 часа «Ваша корзина ждёт — вот −10%, чтобы завершить». Антипример: полную стоимость доставки показывают только на последнем шаге плюс обязательная регистрация — классический уход двух третей.
- **Book ref:** Whitman cites Baymard's 11-year, 10,721-participant study ranking abandonment causes (extra costs 48%, forced account 24%, slow delivery 22%, payment distrust 18%) and their fixes across 37,000+ reviewed checkout elements.

### [CSHO:21] Email Subject-Line Rules  (ch 5, Secret #27)
- **Essence:** The subject line is the email's headline and gate — ~47% open (and ~69% report spam) based on it alone. Small, testable wording choices move open rates measurably.
- **Copy move:** Data-backed rules — personalize with the recipient's name (and ideally a second personal fact) for the biggest lift; keep it ~6–10 words (open rate falls as length rises); "Free" ~+10%; explicit urgency/exclusivity ~+22%; a cart reminder up to ~48% opens; an *empty* subject can beat a bad one (~+8%). Avoid "Newsletter" (−18.7%), "Fw:" (−17%), and discount-led lines (below average). Emoji effect is genuinely split — test with your list. Prefer response words (Demo, Connect, Opportunity, Apply, Conference, Cancellation, Payments); avoid weak ones (Invite, Social, Speaker, Press, Join, Confirm, Assistance). Always follow up: ~6 emails over 3 weeks, spaced 3–4 days, first within 24h (~25% response). For influencer outreach, the subject is the first name only ([CSHO:08]).
- **Applies to:** email
- **Applies / fails when:** For any email/newsletter/outreach open-rate optimization. Rules are directional averages — the standing instruction is test-on-your-list (emoji, discount, length all flip by audience). Over-personalization or false urgency erodes trust and trains the spam reflex.
- **Few-shot (EN):** "Scott, your Onion Ring Journal piece + a quick idea" (name + second fact, curiosity). Negative: "NEWSLETTER: Fw: Huge Discounts Inside!!!" — three open-rate killers stacked ("Newsletter," "Fw:," discount-led).
- **Few-shot (RU):** «Скотт, ваша статья в "Луковых кольцах" + короткая идея» (имя + второй факт, интрига). Антипример: «РАССЫЛКА: Fw: Огромные скидки внутри!!!» — три убийцы открываемости в одном («рассылка», «Fw:», лидирующая скидка).
- **Book ref:** Whitman compiles 14 subject-line facts — name personalization (~18–50% open), "Free" +10%, urgency +22%, cart reminder up to 48%, "Newsletter" −18.7% — plus Yesware's follow-up cadence (6 emails / 3 weeks / 3–4 days apart) and Boomerang's best/worst word lists.

### [CSHO:22] Posting Timing & Format Optimization  (ch 5, Secrets #9, #12, #14, #15)
- **Essence:** Beyond the words, the *container* — post format, length, media mix, video structure, and send-time — measurably shifts engagement. Large studies give defaults that beat guessing, but timing findings conflict enough that they're starting points to test, not laws.
- **Copy move:** Content-format rules (100k-message + German page studies): always include a photo (photos win likes/comments; text-only and *links* lose); questions and fill-in-the-blanks spike comments (blanks +214%) but depress likes, so also ask for the Like; explicitly *invite* engagement (strongest comment driver); keep messages short, simple, positive, emotional, and *persuasive* (product-info-alone reduces engagement — pair it with persuasion); bigger visual size and more media elements lift likes; avoid holiday posts. Cadence: comments die ~2 days, likes ~6 days, ~99.9% of engagement within 15 days — repost before you vanish. Video rules (Sitaraman): mid-roll placement completes best (~97% vs. 74% pre / 45% post); 15s and 30s beat 20s; hook in the first 3 seconds (first words = the video's headline); always add captions (~69% watch muted, +~12% view time); give a dynamic first frame; show the play button. Send-time: use per-platform windows as a first guess, then A/B on your own audience.
  - **Rough send-time defaults (local, guide-not-gospel):** Facebook — Thu/Fri 1–3pm; Instagram — Tue/Wed 10am–2pm; Twitter/X — weekday mornings ~9am–noon; LinkedIn — Tue–Thu 9–11am; TikTok — Tue/Thu afternoon–evening; YouTube/Pinterest — late afternoon–evening, weekends.
- **Applies to:** short-form | VSL
- **Applies / fails when:** For organic and paid social creative and scheduling. Format rules are robust; send-times conflict across sources, so treat them as priors to test — an empty best-time slot won't save weak copy/offer/targeting. "Likes vs. comments" tradeoffs mean you optimize for the metric that matters (comments = deeper social proof).
- **Few-shot (EN):** A short, positive, photo-led post ending "Which would you pick — A or B? 👇 (Like first, then comment)," a 15s captioned video with a mid-roll ad slot, shipped Thursday 1pm, then reposted day 6. Negative: a long text-only link post with no image, no question, sent Sunday — low on every axis.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Короткий позитивный пост с фото, в конце «Что выберете — А или Б? 👇 (сначала лайк, потом коммент)», 15-секундное видео с субтитрами, отправлено в четверг в 13:00, репост на 6-й день. Антипример: длинный текстовый пост со ссылкой, без картинки и вопроса, в воскресенье — низкий отклик везде.
- **Book ref:** Whitman translates Lee/Hosanagar/Nair's 100,000-message Facebook study (16 findings), the Bamberg fan-page study, and Sitaraman's video study (362M videos) into format rules, then tabulates a dozen sources' conflicting best-post-times per platform.

### [CSHO:23] Opt-in Headline Formulas  (ch 6)
- **Essence:** Your house list is your highest-value asset, but it only grows if the subscribe headline converts. Fifty proven opt-in headlines reduce to a handful of reusable template families built on curiosity, specificity, free value, and low-friction asks.
- **Copy move:** Assemble an opt-in headline from these pattern families (fill the blanks with your niche): (1) **Numbered list** — "[X] Ways/Steps/Tricks to ___ (without ___)"; (2) **Free lead-magnet** — "Grab my free [X]-page report ___ and start ___ today"; (3) **Timeframe promise** — "Give me [X] days and I'll show you exactly how to ___"; (4) **Question-target** — "Do you want to ___ faster, with less ___?" (pairs with [CSHO:16]); (5) **Emotional interjection opener** — "ARGH! / OUCH! / OMG-WHAT?! ___" for high-arousal stop; (6) **Social-proof count** — "Join over [X] subscribers who get ___"; (7) **Results-story** — "How we went from [$X] to [$Y] in under a year — enter your email to see"; (8) **Scarcity** — "Limited: only 47 spots left ___". Front-load a benefit or curiosity gap, promise a fast/easy payoff, and end with a one-field email ask.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | short-form
- **Applies / fails when:** For lead-capture pages, content upgrades, and list-building CTAs. Templates are scaffolds — a fill-in headline that fails the table test ([CSHO:02]) or over-hypes a thin lead magnet won't convert. Scarcity/results-story variants must be true (see credibility [CSHO:17]).
- **Few-shot (EN):** "7 Tricks to Cut Your AWS Bill in Half — grab my free 9-page checklist and start today." (numbered + free lead-magnet + fast payoff). Negative: "Subscribe to stay updated" — no benefit, no curiosity, no reason.
- **Few-shot (RU):** «7 приёмов, чтобы урезать счёт за облако вдвое — заберите бесплатный чек-лист на 9 страниц и начните сегодня» (список + лид-магнит + быстрая выгода). Антипример: «Подпишитесь, чтобы быть в курсе» — ни выгоды, ни интриги, ни причины.
- **Book ref:** Whitman supplies 50 fill-in-the-blank opt-in headline templates ranging from "[XX]-Point Success Checklist" to "OMG…WHAT?! Did you ever see how millionaires ___?" — meant as copy-or-adapt springboards.
