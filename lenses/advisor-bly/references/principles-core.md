# The Copywriter's Handbook — Robert W. Bly  ·  prefix [CPH]
> Copy is salesmanship in print: every word earns its place by moving the reader one step closer to buying. Reach for this lens when a copywriting decision hinges on persuasion mechanics — clarity, benefit selection, promise strength, proof, and the call to action — rather than on channel logistics or brand aesthetics.

## Principles

### [CPH:01] Copy is salesmanship, not art  (ch 1)
- **Essence:** The copywriter is a salesperson at a keyboard, not a creative artist. The only scoreboard is response — orders, leads, conversions — so cleverness that obscures the sell is a defect, not a flourish.
- **Copy move:** Before finalizing any line, ask "does this move the prospect toward buying?" If a pun, borrowed-interest scenario, or award-bait phrase doesn't advance the sale, cut it. Reuse what already works instead of chasing originality.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL — all.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to any direct-response piece where a measurable action is the goal. Loosens for pure brand/awareness work funded to build long-term recognition (rare for small budgets), where memorability may legitimately outrank immediate response.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — "Cut your AWS bill 40% in one afternoon" (sells) over "Cloud, reimagined." (award-bait). Negative — a fintech app headlines "Banking that sparks joy" with no benefit; readers feel amused, nobody signs up.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — «Сократи расходы на облако на 40% за один вечер» вместо абстрактного «Облако. Переосмыслено». Антипример — сервис доставки берёт слоган «Еда, которая обнимает» без выгоды и оффера: красиво, но заказов не даёт; замена на «Горячий обед у двери за 25 минут — или вернём деньги» поднимает отклик.
- **Book ref:** Bly cites the maxim that copywriters are not in the business of being original but of reusing things that work, and warns that creativity-for-its-own-sake produces cleverness that hides the selling message.

### [CPH:02] The digital triad: emotion, data, compliance  (ch 1)
- **Essence:** Pre-internet, good copy was driven mainly by human emotion. Today three forces steer it at once: human emotion (still primary), data from live analytics, and compliance with each platform's ad rules. Human nature hasn't changed; the constraints and feedback loops around it have.
- **Copy move:** Write the emotionally strongest version first, then pressure-test it against measured results (let data trump your instincts when they conflict) and against the platform's policy so the ad actually runs. When compliance would gut the message, find an honest reframe rather than a limp "perfectly compliant" line — over-zealous compliance measurably weakens response.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL — especially paid social/search.
- **Applies / fails when:** Data must win when you have a statistically real sample; below that, trust craft and emotion. Compliance is non-negotiable where rejection means zero reach (Meta, Google), looser on owned channels (your own email/site).
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — a weight-loss ad banned for "lose 10 lbs in 7 days" is honestly reframed as a curiosity/food angle: "The breakfast most 'diet' plans get wrong." Negative — writer keeps resubmitting a specific-claim ad that never gets approved, so it reaches no one.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — прямой оффер по инвестициям, который отклоняет модерация, переписывается в образовательный: «3 ошибки новичков на бирже, которые видят все опытные трейдеры». Антипример — таргетолог упирается в запрещённую формулировку «гарантированный доход» и месяц крутит объявление, которое не проходит модерацию Яндекс.Директа → нулевой охват.
- **Book ref:** Bly frames modern copy around emotion, data, and compliance, noting a rule of thumb that each 10% closer to "perfect" compliance can cost about 10% of response.

### [CPH:03] The headline's four functions  (ch 2)
- **Essence:** On average five times as many people read the headline as the body, so the headline carries ~80% of the ad's work. A strong one does four jobs: (1) get attention, (2) select the right audience, (3) deliver a complete message on its own, (4) pull the reader into the body copy.
- **Copy move:** Draft the headline to hit as many of the four functions as the piece needs. Lead with the single strongest benefit or news; name or flag the target audience to screen out non-buyers; when most prospects won't read the body, put the full promise (and often the brand) right in the headline; use curiosity, a question, or an intrigue to draw serious readers deeper.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email (subject line) | VSL (first 20s).
- **Applies / fails when:** All four functions rarely fit one headline — prioritize by medium. Audience-selection matters most where an unqualified response wastes money (high-ticket, niche); "complete message" matters most where body copy won't be read (skimmed print, half-page ads).
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique (select + complete) — "To freelancers earning $100k+ who still overpay their taxes: this 15-minute setup saved my clients $8,300 last year." Negative (blind headline) — "Give it a hand" over a copy block; means nothing until you read on, so few do.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём (выбор аудитории + полный посыл) — «Для самозанятых с оборотом от 2 млн: как легально платить меньше налогов и не бояться проверок». Антипример — «Дай пять!» как заголовок баннера страховой: интригует, но не отбирает аудиторию и ничего не обещает → клики есть, заявок нет.
- **Book ref:** Bly quotes Ogilvy that once you've written the headline you've spent 80 cents of your dollar, and lists the four headline functions with worked examples (Dale Carnegie, Duncan Hines, life-insurance targeting).

### [CPH:04] The 4 U's headline test  (ch 2)
- **Essence:** A diagnostic for headlines, subject lines, teasers, leads, and bullets: strong ones are **Urgent** (a reason to act now), **Unique** (says something new or in a fresh way), **Ultra-specific** (concrete numbers/details, not vague), and **Useful** (a self-interest benefit).
- **Copy move:** Score your draft 1–4 on each U. If it doesn't rate 3–4 on at least three of the four, rewrite to lift at least two categories by a point. Add a time element for urgency; sharpen vague nouns into specifics ("free report" → "free 12-page report on cutting cloud costs 40%").
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email (subject line) | VSL — any headline/hook/bullet.
- **Applies / fails when:** Universally applicable as a scoring pass. Urgency can ring false if fabricated (a "deadline" that never expires erodes trust); specificity must be true — inventing numbers to score higher is a compliance and credibility risk.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — weak "Free Marketing Tips" (U:1/2/1/2) → strong "Get our 27-point pre-launch checklist free — before your next campaign flops" (adds urgency, specificity, usefulness). Negative — "Big News Inside!" scores 1 on unique, specific, and useful; it's pure filler.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — слабое «Полезные советы по продажам» → сильное «7 скриптов возражений, которые закрыли 3 из 5 холодных клиентов в нашем отделе — заберите до конца недели». Антипример — тема письма «Важное сообщение!» проваливает уникальность, конкретику и пользу → низкий open rate.
- **Book ref:** Bly presents Mark Ford's 4 U's formula and rescores a real "Free Special Report" subject line category by category to show how each U can be lifted.

### [CPH:05] Write to communicate — clarity & the you-orientation  (ch 3)
- **Essence:** Advertising sells more when it's easy to understand. Clarity comes from putting the reader first and speaking to them as "you," plus mechanics: organized selling points, short sections and sentences, small words, no jargon, concision, specifics, getting straight to the point, and a friendly conversational tone. "Borrowed interest" (hiding behind a cute unrelated scenario) is a top cause of confusion.
- **Copy move:** Rewrite advertiser-oriented statements into reader-oriented ones — replace "the product does X" with "you get Y." Break dense copy into short chunks; cut needless words; swap abstractions for concrete specifics ("saves money" → "saves $340 a year").
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL — all prose.
- **Applies / fails when:** Always applies. The only tension: some products/audiences reward long copy — clarity does not mean brevity (see [CPH:13]); it means every retained word is understood instantly.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — advertiser-oriented "Our platform leverages proprietary ML for churn mitigation" → you-oriented "You'll spot which customers are about to leave — in time to keep them." Negative — a bridal-gown ad set on a fictional planet to seem clever; readers can't tell what's for sale.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — «Наша платформа использует проприетарные ML-алгоритмы для снижения оттока» → «Вы заранее увидите, кто из клиентов вот-вот уйдёт — и успеете их удержать». Антипример — реклама CRM через выдуманную сценку про космонавтов «в далёкой галактике продажи тоже буксуют»: заимствованный интерес, продукт теряется.
- **Book ref:** Bly's 11 tips for clear copy contrast advertiser-oriented vs. you-oriented columns and dissect a real "borrowed interest" bridal ad his students nicknamed "What did she say?".

### [CPH:06] Sell benefits, not features  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** A feature is what the product is or has; a benefit is what it does for the reader. Prospects buy outcomes, not specs. Every feature should be translated into the concrete payoff the buyer actually cares about.
- **Copy move:** Build a two-column features/benefits list, then write from the benefit side. Use the "which means" bridge: "800mg battery (feature) — which means you go two full days without a charger (benefit)." Lead with the benefit that matters most to this audience, confirmed by knowing them (see [CPH:09], [CPH:15]).
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL — all.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies broadly. For highly technical B2B buyers (engineers, IT), specs themselves can be the benefit — they need exact specifications to decide; there, list features precisely and still tie the decisive ones to outcomes.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — feature "end-to-end encryption" → benefit "your client contracts stay private even if our servers are breached." Negative — a laptop ad lists "16GB RAM, 512GB SSD, 14-inch panel" with no payoff; the shopper can't tell why it beats the cheaper one.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — фича «шумоподавление 2-го поколения» → выгода «слышите только собеседника — даже в вагоне метро». Антипример — карточка ноутбука на Ozon перечисляет «16 ГБ ОЗУ, SSD 512 ГБ, экран 14″» без выгод; покупатель не понимает, чем он лучше модели за меньшие деньги, и уходит по цене.
- **Book ref:** Bly instructs writers to draw up a complete features-and-benefits list and sell from the benefits, using the insulin-needle case where "sharp" was reframed from a scare into a benefit ("goes in smoothly, nearly painless").

### [CPH:07] Big promise + secondary promise  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** To break through clutter, copy must make a large, powerful promise — small promises don't move people. But a promise so big it strains belief triggers skepticism. The fix is a secondary promise: a smaller, easily believed benefit that, on its own, still justifies buying.
- **Copy move:** Lead with the boldest true promise, then place a secondary promise right beneath it (in the subhead or lead) so the skeptic can think "even if the big claim is only half true, the backup alone is worth it — I can't lose." Support both with proof (see [CPH:11]).
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email — long-form direct response especially.
- **Applies / fails when:** Strong where the offer genuinely carries a bold outcome (investing, health, income, transformation). Fails when the "big promise" is fabricated or non-compliant; the secondary promise is pointless if the whole claim is untrue.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — big: "This course can take you from zero to a $5k/month freelance income in 90 days." Secondary: "And even if you never land a single client, you'll walk away with a portfolio and three vetted lead sources worth the price alone." Negative — a single sky-high promise with no fallback ("Make $50k a month!") that readers dismiss instantly.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — большое: «Этот курс способен вывести вас с нуля до 150 000 ₽/мес на фрилансе за 90 дней». Вторичное: «А даже если ни одного клиента вы так и не найдёте — у вас останется готовое портфолио и 3 проверенных источника заявок, которые окупают цену сами по себе». Антипример — один гиперобещающий заголовок «Зарабатывай 500 000 в месяц!» без подстраховки: читатель сразу отметает как развод.
- **Book ref:** Bly shows a biotech-stock promotion whose 50-fold "big promise" was buttressed by a subhead promising a 500% gain even if the lead treatment failed, so skeptics could still justify the purchase.

### [CPH:08] The Unique Selling Proposition  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** Rosser Reeves's USP: a claim that (1) makes a specific benefit proposition to the buyer, (2) is one the competition cannot or does not make, and (3) is strong enough to pull people over to your product. Without a differentiated, meaningful promise, copy sounds like everyone else and gives no reason to respond.
- **Copy move:** Find the benefit only you can claim, or a true benefit rivals have simply never voiced, and build the whole promotion on it. If no product-level difference exists, differentiate by application ("call us for these specific jobs"), by dramatizing a benefit, by the name/package, or (with big budgets) brand personality. Avoid USPs built on differences the buyer doesn't care about.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL — sets the spine of the whole message.
- **Applies / fails when:** Essential for any competitive category. The trap: a difference that's real but trivial to the buyer (a novel pump design that yields no performance gain) — it differentiates without persuading.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique (unvoiced true claim) — "The only project tool that shows you which task is silently blocking every deadline." Negative — "Now with a redesigned settings menu" as the lead differentiator: unique, but nobody switches tools for it.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём (озвучить то, что не сказали конкуренты) — «Единственный конструктор лендингов, где страница грузится меньше секунды даже на 3G». Антипример — «Теперь с обновлённым дизайном личного кабинета» как главное отличие: уникально, но никто ради этого не меняет сервис.
- **Book ref:** Bly explains Reeves's three USP requirements and the Blackmer Pump campaign, which built its USP on application ("there are only certain times you should call Blackmer — know when?") rather than an unpersuasive design tweak.

### [CPH:09] Know your customer — three levels & the BDF formula  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** Powerful copy reaches the prospect on three levels — intellectual (logic), emotional (fear, greed, love, vanity), and personal (their own life and stakes), the personal being strongest. To get there, map the buyer's "Core Complex" via BDF: **Beliefs** (what they hold true / their attitude to the problem), **Desires** (what they want and the change they seek), **Feelings** (how they feel — anxious, proud, resentful).
- **Copy move:** Before writing, draft the BDF of the target in narrative form and get the team to agree on it; then write from it, especially the feelings. Empathize and "pace" the reader — mirror their situation so they sense "this writer gets me" — and stress only the benefits that map to their real motivations.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL — all; the research backbone of persuasion.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies whenever you know who the buyer is. Weak when BDF is guessed in a vacuum — assumptions about beliefs/feelings can be dead wrong; validate against interviews, reviews, focus-group transcripts (see [CPH:15]).
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique (feelings-led) — for burnt-out agency designers: "For the designer who's done redoing 'just one more' round of revisions at 11pm." Negative — a generic "Boost your productivity!" that ignores what the audience actually believes and feels.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём (через чувства) — для выгоревших дизайнеров агентств: «Для дизайнера, который в 11 вечера снова переделывает "последнюю правочку"». Антипример — безликое «Повысьте свою продуктивность!», не задевающее ни одну реальную боль или установку аудитории.
- **Book ref:** Bly presents Mark Ford's BDF formula and the IT-professionals case, where mapping their feelings produced the winning headline "for any IT professional who has ever felt like telling an end user, 'Go to hell.'"

### [CPH:10] Copy motivators — the buying reasons  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** People buy the same product for different underlying reasons. Bly's checklist of ~22 motivators (to be liked, appreciated, right, important; to make/save money, save time, make work easier; to be secure, attractive, comfortable, distinctive, healthy; out of fear, greed, guilt; curiosity, convenience, fun, knowledge…) is a prompt to find the true driver behind the purchase.
- **Copy move:** Before writing, run the product past the motivator list and identify which one or two actually drive this audience's purchase, then aim the copy at that emotional lever — not at the feature you personally find interesting.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL — angle selection.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to consumer and emotionally-driven B2B buys. Weaker for pure commodity/spec purchases where the "motivator" is simply price or availability; there, lead with the practical, not the emotional.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — same watch, two motivators: "Never miss a heartbeat you should worry about" (fear/health) vs. "The one detail that tells the room you've arrived" (distinctive/status). Negative — selling a luxury pen on "reliable ink flow" (function) when buyers want status and self-image.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — один и тот же курс английского, два мотива: «Перестаньте молчать на совещаниях с иностранными коллегами» (страх/статус) vs. «Смотрите сериалы в оригинале уже через 3 месяца» (удовольствие). Антипример — премиальную ручку продают через «надёжную подачу чернил», хотя покупают её ради статуса и образа себя.
- **Book ref:** Bly lists 22 copy motivators and illustrates that a car buyer may want plain transport while a Porsche buyer is buying status — same category, different driver.

### [CPH:11] Prove the claim — credibility & proof  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** Asserting a benefit isn't enough; skeptical readers risking their money need proof. The persuasion arc's fourth beat is demonstrating the product does what you say. Proof devices: testimonials (third-party praise beats self-praise), competitive comparisons, cited studies/clinical trials, track record and company stability, guarantees.
- **Copy move:** For every major claim, attach the strongest available proof. Prefer specific, verifiable evidence (named customers, real numbers, study citations, "in business since 1998, 12,000 clients served") over adjectives. Use a guarantee to reverse the buyer's risk.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL | short-form — anywhere a claim is made.
- **Applies / fails when:** Critical for bold promises, high price, unfamiliar brands, and skeptical audiences. Less load-bearing for cheap, low-risk, familiar impulse buys where proof adds friction more than reassurance.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — "Independently audited: our clients cut support tickets 38% in the first 90 days (see the 2024 study, linked)." Negative — "Trusted by thousands!" with no name, number, or link — an empty adjective the reader discounts.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — «По данным независимого аудита, клиенты сократили число обращений в поддержку на 38% за первые 90 дней (исследование 2024 — ссылка)». Антипример — «Нам доверяют тысячи!» без единого имени, цифры или ссылки: пустое прилагательное, которому не верят.
- **Book ref:** Bly's fourth motivating-sequence step lists proof techniques — testimonials, benefit-by-benefit comparison, cited studies (double-blind trials for supplements), and evidence of company stability.

### [CPH:12] Always ask for action  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** The last step of any piece of copy is a call for action. Copy that informs but never asks leaves the sale on the table. Make the next step explicit, easy, and — where possible — urgent.
- **Copy move:** End with one clear CTA that names the exact next step (buy, download, book a call). Remove friction: give the phone/URL, accept cards, provide the form/map/hours; if it's a lead step, say what happens next. Add an incentive for acting now (discount, deadline, bonus for the first N).
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL — every response piece.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies universally. Multiple competing CTAs dilute response — favor a single primary action per piece; a two-step (lead) offer needs a lighter ask than a one-step (direct sale).
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — "Start your free 14-day trial now — no card needed. Set-up takes under 5 minutes, and the first 100 signups this week get our onboarding call free." Negative — a page that ends "Learn more about our solutions" with no button, price, or next step.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — «Начните бесплатный 14-дневный период прямо сейчас — карта не нужна. Настройка займёт меньше 5 минут, а первым 100 за эту неделю — бесплатный созвон по внедрению». Антипример — страница заканчивается фразой «Узнайте больше о наших решениях» без кнопки, цены и понятного следующего шага.
- **Book ref:** Bly's fifth motivating-sequence step insists every piece close by asking for the order right away, making response effortless and giving an incentive to act now rather than later.

### [CPH:13] Copy length = emotion × involvement  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** The right length isn't a word count — it's however much information is needed to accomplish the sale. Bly's Copy Length Grid uses two axes: **emotion** (how emotional the purchase is) and **involvement** (how much thought/effort goes into it). High-high (engagement ring, luxury car) wants long copy; low-low impulse buys (paper clips) want almost none. Modifiers: price, purpose (one-step vs. lead), audience time, need vs. want, familiarity.
- **Copy move:** Rate the offer high/low on emotion and involvement to pick a length quadrant, then adjust for the modifiers. Don't fear long copy for high-emotion, high-involvement, high-price "must-be-sold" items; don't pad low-stakes impulse products.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email | short-form — length strategy.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to the length decision. Constrained channels (banner, PPC ad, SMS) force short copy regardless — there the job is to earn the click to a long page where the real selling happens.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — a $4,000 coaching program (high emotion, high involvement) gets a 4,000-word VSL/page; a $6 phone case gets a headline, three bullets, and a buy button. Negative — 2,000 words of passionate prose selling a pack of binder clips: effort wasted on a low-low purchase.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — наставничество за 300 000 ₽ (высокая эмоция и вовлечённость) заслуживает длинной VSL-страницы на 4000 слов; чехол для телефона за 500 ₽ — заголовок, три буллета и кнопка «Купить». Антипример — 2000 слов страстного текста ради упаковки канцелярских зажимов: усилия впустую на импульсной покупке.
- **Book ref:** Bly introduces the Copy Length Grid (emotion × involvement) with the diamond-ring vs. paper-clip contrast and lists price, purpose, audience, importance, and familiarity as further length modifiers.

### [CPH:14] Positioning — own a slot in the mind  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** Positioning (Ries & Trout) is what advertising does for the product in the prospect's mind, not just communicating its features. If your product fills a niche, positioning it relative to a well-known reference plants a fast, clear identity. Positioning complements features/benefits — it doesn't replace the need to say what the product does and why it's better.
- **Copy move:** Decide the one mental slot you want to own and anchor it to something the prospect already understands (a category leader, an alternative, a use-occasion). Then still deliver the benefit and proof — positioning gets you considered; the sell closes.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email — brand/message framing.
- **Applies / fails when:** Strong in crowded categories where a reference point clarifies fast. Fails as a substitute for persuasion — a clever position with no benefit or proof gets you thought about but not bought.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — "The Notion for accountants — every client, doc, and deadline in one place, built for the way books actually get done." Negative — "A bold new paradigm in workflow" — a position with no reference point and no benefit, so it means nothing.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — «Тинькофф для самозанятых-творческих: счета, договоры и налоги в одном приложении, заточенном под фриланс». Антипример — «Новая парадигма управления задачами» — позиция без точки отсчёта и без выгоды, читатель не понимает, куда её приложить.
- **Book ref:** Bly cites Ries & Trout and examples like Coors positioned on Rocky Mountain water, stressing that positioning complements — never replaces — features, benefits, and the sales argument.

### [CPH:15] Prepare before you write  (ch 5)
- **Essence:** Roughly 90% of what you need is already sitting in background material; interviews supply the last 10%. Skipping preparation produces copy written "in a vacuum" that stresses what the writer finds interesting instead of what the buyer cares about. Specifics — which only research surfaces — are what sell.
- **Copy move:** Before drafting, gather everything (past ads, brochures, reviews, tech specs, competitor sites, market research, customer letters) and answer three question sets: about the product (features/benefits, differentiators, price, guarantee), the audience (who buys, why, main concern, what motivates them), and the objective (sale, lead, traffic, image). Fill remaining gaps with a fact-seeking interview of product experts.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL — pre-writing for all.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to every serious assignment. Scale it down for tiny/familiar jobs; scale it up for technical, high-ticket, or unfamiliar products where wrong facts kill credibility.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — before writing a SaaS page, the writer reads 40 support tickets and 30 reviews, and finds the real buying trigger is "onboarding takes weeks" — which becomes the headline. Negative — writer opens a blank doc and leads with the CTO's favorite feature (the API), which buyers never mention.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — перед текстом для SaaS автор читает 40 обращений в поддержку и 30 отзывов и находит настоящий триггер покупки — «внедрение тянется неделями», который и становится заголовком. Антипример — автор открывает пустой документ и выносит в оффер любимую фичу техдиректора (открытый API), о которой покупатели не заикаются.
- **Book ref:** Bly's four-step prep (get all published material; ask about product, audience, objective) claims background reading yields ~90% of needed information, with interviews supplying the rest, because "specifics sell."

### [CPH:16] False logic — marshal facts to the conclusion  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** "False logic" (Mark Ford's term) is skillful framing of true facts so the reader draws the conclusion you want — without lying or misrepresenting. People buy on emotion and rationalize with logic, so credible-sounding support for what they already want to do is persuasive even if a strict logician would parse it differently.
- **Copy move:** Select and frame genuine facts to lean toward the sale — a price anchored against a far costlier alternative, a statistic angled toward exclusivity, a comparison that flatters your product. Keep it honest (no false claims); the ethic is advocacy from true facts, like a lawyer arguing a case.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email — value and price framing.
- **Applies / fails when:** Useful where facts genuinely support the frame. Crosses the line — and courts legal/compliance risk — when the framing implies something false; Bly draws the line at deception, not at advocacy.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique (price anchor) — "A consultant would charge $5,000 to build this system for you. The course that teaches you to build it yourself: $199." Negative — implying a "limited stock" scarcity that doesn't exist: that's deception, not framing.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём (якорь цены) — «Настройка такой воронки под ключ у агентства стоит 250 000 ₽. Курс, который научит вас собрать её самому, — 12 900 ₽». Антипример — «осталось 3 места» при неограниченном наборе: это уже обман, а не подача фактов.
- **Book ref:** Bly explains false logic with the Harry & David pears ("not one person in 1,000 has tasted them," read as rare, not unpopular) and a newsletter priced against a $2,000 managed-account fee.

### [CPH:17] Critique on strategy, not taste  (ch 18)
- **Essence:** The #1 mistake in reviewing copy is editing it to sound the way the reviewer would have written it. Copy should be judged by whether it sells and follows agreed rules — not by personal style. Committee review and vague feedback water copy down; specific, strategy-based critique strengthens it.
- **Copy move:** When reviewing (or defending) copy, replace taste-based notes ("I don't like it," "needs more pizzazz") with specific, factual direction tied to strategy ("lead with the 20%-efficiency claim," "cut features, add the four buyer benefits"). Give rules up front; keep approvers to ≤4 (6 max); apply the 25-50-25 rule — take the ~25% of edits that help and the neutral 50% graciously, and push back only on the ~25% that would hurt results.
- **Applies to:** process — reviewing/approving any copy, incl. self-review.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to every review cycle. The push-back stance assumes an experienced writer and a real strategy; when the draft genuinely misses the brief, the fix is specific redirection, not style-imposition.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — instead of "make it punchier," the reviewer writes "the headline buries the benefit; our tool is the only one with same-day migration — put that up top." Negative — a five-person committee each rewrites a sentence to taste; the final ad has no point of view and pulls half the response.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — вместо «сделай поживее» ревьюер пишет «заголовок прячет выгоду; мы единственные, кто переносит данные за один день, — вынеси это вперёд». Антипример — комитет из пяти человек, каждый переписывает по фразе «на свой вкус»; итоговый текст без позиции даёт вдвое меньше откликов.
- **Book ref:** Bly warns against rewriting a hired writer's strong copy into your own voice, contrasts non-specific vs. specific critiques, caps approvers at four to six, and states the 25-50-25 rule of revisions.

### [CPH:18] Copy leads, design serves  (ch 19)
- **Essence:** In direct response, copy is king and layout's first job is to make that copy easy to read. Serious buyers do read long copy; casual browsers who skip it usually weren't buying. The visual supports, enhances, and explains the words — but the copy generally stands on its own and shouldn't depend on the image.
- **Copy move:** Design so the eye is pulled through headline → subheads → body → CTA; keep type readable for the actual audience. Where a concept needs a visual (travel, fashion, jewelry, demos), specify it — describe it in the copy or supply a "copywriter's rough" showing element positions — rather than hoping a picture carries the sell alone.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form | VSL — copy–design collaboration.
- **Applies / fails when:** Holds for information-rich, considered purchases. Loosens for inherently visual products where the image is much of the value (fashion, food, real estate) — there a strong visual plus a persuasive caption may lead, but words still close.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Technique — a copywriter's rough for a landing page marks headline top-center, benefit bullets left, product shot right, single CTA button below the fold — guiding the designer to serve the reading order. Negative — a beautiful hero image with a two-word tagline and no readable copy; browsers admire it, buyers get no reason to act.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Приём — набросок лендинга размечает: заголовок сверху по центру, буллеты выгод слева, фото продукта справа, одна кнопка CTA — дизайнер выстраивает порядок чтения под текст. Антипример — красивый баннер с фото и слоганом из двух слов без читаемого текста: любуются, но повода купить нет.
- **Book ref:** Bly describes the copywriter's rough for guiding designers and cites designer Lori Haller's first rule — "Copy is KING. ALWAYS." — with readability as the make-or-break of a campaign.
