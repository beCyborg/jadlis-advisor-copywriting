## [CPH:F1] Persuasion-arc formulas  (ch 4)
Every long-form promotion follows one underlying arc: **get attention → build the case → ask for action.** Four named versions, all interchangeable — pick the one whose beats fit your offer. Maps to principles [CPH:03] (attention), [CPH:06] (benefits), [CPH:07] (promise), [CPH:11] (proof), [CPH:12] (CTA).

| Formula | Steps | Best for |
|---------|-------|----------|
| **AIDA** | Attention → Interest → Desire → Action | Default; ads, letters, pages |
| **ACCA** | Awareness → Comprehension → Conviction → Action | New/unfamiliar products needing explanation |
| **4 P's** | Picture → Promise → Prove → Push | Emotional, aspirational offers |
| **Motivating Sequence** (Bly's pick) | Attention → Need → Satisfy → Prove → Ask | Any direct-response piece |

**Motivating Sequence — the working skeleton (Bly's five steps):**
1. **Get attention** — headline + visual lead with the single strongest benefit. Not a clever windup saved for the end.
2. **Show a need** — surface or dramatize the problem the product solves; make the reader feel the need before you pitch.
3. **Satisfy the need / position as the solution** — introduce the product as the answer, fast.
4. **Prove it** — testimonials, comparisons, studies, track record, guarantee ([CPH:11]).
5. **Ask for action** — one clear CTA, made easy, with an incentive to act now ([CPH:12]).

> Note: whichever formula, the sequence is a scaffold, not a straitjacket — don't force-fit; start from the sales message and let the arc organize it.

## [CPH:F2] PAS — Problem · Agitate · Solve  (ch 3–4)
A compact, problem-led variant of the arc — the fastest way to open short copy. Not one of Bly's four named formulas, but it is exactly his "show a need, then satisfy it" logic ([CPH:F1] steps 2–3) compressed for email, ads, and VSL openers.

| Beat | Job | Copy move |
|------|-----|-----------|
| **Problem** | Name the reader's pain in their own words | Lead with the specific problem, not the product ([CPH:09] BDF feelings) |
| **Agitate** | Make the cost of the problem vivid | Twist the knife: what it keeps costing them in time/money/stress if unsolved |
| **Solve** | Present the product as the relief | Bridge straight to benefit + proof + CTA |

- **Few-shot (EN):** *Problem* — "Your best customers are churning and you don't know until the cancel email lands." *Agitate* — "By then it's too late: you've lost the renewal, the referrals, and the reason they left is a mystery." *Solve* — "ChurnScope flags at-risk accounts 30 days early, so you save them while you still can. Start free →"
- **Few-shot (RU):** *Проблема* — «Лучшие клиенты уходят, а вы узнаёте об этом только из письма об отмене». *Нагнетание* — «А значит уже поздно: потеряны и продление, и рекомендации, и причина ухода так и осталась загадкой». *Решение* — «ChurnScope помечает клиентов в зоне риска за 30 дней — вы спасаете их, пока ещё можно. Попробуйте бесплатно →»

## [CPH:F3] The 4 U's scoring pass  (ch 2)
A revision checklist for any headline, subject line, teaser, lead, or bullet. Score 1–4 on each; if not 3–4 on at least three, rewrite. Full principle at [CPH:04].

| U | Question | Fix if weak |
|---|----------|-------------|
| **Urgent** | Reason to act now? | Add a time element / deadline / "this year" |
| **Unique** | New, or said freshly? | Reframe a tired claim from a new angle |
| **Ultra-specific** | Concrete numbers/details? | Replace vague nouns with figures ("free report" → "free 12-page guide to cutting cloud spend 40%") |
| **Useful** | Clear self-interest benefit? | Name the payoff the reader gets |

## [CPH:F4] Sales-letter skeleton  (ch 7)
Long-form direct-mail / email letter. More opening options than an ad (which is locked to headline→visual→lead).

**Mechanics up front:**
- **Personalize or not:** personalize for existing customers and senior executives (higher up the ladder / bigger company = more payoff); form-letter is fine otherwise. Don't over-repeat the name — it reads as insincere.
- **Headline treatment:** optional bold headline (e.g. 16pt Arial Black) above the salutation; center it in a box (a **Johnson Box**) to spotlight it. Or open on the salutation alone, or headline without salutation.
- **Salutation:** identify the reader's interest — "Dear Farmer," "Dear Future Millionaire," "Dear Computer Enthusiast" — always beats "Dear Sir / Dear Friend."

**Body skeleton (runs on the Motivating Sequence [CPH:F1]):**
1. **Lead / first sentence** — the most important line; hook + entice to read on. Pick one of the 15 leads ([CPH:F5]).
2. **Need** — dramatize the problem / build empathy (story leads do this well).
3. **Satisfy** — position the product/offer as the solution.
4. **Proof** — testimonials, track record, guarantee ([CPH:11]).
5. **The offer** — product, price, terms/discount, guarantee, stated plainly.
6. **Call to action** — one clear ask + easy response mechanism (form, phone, URL), with an incentive to respond now ([CPH:12]).
7. **P.S.** *(standard DM convention)* — one of the most-read lines; restate the offer, the deadline, or the guarantee.

- **Length:** governed by emotion × involvement, price, and purpose, not a fixed page count ([CPH:13]). Book-club tests found a 12-page letter outpulled shorter ones for a reading audience.

## [CPH:F5] The 15 sales-letter leads  (ch 7)
Proven openings. Choose by offer strength and audience. All aim to hook attention *and* pull the reader forward.

| # | Lead | When to reach for it |
|---|------|----------------------|
| 1 | **State the offer** | Offer itself is the strongest element (free trial, big discount) |
| 2 | **Highlight the free literature** | Lead-gen; sell the booklet's benefits, not just the product |
| 3 | **Make an announcement** | Genuine news: new product, event, edition |
| 4 | **Tell a story** | Build empathy; problem the reader fears could be theirs |
| 5 | **Flatter the reader** | "You're part of a select group" — owns the list-source fact |
| 6 | **Peer-to-peer** | Narrow interest group; signed by one of their own |
| 7 | **Personal message from the president** | Adds authority + credibility ("he wouldn't sign it if untrue") |
| 8 | **Provocative quote** | A striking third-party line that frames the pitch |
| 9 | **Ask a question** | Question the reader wants answered ([CPH:03] curiosity) |
| 10 | **Make it personal** | Speak to the reader's specific situation |
| 11 | **Identify the reader's problem** | Problem-led (PAS-style [CPH:F2]) |
| 12 | **Stress a benefit** | Lead on the single biggest payoff |
| 13 | **Use human interest** | A relatable human angle draws the reader in |
| 14 | **Inside information** | "Let me let you in on something few know" |
| 15 | **Sweepstakes** | Prize/entry offer as the hook |

- **Few-shot (EN, #11 problem lead):** "If you've ever stared at a blank invoice wondering which client still owes you — and how much — this letter is for you."
- **Few-shot (RU, #4 story lead):** «Три года назад мой знакомый — крепкий сеньор-разработчик — ушёл с найма и открыл своё агентство. Всё шло в гору… пока в один месяц не посыпалось всё сразу. Что он сделал не так — и как этого избежать вам?»

## [CPH:F6] Landing-page skeleton  (ch 12)
One page, one decision: respond or leave. **No site navigation** — remove every link that isn't the CTA. Conversion typically 1–50% depending on ask.

**Assembly, top to bottom:**
1. **Banner + credibility builders (first screen):** logo/company name if known; institutional seal top-left; one to three strong testimonials above the headline; optional pre-head with mission/credentials. Build trust *before* the pitch.
2. **Headline:** arouse curiosity or make a big, compelling promise ([CPH:07]); name the reader's benefit.
3. **Lead:** emotional hook + dramatize the problem; conversational, human tone — "a letter from one person to another," even for technical buyers.
4. **Solution:** show the product as the quickest route to solving *this* problem (not "go browse our site").
5. **Benefit bullets:** many, scannable, **feature—dash—benefit** ("Quick-release adhesive — your graphics peel off clean, never stick together").
6. **Proof block:** lots of testimonials (video with click-to-play, not autoplay; get signed releases), case studies, specifics — facts, figures, diagrams.
7. **Timeliness:** tie to current events/news where relevant; keep copy updated (stale content kills credibility).
8. **Risk reversal:** money-back guarantee (for sales) or "free / no obligation" (for lead-gen), stated clearly.
9. **CTA:** big, bold, colorful button to cart/form; optionally hyperlink keywords in the copy (blue/underlined) and add a toll-free number.
10. **Email capture for non-buyers:** a **floater** (slides in, not blocked like pop-ups) offering a free report/e-course in exchange for the address — so a non-buyer still becomes a lead.

- **Design rule:** graphics exist to make copy *easy to read* — kill low-contrast type or clever layouts that fight legibility ([CPH:18]).

## [CPH:F7] Email (single broadcast) skeleton  (ch 13)
One promotional email. Job #1 is getting opened; job #2 is the click-through. **Short generally beats long** here (unlike classic mail-order) — deep detail lives on the linked page.

**Assembly:**
1. **FROM line** — pick by relationship: *List Owner* (opt-in community/e-zine), *Your Company* (known brand/market leader), *You* (personal / unknown sender), *Team* (e.g. "The [Product] Team" — implies energetic people behind it).
2. **SUBJECT line** — short teaser: curiosity-arousing, attention-grabbing, not blatantly promotional; run the 4 U's ([CPH:F3]). ("FREE" still tends to lift response despite spam-filter risk — test it.)
3. **Killer headline / lead-in sentence** — a terrific benefit up front, like envelope-teaser copy.
4. **First paragraph = mini complete message** — state the offer + give an immediate response link. Skimmers who read only this still get the whole story.
5. **Expanded copy** — features, benefits, proof for readers who need detail.
6. **Close** — repeat the offer + response link (offer/link appear at both top and bottom).
7. **Opt-out** — respectful unsubscribe line to prevent spam complaints.

**Constraints:** ≤3 click-through links (first two get most clicks); ~55–60 characters per line to avoid ugly wraps; go easy on ALL CAPS (reads as shouting); tone helpful/informative/educational, not hard-sell; be specific — "best" and "quality" are empty.

- **Few-shot (EN) subject + lead:** *Subject:* "The pricing mistake quietly capping your MRR" — *Lead:* "Most SaaS founders set one price and forget it. Here's the 3-tier tweak that lifted our reader's revenue 22% in a quarter — full breakdown: [link]"
- **Few-shot (RU) subject + lead:** *Тема:* «Ошибка в тарифах, из-за которой ваша выручка стоит на месте» — *Лид:* «Большинство основателей ставят одну цену и забывают о ней. Вот перестройка на 3 тарифа, которая подняла выручку нашего читателя на 22% за квартал — разбор: [ссылка]»

## [CPH:F8] Email conversion sequence  (ch 13)
The "online conversion" model: free content → captured email → nurture to paid. Turns cold opt-in names (who won't buy from strangers) into buyers.

**Mechanism:**
1. Create/repurpose **free content** (a "free special report," PDF or HTML microsite).
2. **Capture email** — offer the report; reader submits address to download/read. Seed the free report with links to the paid product's landing/transaction page.
3. Send the **conversion series** — **3 to 7 emails**.

**Series structure:**
- **Free-touch emails (first 1–2):** pure goodwill — thank them, reinforce the value of the free content, point out its best ideas, sometimes give *more* free content. No ask.
- **Conversion emails (the rest):** ask for the order; each links to a landing page (heavy copy) or transaction page (thin order form).
- Each email runs its own mini-arc: attention → interest → desire → ask ([CPH:F1]).
- **Always acknowledge in the lead** that this follows up the free report *they requested* — creates mild obligation + a receptive mood.

**Best offer:** a free 30-day trial (card not billed until day 30 = truly free; billed now with refund = "risk-free").

**Sample 5-effort cadence:**
| Day | Email | Type | Job |
|-----|-------|------|-----|
| 1 | #1 | Free-touch | Thank + reinforce the free content's value |
| 2 | #2 | Free-touch | Nudge them to actually read it; highlight best tips |
| 4 | #3 | Conversion | Offer free 30-day trial; sell the product |
| 7 | #4 | Conversion | Remind: your problem is still solvable — take the trial |
| 14 | #5 | Conversion | Trial offer expiring; resell; act today or miss it |

## [CPH:F9] VSL script skeleton  (ch 16)
Video sales letter: 15–45+ min, script ~2,000–7,000 words. Converts better than static pages in testing, but the viewer can't rewind or skim — so it must be linear, clear, and paced. Health/finance are classic verticals. Format the script as a two-column **AV script** (Visual | Audio), ~100–120 words/minute.

**Script arc:**
1. **Pattern-interrupt open** — a statement that breaks the viewer's normal pattern; surprise/shake them ([CPH:03]).
2. **State the problem** — concisely, in the lead.
3. **Reveal the solution within the first minute or two** — wait too long and they click away.
4. **Engaging story** — sweep the listener along (the "velvet slide").
5. **Prove** — insert a chart/graph even for a few seconds; it signals the claim is backed ([CPH:11]).
6. **CTA** — call the number / visit the URL now.

**Craft constraints (tighter than text):**
- Information density ~20% *lower* than a text promo.
- Short words (Bly caps at 9 letters), short sentences, short paragraphs (a couple of sentences) — easier to read on screen.
- ≤2 numbers per sentence; round at least one.
- Tone positive, enthusiastic, *and* authoritative (a voice is reading it aloud).
- Read the draft aloud / record it to check cadence — natural rhythm, not too fast or sleepy.

**AV two-column format (illustrative skeleton — invented):**

| Scene | Visual | Audio |
|-------|--------|-------|
| 1 | Fade in on product logo; soft music | Pattern-interrupt: "If you've been told your slow sales are a traffic problem — you've been lied to." |
| 2 | Presenter in a small office; overwhelmed founder at a laptop | State problem: "Most founders pour money into ads while the real leak is on the page itself." |
| 3 | Screen recording of the fix in action | Solution + story: "Here's exactly what we changed for one reader — and what happened next." |
| 4 | Simple bar chart: conversion before/after | Proof: "Conversions went from 1.8% to 4.3% in six weeks. Same traffic." |
| 5 | Presenter to camera; URL on lower third | CTA: "Go to [URL] now and get the free teardown checklist before your next campaign." |

- **Few-shot (RU) pattern-interrupt open:** «Если вам говорят, что низкие продажи — это про трафик, вам говорят неправду. Настоящая дыра — на самой странице, и сейчас я покажу, где именно.»
