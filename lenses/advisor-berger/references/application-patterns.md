## 1. Cross-STEPPS Combinations

### Pattern: The Full Stack (All 6 STEPPS)

The $100 cheesesteak at Barclay Prime is Berger's "poster child" because it hits all six principles:

| STEPPS | How it applies |
|--------|---------------|
| `[CTG:SC]` Social Currency | A $100 cheesesteak is remarkable — it makes you seem in-the-know for discovering it |
| `[CTG:T]` Triggers | Cheesesteaks are everywhere in Philadelphia — constant environmental reminders |
| `[CTG:E]` Emotion | Surprise and amazement at the concept and price |
| `[CTG:P]` Public | You can see it on the menu, see others ordering it, see media covering it |
| `[CTG:PV]` Practical Value | Useful information about a high-quality steakhouse experience |
| `[CTG:S]` Stories | "You won't BELIEVE what they serve at this steakhouse..." — perfect dinner party story |

**Key Insight:** Not all 6 are required. Think of STEPPS less like a recipe (all ingredients needed) and more like salad toppings — a salad with just cheese and bacon is still delicious. But the more principles, the better.

### Pattern: Social Currency + Triggers (Ignition + Sustain)

`[CTG:SC]` gets people talking initially. `[CTG:T]` keeps them talking over time.

- **Will It Blend?** — Remarkable (SC) but also triggered every time someone uses a blender or sees an iPhone
- **Rebecca Black's "Friday"** — Terrible song is remarkable (SC) but spiked every Friday (T)
- **$100 Cheesesteak** — Remarkable (SC) and triggered by every cheesesteak sighting in Philly (T)

**Application Rule:** If you only have budget for one campaign element, choose between:
- High SC + Low T = viral spike that fades (pirate costume at work)
- Low SC + High T = steady word-of-mouth without a spike (Cheerios, Ziploc bags)
- High SC + High T = viral spike that sustains (Kit Kat + coffee after brand relaunch)

### Pattern: Emotion + Stories (The Sharing Engine)

`[CTG:E]` (high-arousal emotion) + `[CTG:S]` (narrative vessel) = the most powerful sharing combination.

- **Dove "Evolution"** — Anger at beauty industry (E) wrapped in a compelling visual narrative (S). 16M views, double-digit sales growth.
- **Google "Parisian Love"** — Awe and joy (E) told through search queries as a love story (S). Internal hit → public viral success.
- **"United Breaks Guitars"** — Anger (E) channeled into a song/story (S). 3M views in 10 days, United lost $180M in stock value.
- **Susan Boyle** — Awe (E) embedded in an underdog narrative (S). 100M views in 9 days.

**Application Rule:** When designing emotional content, always wrap it in a story. Raw emotion without narrative feels manipulative. Story without emotion feels boring.

### Pattern: Social Currency + Exclusivity + Practical Value (The Premium Engine)

`[CTG:SC.3]` (insider feeling) + `[CTG:PV.3]` (exclusive deals) creates a powerful premium positioning.

- **Rue La La** — Invitation-only (SC.3) + time-limited flash deals (PV.3). Same products as SmartBargains but sold for $350M.
- **Please Don't Tell** — Secret entrance (SC.3) + great cocktails (PV). Most sought-after reservation in NYC.
- **McDonald's McRib** — Limited availability (SC.3) + cult food item (PV). Facebook groups, locator websites, obsessive fans.

**Application Rule:** Restriction amplifies both social currency AND perceived practical value. Limiting access makes people feel special AND makes the offer seem better.

### Pattern: Public + Behavioral Residue + Social Currency (The Self-Perpetuating Loop)

`[CTG:P.2]` (self-advertising) + `[CTG:P.3]` (behavioral residue) + `[CTG:SC]` = products that market themselves indefinitely.

- **Livestrong wristband** — Visible (P), persists after purchase (P.3), signals values (SC). 85M sold.
- **iPod white headphones** — Visible during use (P.2), distinctive vs. competitors. Social proof for adoption.
- **"Sent from my iPhone"** — Visible in every email (P.2), persists as default (P.3), signals tech-savviness (SC).
- **Hotmail signature** — Every email = an ad (P.2). 8.5M users in ~1 year.

**Application Rule:** The most efficient marketing is a product that advertises itself. Design for visibility during use AND persistence after use.

## 2. Virality Mechanics

### Word-of-Mouth: The Primary Driver

**Berger's foundational data points:**
- People share 16,000+ words per day
- Americans mention specific brands 3 billion+ times per day
- Word-of-mouth is the primary factor behind 20-50% of all purchasing decisions
- A word-of-mouth conversation by a new customer → ~$200 increase in restaurant sales
- WOM is at least 10x more effective than traditional advertising
- Customers referred by friends spend more, shop faster, are more profitable

**Why WOM beats advertising:**
1. **More persuasive:** Friends tell it straight. Ads always say their product is the best. Our friends' objectivity and candidness make us trust them more.
2. **More targeted:** We naturally select the right person to share with. We don't tell a non-skier about new skis. This natural targeting means WOM reaches interested audiences without waste.

### Immediate vs. Ongoing Word-of-Mouth

**Immediate WOM:** Sharing soon after encountering something. Driven by:
- `[CTG:SC]` Social Currency — interesting/remarkable things get shared right away
- `[CTG:E]` Emotion — high-arousal events trigger immediate sharing
- Novelty and surprise

**Ongoing WOM:** Sharing weeks or months later. Driven by:
- `[CTG:T]` Triggers — environmental cues that keep reminding people
- `[CTG:PV]` Practical Value — useful information gets shared when relevant situations arise
- Frequency of product usage/encounter

**Critical Finding:** Interesting products received more IMMEDIATE word-of-mouth but did NOT sustain high levels over time. More novel/surprising products didn't get more ongoing buzz. Triggers drove ongoing WOM — more frequently triggered products got 15% more WOM.

**Application Rule:** For products needing sustained buzz (most products), invest in triggers. For products needing launch spikes (movies, events), invest in social currency and emotion.

### The Message vs. The Messenger

**Berger's contrarian position against "influencer theory":**

Malcolm Gladwell's *Tipping Point* argues social epidemics are driven by special people (mavens, connectors, salesmen). Berger disagrees:

- "In most cases [being more persuasive or having more friends] doesn't make them any more influential in spreading information or making things go viral."
- "By focusing so much on the messenger, we've neglected a much more obvious driver of sharing: the message."
- **Analogy:** Some jokes are so funny that it doesn't matter who tells them. Contagious content is like that — "so inherently viral that it spreads regardless of who is doing the talking."
- **Duncan Watts comparison:** Big forest fires aren't caused by big sparks. Lots of individual trees have to catch fire and carry the flames. Contagious products need hundreds/thousands of regular people passing the message along.

**Application Rule:** Instead of spending millions finding "influencers," invest in making the message itself contagious using STEPPS. The right message spreads regardless of the messenger.

### Virality is Made, Not Born

**The Blendtec Proof:**
- Product: a boring household blender
- Budget: $50
- Result: 300M+ views, 700% sales increase

**Key Insight:** "Virality isn't born, it's made." Even regular everyday products can generate massive word-of-mouth if someone figures out the right way to do it. The STEPPS framework provides the recipe.

**Counter-examples of "naturally exciting" products that failed:**
- Not specified, but Berger notes: "most restaurants bomb, most businesses go under, and most social movements fail to gain traction" — proving that being interesting/exciting isn't sufficient.

## 3. Online vs. Offline Sharing

### The 7% Insight

**Berger's most counterintuitive finding:**

> "What percent of word of mouth happens online? If you're like most people you probably guessed 50-60%. The actual number is 7 percent."

- Keller Fay Group research: only 7% of WOM happens online
- People spend ~2 hours/day online but 8x more time offline
- We overestimate online WOM because it's easier to see (tweets, posts leave records)
- Offline conversations are invisible but far more prevalent

### Why Offline WOM Matters

- Face-to-face conversations may be more in-depth
- Offline WOM may be more impactful per conversation
- Average tweet reaches 100+ people but <10% of friends respond to a posted message
- "Online conversations COULD reach a much larger audience, but given that offline conversations may be more in-depth, it's unclear that social media is the better way to go."

### The Technology vs. Strategy Error

**"Facebook and Twitter are technologies, not strategies."**

- WOM marketing works only if people actually talk
- 50% of YouTube videos have fewer than 500 views
- Only 1/3 of 1% get more than 1 million views
- Just putting up a Facebook page or tweeting doesn't mean anyone will notice or spread the word

**Application Rule:**
1. Don't ignore offline WOM — it's 93% of the total
2. Online tools are amplifiers, not strategies
3. Design for shareability (STEPPS), then choose channels
4. Consider where the desired action takes place: online product → online WOM great; offline product → offline WOM may be more powerful

### When Online WOM is Best

- Product/action is online (websites, apps, digital services)
- Target audience does research online before offline purchase (cars — most research is online even though purchase is at dealer)
- Need to reach geographically dispersed audience quickly
- Content is visual/video (hard to share a video face-to-face)

### When Offline WOM is Better

- Product is consumed offline (restaurants, retail, services)
- Need depth of recommendation (complex purchase decisions)
- Target audience is local (neighborhood business)
- Trust is critical (medical recommendations, financial advice)

## 4. Practical Application Frameworks

### The Contagious Content Checklist

Berger's own checklist from the Epilogue, with citation tags:

| Principle | Diagnostic Questions |
|-----------|---------------------|
| `[CTG:SC]` Social Currency | Does talking about your product/idea make people look good? Can you find inner remarkability? Leverage game mechanics? Make people feel like insiders? |
| `[CTG:T]` Triggers | Consider the context. What cues make people think about your product/idea? How can you grow the habitat and make it come to mind more often? |
| `[CTG:E]` Emotion | Focus on feelings. Does talking about your product/idea generate emotion? How can you kindle the fire? |
| `[CTG:P]` Public | Does your product/idea advertise itself? Can people see when others are using it? If not, how can you make the private public? Can you create behavioral residue that sticks around even after people use it? |
| `[CTG:PV]` Practical Value | Does talking about your product/idea help people help others? How can you highlight incredible value, packaging your knowledge and expertise into useful information others will want to disseminate? |
| `[CTG:S]` Stories | What is your Trojan Horse? Is your product/idea embedded in a broader narrative that people want to share? Is the story not only viral, but also valuable? |

### The Valuable Virality Test

Before launching any viral campaign, apply these checks:

1. **The Detachability Test `[CTG:S.2]`:** Can someone retell the story without mentioning your brand? If YES → redesign.
2. **The Telephone Test `[CTG:S.2]`:** After 5 retellings, will the brand survive? 70% of details are lost — only critical plot elements persist.
3. **The Arousal Check `[CTG:E.1]`:** Does the content evoke HIGH-arousal emotions (awe, excitement, anger, anxiety, humor)? Or LOW-arousal (sadness, contentment)? Only high-arousal drives sharing.
4. **The Trigger Check `[CTG:T]`:** What environmental cue will remind people of this content after the initial buzz fades?
5. **The Social Currency Check `[CTG:SC]`:** Does sharing this make the SHARER look good?

### Anti-Patterns (What NOT to Do)

| Anti-Pattern | Problem | Fix | Tag |
|-------------|---------|-----|-----|
| Empty Trojan Horse | Viral content with no brand integration | Make brand integral to story | `[CTG:S.2]` |
| Interesting but untriggered | Initial buzz that fades | Link to frequent environmental cue | `[CTG:T.1]` |
| Sad content | Evoking low-arousal negative emotion | Switch to anger, anxiety, or reframe positively | `[CTG:E.1]` |
| Publicizing bad behavior | Anti-drug ads that normalize drug use | Highlight positive norms instead | `[CTG:P.4]` |
| Perpetual sales | Always-on discounts that lose meaning | Use time/quantity/access restrictions | `[CTG:PV.3]` |
| Influencer obsession | Spending on messengers instead of messages | Make message itself contagious via STEPPS | All |
| Feature-focused messaging | Information without feeling | Apply "Three Whys" to find emotional core | `[CTG:E.4]` |
| Confusing game mechanics | Too many badges/levels nobody understands | Use intuitive systems (gold/silver/bronze) | `[CTG:SC.2]` |
| Monetary incentives for sharing | Paying people crowds out intrinsic motivation | Use social incentives (social currency) instead | `[CTG:SC.2]` |
| Wrong-time triggers | Message at health center, drinking at frat house | Place trigger near point of desired action | `[CTG:T.5]` |

| Domain | Primary STEPPS | Key Example from Book | Key Technique |

## 6. STEPPS vs. Other Frameworks

### STEPPS vs. Made to Stick (SUCCESs)

Berger acknowledges Chip and Dan Heath's *Made to Stick* (Chip Heath was his mentor). Key difference:

- **Made to Stick** focuses on making ideas STICK — getting people to remember them (Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories)
- **Contagious** focuses on making ideas SPREAD — getting people to pass them on

These are complementary but distinct goals. A message can stick (be memorable) without spreading (being shared). And a message can spread temporarily without sticking in memory.

### STEPPS vs. The Tipping Point

Berger explicitly positions against Gladwell's influencer-focused model:

- **Gladwell:** Social epidemics are driven by special people (mavens, connectors, salesmen)
- **Berger:** Social epidemics are driven by the characteristics of the message/product itself
- **Evidence:** "Right now there is little good empirical evidence that people who have more social ties or who are more persuasive have a bigger impact on what catches on" (notes section)

### STEPPS and Cialdini's Principles

Several STEPPS map directly to Cialdini's influence principles:

| STEPPS | Cialdini Parallel | Distinction |
|--------|------------------|-------------|
| `[CTG:P]` Public | `[INF:SP]` Social Proof | Both about observability and imitation. Berger adds behavioral residue and self-advertising. |
| `[CTG:SC.3]` Insiders | `[INF:SC]` Scarcity | Both about limited availability. Berger focuses on WOM effect; Cialdini on compliance. |
| `[CTG:SC.2]` Game Mechanics | `[INF:CC]` Commitment/Consistency | Progress markers and goals. Berger focuses on social comparison; Cialdini on internal consistency. |
| `[CTG:E]` Emotion | (no direct parallel) | Berger's arousal model is unique to virality literature. |
| `[CTG:T]` Triggers | (no direct parallel) | Environmental cuing for top-of-mind. Uniquely Berger. |

**Application Rule:** Use Cialdini `[INF:*]` for one-on-one persuasion and compliance. Use Berger `[CTG:*]` for mass word-of-mouth and virality. They complement each other.
