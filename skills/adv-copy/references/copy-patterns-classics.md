# Classic copywriting layer (adv-copy)

Distilled operational patterns from three foundational direct-response books. These classics have no standalone advisor — this file is the shared reference the adv-copy layer consults when a decision turns on time-tested copy craft (headlines, offer, specificity, structure, action). Three sections, one per book, each with its own prefix and Principle index.

- **[SCIAD]** *Scientific Advertising* — Claude Hopkins (1923)
- **[TAM]** *Tested Advertising Methods* — John Caples (4th ed., 1974)
- **[USL]** *The Ultimate Sales Letter* — Dan Kennedy (rev. ed.)

Codes are stable, uppercase, and unique within a prefix. Cite them from copy critiques and drafts.

---

# Scientific Advertising — Claude Hopkins  ·  prefix [SCIAD]
> Advertising is salesmanship multiplied, and every dollar of it must be traceable to a sale. Use this lens when a copy choice can be settled by "what would a good salesman do?" and by measured results rather than taste.

## Principles

### [SCIAD:01] Salesmanship in print  (ch 2)
- **Essence:** An advertisement is a salesman speaking to thousands at once. Its only job is to make sales, not to build "general effect" or keep a name in view; because it costs far more than one salesman's pitch, its mistakes cost proportionally more.
- **Copy move:** Test every line against "would this help a live salesman close a half-sold buyer standing in front of me?" Write to one imagined individual, not the mass; strip fine writing, clever style, and anything that draws attention to the selling effort itself.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email | VSL — universal frame.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies whenever the goal is a sale or measurable action. Weaker frame for pure brand/awareness plays where no direct response is expected — but Hopkins would argue those are usually waste.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Add this to your morning coffee and your afternoon crash disappears — here's the 30-second routine." (a salesman's plain benefit). Bad — "Reimagine your relationship with wakefulness." (writer performing, not selling).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Добавьте одну ложку в утренний кофе — и дневной упадок сил не наступит. Вот как это работает за 30 секунд». Плохо — «Переосмыслите свои отношения с бодростью» (автор рисуется, а не продаёт).
- **Book ref:** Hopkins argues literary polish repels buyers the way a smooth-talking salesman inspires fear of being over-influenced; the ablest ad men he knew were former door-to-door canvassers.

### [SCIAD:02] Traced returns, test everything  (ch 1,15)
- **Essence:** Advertising becomes a science only when results are keyed and traced to the fraction of a cent. Opinions and table arguments are worthless next to a small test in a few towns; a thousand customers reliably predict a million.
- **Copy move:** Run competing versions with keyed coupons/links; decide by cost per converted customer, not clicks or applause. Before scaling, prove the offer pays in a limited test; keep iterating plans to drive cost down (Hopkins cut one campaign's selling cost 75% over five years).
- **Applies to:** landing | email | short-form — anywhere a response can be measured.
- **Applies / fails when:** Requires a measurable response channel. Fails for untrackable media; there, borrow proven patterns instead of guessing.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — ship headline A vs B to 5% of the list, keep the one with lower cost-per-order. Bad — "the team voted and we all like version B" (taste, not traced return).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — два заголовка на 5% базы, оставить тот, где ниже стоимость заказа. Плохо — «всем в отделе больше нравится второй вариант» (вкус вместо замера).
- **Book ref:** He offered to prove one long-time advertiser's spend was worthless by ending an ad with a $5 reward to anyone who read it through — betting the scarcity of replies would shock him.

### [SCIAD:03] Mail order is the laboratory  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** Mail-order copy is refined by pure survival — every element already survived head-to-head tests — so it is the best available model for all copy. Its verdicts: small type, no wasted space, a reminder coupon, purposeful pictures, and complete copy ("the more you tell, the more you sell").
- **Copy move:** When unsure, imitate long-running mail-order/direct-response copy rather than pretty brand ads. Assume long copy outsells short when the space is genuinely used to sell; a 2x ad should bring ~2x returns only if the extra room is worked, not padded.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email — direct-response heavy.
- **Applies / fails when:** Strong for considered purchases needing a full argument. "More you tell" fails when the extra copy is filler; length only pays when every added line sells.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — a long-form sales page that answers every objection in sequence and ends in a coupon. Bad — a full-page image with a logo and one clever tagline, no argument.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — длинный лендинг, закрывающий возражения по порядку и заканчивающийся оффером с дедлайном. Плохо — экран с красивой картинкой, логотипом и одним слоганом без аргументов.
- **Book ref:** An incubator seller added a decorative row of silhouetted chickens, grew the ad 50%, and raised cost-per-reply by exactly 50% with zero extra sales — buyers wanted the offer, not the picture.

### [SCIAD:04] Offer service, not self  (ch 3)
- **Essence:** People are selfish and care nothing for your profit or brand name; the best ads never say "buy my brand" and often omit price. They win by picturing the customer's benefit until buying is the natural result, frequently via a gift or free trial that triggers reciprocity.
- **Copy move:** Lead with what the reader gets, not what you want. Replace "choose us / buy now" pushes with an offer of information, samples, or trial framed as service. Never "drive people to stores" — coax, don't command.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email — all.
- **Applies / fails when:** Universal. The only failure mode is a genuinely uninteresting benefit — then no framing saves it (see [SCIAD:10]).
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Send for the free sample — try it a week before you decide anything." Bad — "We're the #1 brand, give us your business." (asking, not serving).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Закажите бесплатный пробник — попробуйте неделю, потом решайте». Плохо — «Мы бренд №1, покупайте у нас» (просьба вместо пользы).
- **Book ref:** Door-to-door brush sellers never asked housewives to buy — they said "I was sent to give you a brush, pick one," and the wish to reciprocate produced the order.

### [SCIAD:05] Headlines select the reader  (ch 5)
- **Essence:** A headline's purpose is to flag down exactly the people you can interest and let everyone else pass — like calling a name across a crowd. Changing only the headline routinely multiplies returns five- or tenfold, because the whole return depends on attracting the right readers.
- **Copy move:** Write the headline to name the audience and their strongest desired benefit, not a blind/clever teaser that pulls the wrong crowd. Spend disproportionate time here (Hopkins spent hours per headline, discarding scores); run many headline variants across placements to catch different appeals.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email (subject line) — headline is king everywhere.
- **Applies / fails when:** Always applies. Clever "blind" headlines fail when they attract volume that can't buy and hide the offer from those who could.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "For sciatica sufferers: the 6-minute stretch that lets you sit through a movie again." (selects the exact buyer). Bad — "Change your life today." (selects nobody in particular).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Для тех, у кого болит поясница: 6-минутная растяжка, чтобы снова досидеть до конца фильма». Плохо — «Измени свою жизнь сегодня» (не выбирает никого конкретно).
- **Book ref:** The same soap ad titled about "beauty/complexion" outpulled "Keep Clean" or "No animal fat" many times over; a car body called "The Sportiest of Sport Bodies" beat a "good universal joint" headline roughly fifty to one.

### [SCIAD:06] Applied psychology  (ch 6)
- **Essence:** Fixed traits of human nature can be used deliberately: curiosity pulls readers in; people judge quality by price (high price can signal, cheapness repels); ownership and reciprocity move people; a paid-for sample earns more respect than a "free" one; offers limited to a class of people pull harder.
- **Copy move:** Weaponize curiosity in headlines; state impressive costs/figures to borrow authority ("we paid $100,000 for this formula"); prefer "pay later if you like it" over "money back"; give owned objects ("your name in gilt"); restrict offers to an identifiable group so they act to keep an advantage.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email — all.
- **Applies / fails when:** Powerful when the mechanism fits the product truthfully. Backfires if the price signal, scarcity, or "cost" claim is transparently manufactured.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Only for members: your name embossed on the cover, free — claim the copy already reserved for you." Bad — "Limited offer!!!" with no real limit or reason (empty scarcity).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Только для участников клуба: экземпляр с вашим именем на обложке уже зарезервирован — заберите». Плохо — «Ограниченное предложение!!!» без реального ограничения (пустой дефицит).
- **Book ref:** Offering books with the buyer's name in gilt lettering, with almost no other change, sold hundreds of thousands; and buyers picked the horse whose seller said "try it, then pay me" over the identical "money-back" seller.

### [SCIAD:07] Be specific  (ch 7)
- **Essence:** Generalities and superlatives ("best in the world," "lowest prices") roll off unread and even breed distrust, because everyone expects sellers to exaggerate. A precise, checkable claim is taken at face value and carries its full weight.
- **Copy move:** Replace every vague claim with an exact figure or fact. "More light" becomes "3⅓ times the light"; "reduced prices" becomes "reduced 25%"; "we tested it" becomes "the result of testing 130 formulas."
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email — all.
- **Applies / fails when:** Always applies; the sharper the number, the better. Fails only if the specific is unverifiable or invites an easy disproof — then it becomes a boomerang.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Reduces load time from 4.1s to 0.9s on the median store." Bad — "Blazing-fast performance you'll love." (superlative, discounted on sight).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Сокращает загрузку с 4,1 до 0,9 секунды на среднем магазине». Плохо — «Молниеносная скорость, которая вам понравится» (превосходная степень, которую сразу обесценивают).
- **Book ref:** A new shaving soap broke into a crowded field with "softens the beard in one minute" and "stays creamy for ten minutes on the face" — specifics where rivals cried "abundant lather."

### [SCIAD:08] Tell the full story  (ch 8)
- **Essence:** You get one reading from a prospect who may never return, so an ad must present every persuasive claim at once — not ration them across a serial. Length is set by the sale's difficulty, but the story should always be reasonably complete.
- **Copy move:** Stack all your tested, appealing claims into every ad; don't split arguments across issues or "continue next week." Write to the unconverted new reader (current users won't read it), and give enough to produce action in this single encounter.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email — long-form direct response.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to considered purchases and habit-switching. For trivial, self-evident products (chewing gum) a sentence completes the story — completeness, not length, is the rule.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — a page that stacks benefit, proof, objection-handling, guarantee, and offer in one flow. Bad — "Part 1 of 3: why our mattress is different (more next week)." (serial that never reconnects).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — страница, где выгода, доказательство, работа с возражениями, гарантия и оффер идут одним потоком. Плохо — «Часть 1 из 3: чем наш матрас отличается (продолжение через неделю)» (серия, которая не соберётся).
- **Book ref:** A buyer ready to spend big on a car chose the Marmon because it "told a complete story" in columns of copy, while Rolls-Royce and Pierce-Arrow ran short, dignified ads that argued nothing.

### [SCIAD:09] Samples and the free offer  (ch 13)
- **Essence:** The product is its own best salesman, so sampling is usually the cheapest selling method: it unlocks the word "Free" (which multiplies readership), turns the coupon into a commitment device, and captures an interested prospect's name for follow-up.
- **Copy move:** Offer a genuine free sample/trial and let readers cut a coupon or click to claim it — the act converts a passive reader into a captured, half-sold lead. Deliver samples where they're easiest to claim (local pickup/telephone beat "mail this in"); don't charge even a dime for the sample — it kills "Free" and slashes response.
- **Applies to:** landing | email — lead-gen and trial offers.
- **Applies / fails when:** Strong when trial reliably converts and the sample cost is recoverable. Fails when the product's benefit isn't felt in one trial, or samples leak to non-buyers ("one to a home," "adults only").
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Claim your free 7-day trial — no card, cancel anytime; we'll email your login now." Bad — "Sample available for $2 + a stamp." (a fee that erases 'free' and three-quarters of replies).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Заберите бесплатные 7 дней — без карты, отмена в любой момент; доступ вышлем сразу». Плохо — «Пробник за 100 ₽ и марку» (плата убивает слово «бесплатно» и большую часть откликов).
- **Book ref:** A sewing-machine-motor maker stopped soliciting purchases and offered a free week's home trial with a helper — an offer so resistless that about nine in ten trials became sales.

### [SCIAD:10] Cost-of-conversion economics  (ch 10)
- **Essence:** Some jobs are simply too costly to attempt in copy — changing entrenched habits, teaching a category, or selling "prevention" (people cure trouble but won't act to prevent it). Choosing the wrong appeal can multiply cost-per-sale tenfold even when a strong appeal sits right beside it.
- **Copy move:** Refuse to spend copy educating people into a habit or arguing prevention; pick the appeal tests show is strongest and feature it. A toothpaste sells on "whiter teeth," not "prevents decay"; a soap on "complexion," not "cures eczema" — and mentioning the weak claim can even poison the strong one.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form — positioning of the core appeal.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies when multiple appeals compete for the headline. The boundary: a niche appeal too weak to lead may still earn a place in a fraction of ads or lower in the body.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — lead a vitamin with "noticeably more energy by week two," not "supports long-term cellular health." Bad — headline a mouthwash on "prevents future gum disease" (prevention, low pull) over "fresh breath that lasts."
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — витамины продавать через «заметно больше энергии ко второй неделе», а не «поддержка клеточного здоровья». Плохо — ополаскиватель под заголовком «профилактика болезней дёсен» вместо «свежее дыхание надолго» (профилактика слабо тянет).
- **Book ref:** Educating people to the toothbrush habit was measured at $20–25 per convert — unthinkable — so the disciplined advertiser spent not one line on it and sold on beauty instead.

### [SCIAD:11] Do your homework  (ch 11)
- **Essence:** Behind a simple-looking ad lies weeks of research; a single fact unearthed from a thousand can become the campaign's keynote. Knowing total consumption, spend per user, and the exact percentage of readers who could buy is what keeps cost under control.
- **Copy move:** Before writing, research the product and canvass real consumers (interviews, questionnaires) to find the one differentiating fact and the true buying situation. Verify claims (chemists, authorities) so a strong assertion doesn't become a boomerang; convert vague strengths into exact numbers you can defend.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form | VSL — pre-writing discovery.
- **Applies / fails when:** Always worth doing for a serious campaign. Diminishing returns only for trivial, low-stakes copy.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — canvassing revealed 96% baked beans at home, so the campaign sold "stop baking beans" instead of "buy my brand" (which reached only 4%). Bad — writing a beans ad on "buy my brand" without ever asking how people actually eat beans.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — опрос показал, что 96% пекут фасоль дома, и кампания продавала «перестаньте возиться с духовкой», а не «купите наш бренд». Плохо — писать рекламу «покупайте наш бренд», не выяснив, как люди вообще едят этот продукт.
- **Book ref:** To advertise caffeine-free coffee, weeks of medical reading yielded one fact — caffeine's kick arrives two hours later — so removing it doesn't remove coffee's immediate lift; that single fact reframed the whole campaign.

### [SCIAD:12] Strategy: name, price, position  (ch 12,20)
- **Essence:** Foundational choices — the product name, the price tier, and defenses against substitution — often decide success before copy is written. A descriptive name (Cream of Wheat) is a free ad; a coined name (Kodak) must be given meaning but stays exclusive; a name built on a shared ingredient invites imitators.
- **Copy move:** Prefer a name that tells the story or a defensible coined name; avoid names rivals can share (this is a positioning move — see also adv-influence positioning). Set price to strategy: low price for volume plays, high price where the category is judged by price or margin is needed on low-repeat items; anticipate substitution and build the defense in from the start.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form — naming, pricing, positioning frame.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies at product/offer design time. Less relevant when name and price are fixed and only the copy is in scope.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "OvernightOats" as a self-describing product name a competitor can't own the way "Toasted Corn Flakes" was copied. Bad — pioneering a category under a generic ingredient name every rival can also use.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — самоописывающее имя, которое конкурент не сможет присвоить, вместо родового «овсяные хлопья». Плохо — открыть категорию под названием-ингредиентом, которым потом пользуются все конкуренты.
- **Book ref:** Vaseline nearly monopolized its new demand by choosing an exclusive coined name, while Toasted Corn Flakes and Malted Milk pioneered demand under names rivals could freely share and lost much of the harvest.

### [SCIAD:13] Individuality and persona  (ch 17)
- **Essence:** To be remembered, copy must stand out through admirable difference in manner and tone — a becoming, consistent personality — not through eccentricity. Signing ads gives a human authority; changing the voice forces even loyal readers to "get acquainted all over."
- **Copy move:** Give the brand/author one distinctive, fitting voice (rugged-honest, expert, warm) and never break it; sign copy with a real person where possible so a proud maker, not a "soulless corporation," is speaking. Onboard every new writer into that established persona before they touch the copy.
- **Applies to:** email | landing | short-form — brand voice consistency.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to any ongoing relationship (newsletters, brands). Less binding for a one-off ad with no continuity, though a fitting tone still helps.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — a founder-signed newsletter in the same plain, opinionated voice every week. Bad — copy that is folksy one week, corporate the next, edgy the third — no one recognizes the sender.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — рассылка за подписью основателя, из недели в неделю в одном узнаваемом голосе. Плохо — текст то по-свойски, то канцелярски, то дерзко — отправителя никто не узнаёт.
- **Book ref:** Two next-door installment-clothing sellers targeted poor girls; the one run in the name of a warm, motherly signed persona who "knew what it meant" drove the bare-business rival out.

### [SCIAD:14] Positive over negative  (ch 18)
- **Essence:** Show the bright, attractive side — beauty, health, success — not the ugly problem; a positive ad can outpull a negative one four to one. Attacking rivals is never good policy: the selfish purpose shows and it reads as unsporting.
- **Copy move:** Picture the after, not the before (well-dressed people, pretty teeth, the envied outcome); assume the reader will act ("Send now for this sample") rather than scold ("Why do you neglect this?"). Never knock competitors; invite comparison instead if you're confident.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email — all.
- **Applies / fails when:** Broadly applies. Problem-agitation still has a place (the reader must feel the pain), but the resolution and imagery should point to the bright side, and the ask should presume action.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Picture yourself finishing the 5K feeling strong — here's the 8-week plan." Bad — "Tired of being the slowest, most out-of-shape person on the track?" (dwelling on the gloomy side).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Представьте, как легко добегаете свои первые 5 км — вот план на 8 недель». Плохо — «Устали быть самым медленным и неспортивным на дорожке?» (упор на мрачную сторону).
- **Book ref:** Rather than "look out for substitutes," one advertiser wrote "try our rivals' too" in his headline — inviting comparison corrected the substitution problem that defensive, selfish warnings never could.

### [SCIAD:15] Get action now  (ch 19)
- **Essence:** Most readers who decide to act forget within minutes, so copy must convert interest into action immediately. A reminder to cut/click, a dated deadline, and an inducement for promptness all fight the tendency to delay.
- **Copy move:** End with a device that captures the decision on the spot — a coupon/button, an offer that expires on a date, a gift for ordering now. Follow up inquiries while interest is fresh, and keep follow-up copy on the same argument that created the interest.
- **Applies to:** email | landing | short-form — the close.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to every response ask. Fake urgency erodes trust ([SCIAD:06]) — the deadline or inducement must be real.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Reserve your seat before Friday — the 20% early-bird rate ends when the timer hits zero." Bad — a great pitch that ends with "…so, whenever you're ready, feel free to reach out sometime." (no capture, no urgency).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Забронируйте место до пятницы — скидка 20% сгорает, когда таймер дойдёт до нуля». Плохо — сильный питч, заканчивающийся на «…в общем, когда будете готовы, как-нибудь напишите» (ни фиксации решения, ни срочности).
- **Book ref:** A catalog mailer enclosed a personal card promising a gift if the buyer ordered from him rather than a competing catalog — a concrete reason to act now that paid for itself in larger orders.

## Principles

### [TAM:01] The headline carries the ad  (ch 2)
- **Essence:** The headline is 50–75% of an advertisement's whole effect: it competes with hundreds of other messages in a one-second glance, and far more people read headlines than read copy. If the headline fails to stop the right reader, the best copy in the world never gets read.
- **Copy move:** Spend disproportionate time on the headline (hours, even days); once it's right the copy nearly writes itself. Make the promise concrete enough that the reader can picture it happening to him — small, believable numbers beat huge ones.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email (subject) — everywhere there's a headline.
- **Applies / fails when:** Always applies. A striking illustration can occasionally rescue an ordinary headline, but never bet on it.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "How I sold my first freelance article for $140 a month after this course." (imaginable, specific). Bad — "How I earn $250,000 a year as a writer." (true but too big to imagine — nobody projects themselves into it).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Как я продал первую статью на фрилансе за 12 000 ₽ через месяц после курса» (представимо, конкретно). Плохо — «Как я зарабатываю 20 млн в год копирайтингом» (слишком крупно, чтобы читатель примерил на себя).
- **Book ref:** Changing a correspondence-school headline from "made $25,000 the first year" to "sold his first scenario for $140, one month after this course" drew enormously — readers could imagine the $140, not the $25,000.

### [TAM:02] Four reader-getting qualities  (ch 3)
- **Essence:** Keyed results show the strongest headlines share four qualities, in order of power: self-interest (offer something wanted), news, curiosity, and a hint of a quick/easy way — all constrained by believability. Curiosity alone is weakest: for every curiosity headline that works, a dozen fail.
- **Copy move:** Build the headline on the reader's self-interest first; layer in news and, if it fits, curiosity — but never let a curiosity teaser hide the offer from headline-only scanners. Keep it believable with specific figures ("in one evening," "in 2 years") so "quick and easy" doesn't tip into unbelievable.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email (subject) — headline construction.
- **Applies / fails when:** Broadly applies. A pure-curiosity headline can win occasionally ("What's wrong in this picture?") but is a high-variance bet; default to self-interest.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Retire at 55: the savings plan that quietly beats your 401(k)." (self-interest + news). Bad — "The secret we can't quite tell you yet…" (naked curiosity, hides the offer, pulls the wrong crowd).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «На пенсию в 55: план накоплений, который тихо обгоняет вклад в банке» (личный интерес + новость). Плохо — «Секрет, который мы пока не можем раскрыть…» (голое любопытство, прячет оффер, тянет не тех).  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
- **Book ref:** "You can laugh at money worries if you follow this simple plan" pulled twice the inquiries and sales of the curiosity headline "Here's one question you shouldn't ask your wife" — the self-interest version even sells to headline-only readers.

### [TAM:03] Why headlines fail  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** Headlines fail when they are obscure, "clever," blind (meaningless without the copy), negative, or aimed at the wrong crowd. The public reads the headline first and only reads copy if hooked — so a headline that needs the copy to be understood is already dead, however much award juries admire it.
- **Copy move:** Never judge a headline by how nicely it "ties up" with the copy; judge it cold, as a scanner would. Cut wordplay, riddles, and gloomy framings; make sure the large print alone conveys a wanted message and selects the buyer. Distrust what advertising juries praise — they read the copy first and reward fine writing over sales.
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email (subject).
- **Applies / fails when:** Always applies. The rare exception is a brand with enough equity that a mood headline still gets read — not most work.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Corn gone in 5 days or your money back." (self-explanatory, selects sufferers). Bad — "Tireless Wings" for an airline (poetic, blind, offers and selects nothing).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Мозоль сойдёт за 5 дней или вернём деньги» (само за себя, выбирает страдающих). Плохо — «Крылья без усталости» для авиакомпании (красиво, но пусто — ничего не обещает и никого не выбирает).
- **Book ref:** An award-winning headline, "Kill my cow for an editor? I should say not!", left ordinary readers baffled about its meaning — the misreading was the headline's fault, not the reader's.

### [TAM:04] 29 headline formulas  (ch 5)
- **Essence:** Reliable headline patterns exist and recur because human wants don't change. Use them as springboards (not crutches) when you need a headline fast or want to force the copy into a useful groove.
- **Copy move:** Run the product through this checklist; several openings force good copy (a "How to" headline compels how-to copy). Grouped by function:
  - **News:** 1 Announcing… · 2 announcement words (Introducing/Presenting/Now available) · 3 New… · 4 Now… · 5 At last… · 6 date in headline · 7 news style ("Discovered—…").
  - **Price/offer:** 8 feature the price · 9 feature reduced price · 10 special merchandising offer · 11 easy-payment plan · 12 free offer.
  - **Value/story:** 13 offer information of value · 14 tell a story ("They laughed when…").
  - **Key words:** 15 How to… · 16 How… · 17 Why… · 18 Which…/"Do you have these…" · 19 Who else wants… · 20 Wanted… · 21 This… · 22 Advice to…
  - **Devices:** 23 testimonial style · 24 offer a test ("Can your scalp pass…") · 25 one-word · 26 two-word · 27 warn to delay buying ("Buy no… until…") · 28 advertiser speaks directly · 29 address a specific person/group ("To the man who is 35 and dissatisfied").
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email (subject) — headline generation.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies as an ideation tool. Fails if used as a crutch that produces a formula-shaped headline with no real self-interest behind it ([TAM:02]).
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — apply #29 + #15: "To founders drowning in support tickets: how to cut your reply time 70% this week." Bad — apply #1 with nothing behind it: "Announcing… our updated brand guidelines." (news flavor, zero reader benefit).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — формулы #29 + #15: «Основателям, тонущим в тикетах поддержки: как за неделю сократить время ответа на 70%». Плохо — формула #1 без сути: «Представляем… наш обновлённый брендбук» (новостной привкус, ноль пользы читателю).
- **Book ref:** "They laughed when I sat down at the piano — but when I started to play" (formula 14, tell a story) became one of the most famous ads ever, selling a mail-order music course.

### [TAM:05] Find the right appeal  (ch 6)
- **Essence:** The appeal — the reason you give the reader to buy — is the single most important element, and it lives in the headline. What you say matters far more than how you say it: the right appeal can outsell the wrong one many times over even in identical space, and appeals that "sound good" in a conference room routinely flop.
- **Copy move:** Test several appeals, find the winner by keyed results, then feature that winner in every headline and cash in on it across all media. Rank your drafts by replies and study what the winners share. Prefer plain, basic, self-interested appeals over clever ones; specific beats general every time.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form | email — the strategic core of any campaign.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies whenever multiple angles compete. The method needs a measurable channel to pick the winner; without one, borrow the appeal that proven ads in the category already use.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — after testing, a business school leads every ad with the winning "makes you more money," with named before/after incomes. Bad — a beautifully written "Letters wives don't write to their unsuccessful husbands" that admen adored and that sold nothing.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — после тестов бизнес-школа во всех объявлениях ведёт с победившим «принесёт больше денег», с конкретными «до/после» по доходам. Плохо — изящное «Письма, которые жёны не пишут неудачливым мужьям», которым восхищались копирайтеры и которое ничего не продало.
- **Book ref:** Caples ranked a year's coupon returns and found every ad in the successful group shared one appeal expressed in the headline, while the failures had it only in the body or not at all; one mail-order appeal outsold another 19½ to 1 in the same space.

### [TAM:06] Test, don't guess  (ch 1,7,18)
- **Essence:** Advertising divides into those who test and those who guess; opinions (even experts') are wrong about half the time versus actual sales. Mail-order and department-store copy is trustworthy precisely because it must sell or die, and the key number is to advertising what the X-ray is to medicine.
- **Copy move:** Inject a response mechanism (coupon, hidden offer, reply link) so every ad can be keyed; run split-runs to isolate one variable; keep the offer identical and equally prominent across versions so the test is clean. Trust that inquiries generally predict sales, and prune the losing ads down to a schedule of proven payers.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | short-form — anywhere response is measurable.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to direct-response. Watch the exception: a "Send for this free booklet" giveaway can inflate low-grade leads, so weigh inquiries by quality, not count alone.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — same page, same day, split-run two headlines with an identical free-sample offer; keep the one with more (qualified) replies. Bad — "the CMO likes the sailing-ship visual from an old Christmas card" — a pet idea with no test behind it.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — одна страница, один день, split-run двух заголовков с одинаковым оффером пробника; оставить тот, где больше (целевых) откликов. Плохо — «директору по маркетингу нравится парусник со старой открытки» — любимая идея без единого теста.
- **Book ref:** For a soft drink, centering everything on coupon cost and proven ads dropped the cost per coupon from $120 to 24¢ over three years, and net earnings rose from about $225K to about $890K — the schedule pruned to 15 tested ads.

### [TAM:07] Put in enthusiasm  (ch 8)
- **Essence:** Enthusiasm is as contagious in print as in person — it spreads from writer to reader. The hardest part of an ad is the start because the writer's brain is "cold"; you must first sell yourself on the product, then write hot and fast, and edit cold afterward.
- **Copy move:** Before writing, talk yourself into genuine conviction; write to one friend ("Jim would want to know about this"), not a mass. Write fast and furiously to capture emotion, then in cold blood cut what the critics will object to — you can tame a wild draft but you can't animate a dead one. Combine emotional copy (moves to action) with reason-why copy (wins agreement).
- **Applies to:** email | landing | short-form — the drafting method and tone.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to persuasive copy generally. Enthusiasm without substance is hype; pair conviction with the specific facts of [TAM:09]/[SCIAD:07].
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Right now these pears are hanging like pendants on 40-year-old trees, and I envy you your first taste — every spoonful dripping with sweet liquid sunshine." (felt excitement). Bad — "Our produce meets rigorous quality standards for freshness and flavor." (accurate, dead).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Прямо сейчас эти груши висят на 40-летних деревьях, и я завидую вашему первому укусу — каждая ложка сочится сладким солнечным соком» (живой азарт). Плохо — «Наша продукция соответствует строгим стандартам качества по свежести и вкусу» (точно и мертво).
- **Book ref:** A long, enthusiastic mail-order ad for Royal Riviera pears — written in the heat of the author's own excitement — pulled so well it ran for years and launched the whole business of selling fruit by mail.

### [TAM:08] The first paragraph  (ch 9)
- **Essence:** Too many ads waste the first paragraph on a warm-up ("This is an age of beauty… charm…") that loses the reader instead of holding him. The opening must bridge straight from the headline to the benefit, keeping the promise the headline made.
- **Copy move:** Cut throat-clearing openings — often the real ad starts a paragraph or two down; begin there. Use a strong opener (interrupting idea, shocker, news, story) or simply continue the headline's thought. Three rules: keep it short, continue the headline, and state the main benefit fast. Openings should be fact-packed, telegraphic, specific, few adjectives.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | short-form — the lead.
- **Applies / fails when:** Always applies. The only "warm-up" that earns its place is a story or shocker that itself hooks; abstraction never does.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — Headline "Government jobs" → "Why worry about strikes, layoffs, hard times? Get a government job — steady work, good pay, travel." (continues the promise, packs benefits). Bad — Headline about a TV → "This is an age of beauty, charm, and style." (says nothing, blocks the reader from what he wants to know).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — заголовок «Работа в госсекторе» → «Зачем бояться сокращений и кризисов? Стабильная занятость, хорошая зарплата, командировки». (продолжает обещание, набивает выгодами). Плохо — заголовок про телевизор → «Мы живём в эпоху красоты, стиля и изящества» (ничего не сообщает, отгораживает читателя от того, что он хочет узнать).  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
- **Book ref:** Caples copied the first sentence of every Reader's Digest article and found over half opened with a story or an "interrupting idea" — techniques ad writers can borrow to hold readers the headline just stopped.

### [TAM:09] Right and wrong copy  (ch 10)
- **Essence:** Copy that sells is specific, factual, and about "you"; copy that fails is clever, humorous, poetic, affected, unbelievable, or "card" (too brief). "The more you tell, the more you sell" — the ads that pile up facts outsell the ones that pose. Clever and humorous copy are hundred-to-one bets that shrink your audience.
- **Copy move:** Default to straightforward, factual, "you-and-me" copy that stacks concrete selling points. Style is fine only when it carries facts, not instead of them. Avoid cleverness, humor, poetry, and affectation; never strain believability (a betrayed reader becomes a lifelong skeptic). If a testimonial or story is used, keep it specific and signed.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form | email — copy body register.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies as the safe default. Humor/cleverness can rarely win in the hands of a master, but never bet the campaign on them.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — factual boot copy: "Oil-tanned selected-grain uppers repel water; Goodyear welt, double oak soles, steel shank." (fact on fact). Bad — "Star Sapphire… a cup of night blue, dazed with moonlight…" (affected, sells nothing).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — фактурное описание ботинок: «Верх из отборной кожи, промасленная дубка отталкивает воду; рант по технологии Goodyear, двойная подошва, стальной супинатор». (факт на факте). Плохо — «Звёздный сапфир… чаша ночной синевы, одурманенная лунным светом…» (манерно, ничего не продаёт).
- **Book ref:** Two soap ads: the "style" one urged readers to "revel in its sculptured smoothness"; the "selling" one said "made of olive and palm oils — no other fats whatever" — the concrete, provable version is what tested advertisers use.

### [TAM:10] Increase selling power  (ch 11)
- **Essence:** Twenty tested amplifiers turn a competent draft into a stronger one. They are concrete edits, not theory: present-tense "you," subheads, captions, plain words, specificity, complete sales talk, and a hard urge to act.
- **Copy move:** Run the draft through the checklist. Highest-leverage items:
  1. Present tense, second person — hammer "you."
  2. Subheads that tell the story to scanners.
  3. Captions under every illustration (read more than body copy).
  4. Simple style, short words — don't tax the reader's attention.
  6. Give free information before the sales talk.
  8. Arouse curiosity, never satisfy it (don't reveal the mechanism).
  9. Be specific — "97,482 sold" beats "nearly 100,000."
  10–11. Use long copy to crowd in arguments; write more than fits and boil it down (broth gets stronger).
  12. Sell your product, not the category (don't help competitors).
  14. Prefer understatement to overstatement.
  19. Make every ad a complete sales talk (see [SCIAD:08]).
  20. Urge action, with a reason for now (see [SCIAD:15]).
  Also: 5 simple words · 7 selling>style · 13 mail-order rules for direct mail · 15 avoid trick slogans · 16 test two versions on a critic · 17 never say "a salesman will call" (cuts replies ~75%) · 18 study mail-order catalogues.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | short-form — revision pass.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to nearly every draft. Long copy (10) fails if padded rather than argument-dense; understatement (14) fails where genuine news warrants a bold announcement.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — swap "97,482 owners" for "nearly 100,000," add subheads, put a selling caption under the photo, end with "order before the 31st." Bad — a full-page ad of 24 "card" words and a slogan, no facts, no action ask.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — заменить «почти 100 000» на «97 482 владельца», добавить подзаголовки, дать продающую подпись под фото, закончить «закажите до 31-го». Плохо — полоса из 24 слов «визитки» и слогана — ни фактов, ни призыва.
- **Book ref:** Frank Davis started selling mackerel by mail with a 75-word letter and, through years of testing, grew to a 750-word letter plus a four-page folder of ~1,600 words — long copy simply pulled more orders.

### [TAM:11] Engineer the inquiry  (ch 13)
- **Essence:** When the goal is leads, response can be engineered with 32 concrete devices. Two classes: those that make the whole ad better (long copy + strong headline) and those that lift replies without improving the ad (a "Get this free book" caption). Use both deliberately, and match reply-quality to purpose.
- **Copy move:** Highest-leverage inquiry boosters — mention the offer in the headline and first paragraph; emphasize "Free"; show a picture of the booklet/sample; give the booklet an attractive title and a "table of contents" description; sweeten with a bonus; include a coupon with printed value and selling copy; add a business-reply card, phone number, "no obligation," and a deadline. Caution: if salesmen follow leads, don't over-feature the coupon (it lowers lead quality), and never say "a salesman will call" (cuts replies ~75%).
- **Applies to:** landing | email | short-form — lead-gen mechanics.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies when inquiries are the KPI. The reply-inflating tricks (class 2) mislead if you mistake raw reply count for ad quality or lead value.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Free 32-page guide: '7 ways to cut your energy bill' — no obligation, mail the card below before Friday." Bad — burying the offer so deep that a strong ad pulls two replies, then blaming the product.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Бесплатный гайд на 32 страницы: «7 способов срезать счёт за электричество» — без обязательств, отправьте карточку до пятницы». Плохо — закопать оффер так глубоко, что сильное объявление даёт два отклика, а виноватым назначить продукт.
- **Book ref:** An investment-service ad lifted booklet requests more than 500% simply by replacing "Send for free booklet" with a vivid "table-of-contents" description of what the booklet answered.

### [TAM:12] Appeal to the masses  (ch 14)
- **Essence:** The average reader makes a snap judgment and has a smaller vocabulary and shorter attention than copywriters assume; simplicity is not condescension, it is reach. Headline and illustration must tell the same story, because pictures register faster than words and a mismatch confuses.
- **Copy move:** Write to a fifth-grade reading level — short words, short sentences, short paragraphs; test by reading aloud and by handing it to an average person. Explain even "obvious" words ("exclusive" → "cannot be obtained elsewhere"). Make the picture and headline agree. Sharpen headlines by shortening, lengthening, or reconstructing them ("Five Acres" → "Five Acres and Independence" won big).
- **Applies to:** short-form | landing | email — clarity and reach.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to broad consumer audiences. For a narrow expert audience, some jargon is legitimate — but simplicity still rarely costs you the sophisticated reader.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Thou shalt not steal." (plain, unmistakable) over "It is unlawful to appropriate property belonging to others." Bad — "For a discriminating clientele" (clientele, discriminating — over the average reader's head).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Не воруй» вместо «Противоправно присвоение имущества, принадлежащего иным лицам». Плохо — «Для взыскательной клиентуры» (слова, которых средний читатель не разберёт).
- **Book ref:** Caples was told to poll 100 women on whether they knew what a "Pianola" was, sure they all would — only one in ten did; the lesson: never overrate the reader's familiarity with your words.

### [TAM:13] Layout and illustration  (ch 15)
- **Essence:** The layout's job is salesmanship, not fine art — "the greatest crime an ad can commit is to remain unnoticed," but attention must convert to sales. High-attention pictures (babies, brides, animals) only pay if they relate to the product; a picture is a label, so a bathing beauty sells the wrong mood.
- **Copy move:** Choose illustrations with sales value: the product, the product in use, the reward of using it, or the buyer's ambition attained. Prefer photographs (they prove and convince); use a person's head looking at the reader for a compact, high-pull image, especially in small space. Set copy in plain, readable type like an article; feature the meaningful words of a long headline in big type; an all-type ad with no picture can out-pull illustrated ones. Editorial-style ads lift readership (one pulled 81% more) but hide the product from headline-only scanners.
- **Applies to:** landing | short-form — visual and typographic choices.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to any laid-out ad. Editorial camouflage fails when scanners can't tell what's being sold from the headline alone.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — a photo of a real graduate holding his diploma, captioned with the benefit, for a course ad. Bad — a stock photo of a compass on a cruise ad (clever, but scanners read it as an ad for a compass).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — фото реального выпускника с дипломом и подписью-выгодой в рекламе курса. Плохо — стоковое фото компаса в рекламе круиза (умно, но беглый читатель воспримет это как рекламу компаса).
- **Book ref:** An art director who once won exhibition prizes started showing ads to elevator men and clerks; when dozens picked the plainest, Sears-catalogue-style ad, he switched to salesmanship-first layouts.

### [TAM:14] Make small ads pay  (ch 16)
- **Essence:** Different products need different space sizes, and small ads are cheap, repeatable laboratories. A simple-to-explain product (a reducing belt) sells in 60 lines; a skepticism-heavy one (hair grower) needs a full page of proof. Small ads win for limited-market items because you can run every issue and catch the reader in the moment of need.
- **Copy move:** Boil a big ad down to bone and muscle — telegraphic language, a one-word stopper headline that still means something, condensed benefit phrases. Weigh every illustration against copy it displaces. Choose ad-style or editorial-style, never both. Key everything and repeat the winners in the most responsive publications.
- **Applies to:** short-form — small-space and classified.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to easy-to-explain or narrow-need products. Fails for high-skepticism products that need long proof — those want full pages.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — a 60-line ad headlined "CORNS" with one benefit line and a keyed reply, run every issue. Bad — a padded small ad whose one-word headline is "Because" (brief but meaningless).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — маленькое объявление с заголовком «МОЗОЛИ», одной строкой выгоды и ключевым откликом, в каждом выпуске. Плохо — раздутое малое объявление с односложным заголовком «Потому что» (кратко, но бессмысленно).
- **Book ref:** A reducing belt sold profitably in 60-line ads because a picture and a few lines explained it, while a hair-growing treatment needed full pages of scientific explanation and testimonials to overcome skepticism.

### [TAM:15] Seventeen ways to test  (ch 18)
- **Essence:** New ideas always outrun proven answers, so Caples catalogs seventeen tests spanning free desk-checks to rigorous split-runs. The gold standard is a controlled sales test; opinion tests are quick and cheap but can lie (people won't vote for "bad breath," dodge all-type ads, and reward cleverness).
- **Copy move:** Pick the test that fits your stakes and deadline:
  1. Sleep on the copy; reread cold next day.
  2. Have someone read it aloud to you (stumbles = your fault, rewrite).
  3. Opinion test by interview — always offer ≥2 options; talk to real prospects; beware split-vote artifacts.
  4–8. Mail-order sales test · coupon+salesman · coupon for sample/literature · hidden offer (to limit/screen replies) · continuing keyed sales test.
  9. Telephone-response test (different name/extension per ad).
  10–11. Test appeals by mail · opinion tests by mail.
  12. Bus-card test. 13. Sales test in selected cities. 14. Department-store sales test.
  15–16. Readership tests by interview / by mail.
  17. Split-run copy testing — the cleanest: two versions, same day, same position, one variable.
  To lift thin hidden-offer replies: make it free, set a value, add a free gift, describe it richly, use Sunday/larger/more publications, or bigger ads — never by featuring the offer (that invalidates a copy test).
- **Applies to:** landing | email | short-form — validation method.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies whenever a claim can be measured. Opinion tests fail as a proxy for sales; a hidden offer's reply count is only comparative, not profit.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — split-run the humidity vs. coolness headline, alternating weekly to average out weather; "cool, quiet bedroom" won 2.5×. Bad — showing one ad and asking "is this good?" — everyone says yes, and you learn nothing.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — split-run заголовков «влажность» против «прохлада», чередуя по неделям, чтобы усреднить погоду; «прохладная тихая спальня» победила в 2,5 раза. Плохо — показать одно объявление и спросить «хорошее?» — все скажут «да», и вы не узнаете ничего.
- **Book ref:** Testing a headache remedy, a hidden 25¢-sample offer buried in the last paragraph pulled two replies; switching to a free full-size bottle brought ~100 — the offer's pull, not the appeal, was being measured until it was normalized.

# The Ultimate Sales Letter — Dan Kennedy  ·  prefix [USL]
> A great sales letter is assembled, not conjured: a step-by-step system that starts inside the customer's mind, engineers the message to get delivered-opened-read-believed-acted-on, and lives inside a follow-up sequence. Use this lens for direct-response letters, landing pages, emails, and VSLs where the reader is one person you must out-persuade.

## Principles

### [USL:01] Get into the customer  (ch 1)
- **Essence:** Persuasion starts by understanding the reader so deeply you can visualize their day, fears, and desires — then "enter the conversation already occurring in their mind" (Collier) rather than interrupting it. Address the reader's priorities, never the sender's.
- **Copy move:** Before writing, answer diagnostic questions — what keeps them up at night, what they fear, are angry about, secretly desire, what language they use, who else has tried and failed to sell them. Immerse (read their trade press, lurk where they gather, "play prospect"); list what they want in priority order and speak to it. Align the opening with what they're already thinking (season, news, water-cooler topic).
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL | short-form — the foundation of all of it.
- **Applies / fails when:** Always the first step. The trap is the business owner's "ingrained assumption" — start from scratch as if writing to them for the first time, or stale beliefs sabotage the letter.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — a fundraising letter to executives led with "TV exposure at half the cost — even free," answering the donor's "what's in it for my company?" instead of the charity's needs. Bad — "Our foundation needs funds for research and education" (the sender's priorities, which every ignored appeal already used).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — письмо репетитора родителям школьника открывается «Ваш ребёнок перестанет бояться контрольных по математике за 6 недель», а не «У нас 10 лет опыта и лицензия». Плохо — «Наш центр предлагает качественные образовательные услуги» (приоритеты отправителя, а не тревога родителя).
- **Book ref:** Kennedy's Arthritis Foundation letter pulled only 0.5% but landed one major new donor covering half the telethon's costs — because, unlike every other charity's appeal, it addressed the donor's interests, not the sender's.

### [USL:02] Get into the offer  (ch 1)
- **Essence:** Know your product or offer as intimately as the customer — use it, take it apart, test it on people. Then sell benefits, not features: "people don't buy things for what they are; they buy them for what they do." Look past the obvious benefit to the hidden benefit of profound personal importance.
- **Copy move:** List every feature and its benefit (one per card), then rank by importance to the reader. Translate each feature into "which means you…". Hunt for the hidden benefit — the emotional payoff nobody names — and consider leading with it.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | short-form | VSL — offer articulation.
- **Applies / fails when:** Always applies. The hidden-benefit lead wins when the emotional payoff outweighs the functional one; don't bury the functional benefit entirely if buyers need it to justify.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — an agent-recruiting system sold under "Puts recruiting on autopilot so you can go play golf!" — the hidden benefit (time freedom) the buyers actually craved. Bad — "Our CRM has automated lead scoring and multi-channel sync" (features with no "which means you…").
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — CRM для салонов продают через «Освободит вам два вечера в неделю от записи клиентов вручную», а не список функций. Плохо — «Автоматизированный скоринг лидов и мультиканальная синхронизация» (функции без «а значит, вы…»).
- **Book ref:** Noticing seminar attendees kept talking about golf on breaks, Kennedy and Pamela Yellen sold an insurance-recruiting system circuitously through its hidden benefit — freed time for the golf course — rather than its mechanics.

### [USL:03] Damaging admission  (ch 1)
- **Essence:** Openly admitting a real flaw in your product or offer raises credibility more than pretending perfection — everyone knows nothing is perfect, so honesty about the downside makes the upside believable. It also forces you to confront the reader's real objections.
- **Copy move:** List every reason the reader won't respond (fear, doubt, "too good to be true"), then answer them. Pick a genuine disadvantage and state it plainly, ideally reframing it as part of the selling story — the admission disarms skepticism and buys trust for the claims that follow.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL | short-form — credibility building.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies especially when the offer sounds too good or the audience is jaded. Fails if the "flaw" is fake or trivial (transparent humble-brag) — the admission must cost something to be believed.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "We're not the cheapest, and if the lowest price is all that matters to you, buy elsewhere — but if you want it to still work in five years, read on." Bad — "Our only flaw is that we care too much." (fake admission, reads as a brag).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Мы не самые дешёвые, и если для вас важна только цена — берите у других; но если хотите, чтобы работало через пять лет, читайте дальше». Плохо — «Наш единственный недостаток — что мы слишком заботимся о клиентах» (фальшивое признание, звучит как хвастовство).
- **Book ref:** A small Italian restaurant's letter warned that if you want tuxedoed waiters and silver ice buckets, "our little restaurant is not the place" — turning red-checked plastic tablecloths and a two-wine list into a warm, believable selling story.

### [USL:04] Get delivered and opened  (ch 2)
- **Essence:** A letter faces gauntlets before it's ever read: postal handling and human gatekeepers, then the recipient sorting mail "standing over a wastebasket." The outside's only job is to survive that sort and get opened — and it must match what's inside.
- **Copy move:** For cold prospects, "sneak up": plain envelope, real stamp, hand/ink-jet address, no teaser — looks like personal mail (~98% opened). For known customers, go the opposite way — a full billboard envelope that sells. Never be half-pregnant: reveal you're advertising, then sell hard. Use deliverability signals (personal return name, "important documents"), "magic words" ("Photo enclosed," "Health alert for residents of ___"), personalization, and physical grabbers ("lumpy" mail). Whatever the envelope promises, the inside must immediately fulfill.
- **Applies to:** email (subject/sender/preview) | landing (the ad that drives to it) — the "get opened" layer.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to any gated channel (physical mail, email inbox, gatekept B2B). A deceptive envelope/subject that isn't fulfilled inside destroys credibility — the promise must pay off.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — cold B2B: plain kraft envelope, live stamp, a real person's name in the return corner; email equivalent: a personal-looking sender and a specific, non-salesy subject. Bad — a gummed white label on bulk-rate mail screaming "junk," or a "Re: your account" subject on an unrelated pitch.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — холодный B2B: простой конверт, живая марка, в углу имя реального человека; в email — человеческий отправитель и конкретная, непродающая тема. Плохо — наклейка на конверте эконом-рассылки, кричащая «спам», или тема «Re: ваш счёт» на несвязанном оффере.
- **Book ref:** Mailings to doctors sent in "plain Jane" envelopes with a doctor's name as the return address — which staff don't screen — pulled up to 300% more than the same letters in obvious business envelopes.

### [USL:05] Get it read (greased chute)  (ch 3)
- **Essence:** No one is waiting for your letter; on arrival it's an unwelcome pest that earns "welcome guest" status only by instantly saying something important, valuable, and beneficial to the reader. You buy that with a headline — yes, letters should have headlines (often in a "Johnson box") — following AIDA from attention to interest.
- **Copy move:** Put a real headline at the top (Johnson box or bold line). Use proven fill-in-the-blank structures ("They didn't think I could ___, but I did," "Who else wants ___," "How I ___," "Warning: ___," "Give me ___ and I'll ___," "___ ways to ___"). Add a "flag" — a word or phrase naming the exact prospect ("Annuity agents:", "Foot pain?") ahead of the promise. Consider a grabber to force reading. Then make every line's only job to get the next line read.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL | short-form — the hook and readability spine.
- **Applies / fails when:** Always applies. Gimmick headlines ("this letter is impregnated with a chemical…") get the panel clipped and the letter tossed — say something genuinely important, not merely clever.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — flag + structure: "Restaurant owners: How I filled 40 empty Tuesday tables without discounting." Bad — "A Message From Our Founder" (no benefit, no flag, no reason to read on).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — флаг + структура: «Владельцам кафе: как я заполнил 40 пустых столов по вторникам без скидок». Плохо — «Обращение основателя» (ни выгоды, ни флага, ни причины читать дальше).
- **Book ref:** Kennedy likens the headline to a door-to-door seller's foot in the door — one or two sentences to melt resistance; a client's letter arrived with a mini trash can of peanuts "from a squirrel" calling the non-responder "nuts," a grabber that made recipients read to find out why.

### [USL:06] Beat the bugaboo (price)  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** Price rarely decides a sale but, mishandled, halts it before it starts. The move is not to discount but to build so much perceived value that the price feels small — decide your price-minimizing strategy before writing.
- **Copy move:** Price minimizers — sell bulk (weigh/enumerate everything they get); recount the cost to develop the offer (builds value even if not "relevant"); make the itemized parts add up to far more than the whole, then discount; conceal price in payments ("3 payments of $19.95" tested equal to or better than a lower lump sum); anchor against a much larger alternative cost. Present price openly whenever the letter aims to close.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL — the offer/price section.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies whenever price is in play and aims to close. Omitting price is right only for lead-gen letters where discussing it is premature — but not if price-fear blocks the response.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Add it up: the six workshops alone run $4,975, the toolkit $25,000 — $32,475 in value; your price is $9,895, or 12 payments of $825." Bad — leading with "$9,895" cold, before any value has been stacked.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Сложите: шесть модулей — 300 000 ₽, набор шаблонов — 500 000 ₽; итого ценности на 900 000 ₽, а ваша цена — 190 000 ₽, или 12 платежей по 16 000 ₽». Плохо — начать холодно с «190 000 ₽», не выстроив ценность.
- **Book ref:** In a direct test, "$39.95," "2 payments of $19.95," and "3 payments of $19.95" produced identical sales; a $7,000 item presented as "8 payments of $875" outsold a $5,000 item at "4 payments of $1,250" — the lower monthly figure won 72% of buyers.

### [USL:07] Emotional copy formulas  (ch 4)
- **Essence:** Three whole-letter formulas reliably move readers because people act more to avoid pain than to gain, and buy on emotion. They get the reader focused on something other than price.
- **Copy move:** **Problem–Agitate–Solve** (the most reliable): state the problem plainly, then agitate it — tap anger, fear, embarrassment until the reader is "wringing their hands" — then reveal your product as the solution. **Fortune-telling:** predict a trend/future the reader cares about, then position your offer as preparation. **Winners vs losers:** contrast the fate of those who act with those who don't (the "5% vs 95%"), and make your offer the fork. Combine them; keep the copy emotional, not analytical.
- **Applies to:** landing | VSL | email | short-form — the persuasion arc.
- **Applies / fails when:** Powerful when a real pain or aspiration exists. Over-agitation without a credible solution reads as manipulation; the pain must be true and the solution must actually resolve it.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good (P-A-S) — "Your best employees are quietly stealing from you [problem]. While you admire your lawn, one of them is buying a house with it [agitate]. Here's the audit that stops it in 30 days [solve]." Bad — "Our security service has many features and competitive rates." (no problem, no emotion).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо (P-A-S) — «Ваши лучшие сотрудники тихо вас обкрадывают [проблема]. Пока вы любуетесь ремонтом, один из них на эти деньги покупает машину [нагнетание]. Вот аудит, который остановит это за 30 дней [решение]». Плохо — «У нашей охранной службы много функций и конкурентные тарифы» (ни проблемы, ни эмоции).
- **Book ref:** A theft-control expert's letter agitated grocery CEOs with a deliveryman-thief buying a half-million-dollar home across the street from his victim, then signed by a reformed thief ("I was one of them") — problem, agitation, and solution in one arc.

### [USL:08] Write sales pressure  (ch 5)
- **Essence:** All successful selling applies pressure to force a decision, and a letter must supply it from a distance where escape is easy. Kennedy's proven pressure devices replace hundreds of tricks with a working handful.
- **Copy move:** **Takeaway/intimidation** — "take a position": limited number, bandwagon ("most will buy"), qualify-to-buy ("you can buy only if…"), making the buyer sell you. **Demonstrate ROI** — sell money at a discount ("$199 course, $1,000 income gain = 500% return; even if I'm half right…"). **Ego appeals** — frame the purchase as status. **Strong guarantee** — bolder, not weaker, in a skeptical age (money-back, keep-the-premium, redundant wording, free trial, or make the guarantee the whole offer — even guarantee the letter itself). **Storytelling** — study fiction; wrap the pitch in a rags-to-riches or slice-of-life story.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL — the close and risk-reversal.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to any ask. Fake scarcity or hollow guarantees backfire; takeaway/qualification works only when the offer is genuinely desirable enough to withhold.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "Only 200 seats; applications reviewed, and we accept about 1 in 3. 60-day 'love it or every penny back' guarantee — and keep the toolkit regardless." Bad — "Limited spots! Act now! 100% guarantee!" stacked with nothing real behind any of it.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Всего 200 мест; заявки проходят отбор, берём примерно одного из трёх. Гарантия 60 дней «понравится или вернём каждый рубль» — а набор шаблонов остаётся у вас в любом случае». Плохо — «Мест мало! Только сейчас! Гарантия 100%!» — нагромождение без реального содержания.
- **Book ref:** Kennedy guarantees the letter itself — offering $10–$50 to anyone who reads it and feels their time was wasted; on one mailing to ~4,000 prospects he paid out under $200 and brought in over $100,000, and split-tests confirmed the guarantee lifts readership and response.

### [USL:09] Draft fast, rewrite in passes  (ch 6)
- **Essence:** Write the first draft raw — no editing, no worry about length or grammar — getting every persuasive idea down (often 2–3× final length), then rewrite in separate passes: once for strategy, once for style. Long copy is fine; write for the buyer, not the non-buyer, who is hungry for information.
- **Copy move:** Draft everything, then cut for strategy: serve a **double readership path** (bold headlines/subheads/captions/boxes for skimmers; full copy for analyticals); use **internal repetition** (state the promise ~7 ways — statement, example, story, testimonial, quote, summary); build a **yes-momentum** with easy-agree questions; **tease the bottom of every page** (never end a page on a completed sentence). Then rewrite for style: short punchy sentences and non-sentences, conversational grammar, sensory and "big-impact" phrases, first paragraph as an extended headline, your own personality.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL | short-form — drafting and revision method.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to any substantial piece. Long copy fails only when padded rather than argument-dense (same caveat as [TAM:10]); the reader-friendly devices matter more as length grows.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — end page one mid-phrase: "…and the single change that doubled her list is on the next page—" so the reader turns it. Bad — a grammatically perfect, sentence-complete page that gives the reader a clean place to stop and quit.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — оборвать страницу на середине фразы: «…а одно изменение, которое удвоило её базу, — на следующей странице —», чтобы читатель её перевернул. Плохо — грамматически безупречная страница, законченная точкой, дающая читателю удобное место остановиться.
- **Book ref:** Kennedy rewrites a flat opener ("I would have taken time to write you a personalized letter…") into an extended-headline first paragraph ("Market indicators show silver is getting ready to surge! …fully explained in this important letter!") — selling the reader on reading before selling the offer.

### [USL:10] Answer questions and objections  (ch 7)
- **Essence:** Unanswered objections silently kill sales letters. Unlike a live salesperson, the letter can't see which objections a given reader has, so it must surface and answer every likely one — never underestimate the reader's skepticism, and never assume hiding an issue makes it disappear.
- **Copy move:** Brainstorm every reason not to buy, then address each. Use the "our people brainstormed why you might say no — here are the three reasons, and here's our answer to each" frame, or a FAQ page. Each answer should carry three things: a direct answer, a proof (testimonial/case/story), and a restatement of the guarantee/free trial.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL — objection handling section.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies whenever skepticism or friction exists (almost always). The risk of "planting" doubts is overstated — a real prospect already has them; leaving them unanswered is worse.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "'Isn't this just for big companies?' No — 60% of our clients have under 10 staff; here's one with three [case], and you're covered by the 90-day guarantee anyway." Bad — staying silent on the obvious "too good to be true / too expensive / no time" and hoping the reader won't think of it.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «"Это же только для крупных компаний?" Нет — у 60% наших клиентов меньше 10 сотрудников; вот один с тремя [кейс], и вас в любом случае страхует гарантия 90 дней». Плохо — молчать про очевидное «слишком дорого / нет времени / звучит нереально» в надежде, что читатель не додумается.
- **Book ref:** Kennedy notes a doctor who received a dozen letters, wanted the product, and could afford it but never bought — because it seemed "too good to be true"; had the letter anticipated and answered that one objection, it would have converted him.

### [USL:11] Spark immediate action  (ch 7)
- **Essence:** The largest group of non-responders are those who intend to respond "later" — and later never comes. Since the money is in immediate response, the letter must drive action now, overcoming the reader's busy, procrastinating "conditional yes."
- **Copy move:** Six urgency drivers — genuine limited availability; a strong premium (often lifts response ~50%, usually beats a discount); a deadline (handwritten/stamped look for small mailings); multiple premiums (raise order size); fast-response discounts / slow-response penalties (tiered pricing); and radical ease of responding (toll-free, fax, clean order page, reply envelope). The winning combination: reader trusts you, likes the product, believes a real urgency story, and wants the premium.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL — the call to action.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to every ask. Scarcity/deadlines must be true; manufactured urgency erodes trust (cf. [SCIAD:06]) and repeat buyers grow immune to stale deadlines — which is why a fresh, believable premium story out-pulls a tired "act now."
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "First 100 orders get the toolkit free; enrollment closes Friday or when seats run out — and you can check out in 60 seconds by phone or one-page form." Bad — "Order whenever you're ready, supplies are basically unlimited." (no reason to move today).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «Первым 100 заказам — набор шаблонов в подарок; запись закрывается в пятницу или когда закончатся места; оформить можно за 60 секунд по телефону или в одну форму». Плохо — «Заказывайте когда удобно, товара хватит на всех» (нет причины действовать сегодня).
- **Book ref:** Vegas World's repeat buyers had grown immune to deadline offers, so Bob Stupak added a premium — 1,000 Hawaiian vacations, a genuine limited-availability story — and it pulled Kennedy himself to stop mid-workday and order immediately.

### [USL:12] The creative P.S.  (ch 8)
- **Essence:** Every sales letter needs a P.S. — it can make or break the letter. Many readers jump to the end first (to see the signature, to "eat dessert first"), so the P.S. is a high-impact second headline that can pull them into the whole letter or add a final incentive.
- **Copy move:** Never finish without a P.S. Use it to restate the core offer/promise, spotlight the deadline or premium, or land one more benefit — treat it as a second headline, not an afterthought. In email/landing terms, this is the closing recap block and the last CTA.
- **Applies to:** email | landing | VSL — the closing recap.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to every direct-response letter/email. A P.S. that just says "thanks again" wastes the most-read real estate — it must carry offer, urgency, or benefit.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — "P.S. Remember, the free audit and the toolkit both disappear Friday at midnight — and you risk nothing thanks to the 90-day money-back guarantee. Claim your spot now: [link]." Bad — "P.S. Thank you for your time and consideration." (dead weight in the highest-attention spot).
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — «P.S. Напоминаю: бесплатный аудит и набор шаблонов исчезают в пятницу в полночь — а вы ничем не рискуете благодаря гарантии возврата 90 дней. Займите место: [ссылка]». Плохо — «P.S. Спасибо за внимание» (пустая трата самого читаемого места).
- **Book ref:** Kennedy notes that "perverse" readers who skip to the end — the same people who read a mystery's last page first — turn the P.S. into your opportunity: summarize the offer there and it pulls them back into the whole letter.

### [USL:13] Graphic enhancement  (ch 8)
- **Essence:** Since a letter can't use a salesperson's body language, tone, and props, "Copy Cosmetics" transfer that physical layer to the page — fonts, bold, underscore, highlight, margin notes, captions, color — guiding the eye and making the copy look easy to read (and therefore more likely to be read), without changing a word.
- **Copy move:** Apply selective emphasis to serve both readers ([USL:09]): bold subheads, captioned images, boxed/highlighted key lines, handwritten-style margin notes and doodles as eye-magnets around the offer, guarantee, phone, and CTA. Use color and borders on the guarantee/coupon. Rule: when everything is emphasized, nothing stands out — don't overdo it.
- **Applies to:** email | landing | short-form — visual/typographic layer.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to any laid-out piece, especially long copy. Over-emphasis (everything bold/highlighted/colored) destroys the effect it's meant to create.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — a long email where the deadline, guarantee, and CTA button are the only bolded/colored elements, with a short "handwritten" margin note by the offer. Bad — a page where half the sentences are bold, three highlight colors compete, and the eye finds no anchor.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — длинное письмо, где дедлайн, гарантия и кнопка CTA — единственные выделенные жирным/цветом элементы, плюс короткая «рукописная» пометка на полях у оффера. Плохо — страница, где половина предложений жирные, спорят три цвета выделения, и глазу не за что зацепиться.
- **Book ref:** Kennedy frames Copy Cosmetics as transferring the in-person salesperson's clothes, gestures, and vocal inflections onto paper; his contributor's list runs to 27 devices (bold, borders, captions, cartoons, color, columns…) — all to guide the eye without touching the words.

### [USL:14] Pretest, cool off, test  (ch 9,10)
- **Essence:** Before mailing, buy back objectivity and catch flaws with zero-cost pretests, then — for anything reused at scale — split-test real variables. Never overestimate the reader's sophistication or literacy: err toward simplicity.
- **Copy move:** Pretest free — read it aloud (fix every hang-up); read it to typical customers (do they start asking how to buy?); have a child read it aloud (change words they stumble on). Build a mockup and mail it to yourself to see it in the real mail stack. Take a 3–5 day cool-off, get expert second opinions, final review. Then, for evergreen pieces, split-test a matrix — headline, photo, offer, bonus, guarantee — one variable at a time, banking knowledge over time.
- **Applies to:** landing | email | VSL — validation and QA before and after launch.
- **Applies / fails when:** Pretests apply to every piece; formal split-testing applies when volume/reuse justifies it. "Unable to test" (tiny one-off) differs from "unwilling to test" — don't excuse yourself from A/B when you could.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — read the draft aloud, mail yourself a mockup, then split-test headline A vs B to 10% of the list before rolling out the winner. Bad — writing to a "sophisticated" B2B audience in dense jargon, skipping the read-aloud, and mailing the whole list untested.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — прочитать черновик вслух, отправить макет себе, затем split-test заголовков A и B на 10% базы перед раскаткой победителя. Плохо — писать «искушённой» B2B-аудитории плотным жаргоном, пропустить чтение вслух и разослать всю базу без теста.
- **Book ref:** A copywriter for blue-collar men reads drafts aloud in a neighborhood bar over a round of beers — the real acid test being whether listeners start asking how to buy the product, moving from critic's corner to cash-register line.

### [USL:15] The power of a sequence  (ch 12)
- **Essence:** The biggest mistake is the one-shot mailing; impact takes repetition. A tight, timed sequence (three steps over ~45–60 days) — modeled on collection-agency "first notice / second notice / final notice" — often makes the second and third pieces together double the first's response.
- **Copy move:** Design campaigns as sequences, not single sends. Separate style from structure: keep the proven first/second/third escalation regardless of tone or market. Apply it to follow-up on non-buyers too (appointment-but-no-sale) — a sequenced follow-up routinely brings 10–30% back to buy what they first declined.
- **Applies to:** email | landing (multi-touch funnels) | short-form — campaign architecture.
- **Applies / fails when:** Applies to almost any offer with a reachable list. Fails only where you have a single touch and no way to follow up; even then, the lesson is to build the ability to sequence.
- **Few-shot (EN):** Good — a 3-email escalation over three weeks: "here's the offer" → "did you miss this?" → "final notice: closes tonight," each standing alone but compounding. Bad — one perfect email sent once, then silence, treating non-response as a permanent no.
- **Few-shot (RU):** Хорошо — эскалация из 3 писем за три недели: «вот оффер» → «вы это пропустили?» → «последнее уведомление: закрываем сегодня», каждое самостоятельно, но с накоплением. Плохо — одно идеальное письмо один раз, потом тишина, а неответ трактуется как окончательное «нет».  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
- **Book ref:** Kennedy's famous Giorgio's Italian-restaurant sequence — modeled on dunning letters — makes the restaurant a household topic; he calls sequencing his "million-dollar secret," as responsible as any idea for his and his clients' fortunes.
